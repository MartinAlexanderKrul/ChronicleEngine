## What 1.47 changed

**Section 16 had law and no trigger.** 1.0 authored five titles and said each "is granted the instant its feat resolves" — a sentence that reads like a rule and is not one, because nothing anywhere in the profile ever caused a feat to be tested against the catalog. There was no schedule, no evaluating step in any settlement, no stored field to write a title into, and no event that could produce one. The result held for forty-six versions and every campaign run under them: a section of live mechanical law, a `/system titles` panel, a Console row, and a Tier-1 message block, all of them permanently empty. Titles were dormant, not because a Bearer had failed to earn one, but because nothing was ever asked.

**1.47 gives the section a trigger: the Title Assessment (Section 16.1).** The level-up that settles **level 10**, and every level-up that settles a level that is a multiple of ten thereafter, now runs an assessment inside that same exchange — the System reading the Bearer's **whole recorded career since onset** against the catalog and granting every title the record has earned. The schedule is deliberately the Section 6.6 System Rank ladder: 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 are the rungs into D, C, B, A, and S — every rung above the E a Bearer starts at — and past 50 the assessments continue on the decade because Section 3.1 sets no level cap. Levels 1–9 carry no assessment.

**What the assessment does not do.** It grants nothing between assessments — a feat completed at level 12 waits, uncounted and unannounced, until 20. It publishes no unearned criterion, no progress, and no near-miss (Section 14.2's withholding); it reports only what it granted and how many catalog entries remain unheld. It is never deferred, banked, offered, or declined (Section 3.2's settlement invariant). And it never fails to render: an assessment that grants nothing says so.

**A title now grants two things, and the second one was already promised.** Sections 3.2 and 4.2 have named "item/**title**/skill rewards that state a specific point bonus" as a stat-point source since 1.0, and no title in the 1.0 catalog ever stated one — the same shape of gap as the missing trigger, a hook with nothing on it. Every title now grants **allocatable stat points the moment it is earned** — +2 Common, +5 Rare, +10 Singular — permanently and unconditionally, alongside the passive it carries while equipped. The points are never contingent on equipping: a dormant title's passive sleeps, but the growth it paid for does not.

**And the loadout grows with the Bearer.** 1.0 allowed exactly one equipped title forever, so a Bearer holding nine used one and left eight inert. He now holds **one equipped slot per assessment passed, capped at five** — one at level 10, two at 20, three at 30, four at 40, five at 50 and after. Against a catalog of twenty-one that keeps equipping a decision at every rung rather than eventually becoming a formality. `system_state.title` accordingly holds a **list** rather than a single name; it may never be longer than the derived slot count, and the derived count is read from `titles.last_assessment` and never stored.

**The catalog grows from five entries to twenty-one, across three grades.** Twelve **Common** (+2 points), six **Rare** (+5), three **Singular** (+10) — the grade states how hard the criterion is to reach and nothing else: it gives no priority at an assessment, no ordering in a panel, and no restriction on which slot a title may sit in. The five 1.0 titles keep their passives and have their criteria restated as statements about the record, which is what an assessment can test — "first Gate cleared solo" becomes "at least one Gate cleared alone", the same feat asked of a career rather than of a moment. Every entry is decidable from resolved Events without a ruling.

**Every entry is named for what its feat made the Bearer, not for the feat.** A commendation is a name for the man, and the System does not narrate an afternoon in a title. Two 1.0 names are corrected on that principle with their criteria and passives untouched — **Lone Clear → Loner** and **Untouched → Untouchable** — while **Red Survivor**, **Centurion**, and **Break-Breaker** already named a person and stand unchanged. **No renaming touches a criterion, a passive, or a stored value**, and none can strand a reference: no Bearer in any Gatefall campaign has ever held a title, under any name.

**The Rare and Singular grades are the new reach.** Rare: **Cartographer** (a Gate of every archetype on the Section 10 d8, cleared and credited), **Deathless** (twenty consecutive clears with no classified injury and Health never below a quarter), **Executioner** (fifty kills of creatures above his System Rank), **Warden** (ten clears in which no ally took a classified injury), **Vanguard** (ten Gates entered alone and first), **Wellspring** (more than twice his maximum Mana spent inside one sealed instance). Singular: **Godslayer** (an S-Rank boss dead to his credit), **Unbroken** (a hundred consecutive dailies, no failure, no reset), **The Unmeasured** (a carded Rank three or more Ranks below his System Rank at an assessment). A Singular passive grants a capability the profile grants nowhere else, and most Bearers will die holding none.

**Supporting sections.** Section 3.2 gains the assessment as a further settlement on decade levels, under the invariant it already carried. Section 14.3's Tier-1 list trades the untriggerable `TITLE EARNED` line for the `TITLE ASSESSMENT` block. Section 15.3.4's `/system titles` panel gains an `ASSESSMENT` section reporting last, next, and held-of-catalog — and naming no unearned title. **No other section changes**, and no number, cost, timer, pool, price, Rank, counter, quest, item, or resolved outcome moves anywhere in the profile. Data Model 0.1.6 is unchanged and no engine decision is required.

## Migration procedure

**Required 1.46 → 1.47 migration.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon. The transformation is confined to titles: nothing else in `system_state` is recomputed, re-derived, re-rolled, or rewritten.

**1. Author the `titles` block, and widen `title` to a list.** Every Bearer's ledger gains `system_state.titles` with three fields: `earned` (a list, empty until an assessment grants), `last_assessment`, and `next_assessment`. A Bearer below level 10 at adoption takes `last_assessment: null` and `next_assessment: 10`. The existing `system_state.title` field is **retained in name and meaning** — it holds what is equipped — but now holds a **list** of names rather than one. **No Bearer's value changes at adoption**: it was `null` in every campaign, because no title has ever been grantable, and `null` remains a valid empty reading. Every startup dispatch, panel template, and Console row that already reads the field continues to read it.

**2. Run the missed assessments, once, at adoption, in level order.** A Bearer standing at **level 10 or above** at adoption has passed one or more assessment rungs that could not fire, because the rule did not exist when he crossed them. At adoption he runs one assessment for each passed decade — 10, then 20, then 30, as far as his level reaches — and no more; `last_assessment` records the highest of them and `next_assessment` the following decade. A Bearer below level 10 runs none and reaches his first at 10 in ordinary play.

**3. Each missed rung is judged at the level it belongs to, and the last one absorbs the remainder.** A catch-up spanning several rungs must not collapse into one grant at the highest of them, or the earlier rungs would be fiction. Each missed rung **L** therefore tests the catalog against the record **as it stood when level L was reached** — the campaign chronicle fixes that moment exactly, since every Event carries its own resolution — and a title is credited to the **earliest** rung whose record satisfied it. The **final** missed rung, and only that one, additionally counts everything resolved between its own crossing and adoption: a catch-up cannot un-resolve Events promoted since (Rules Section 13.2), so they belong to the last assessment rather than to none. This is the one respect in which a catch-up differs from an assessment fired on time. Every assessment from adoption forward fires inside its own level-up exchange under Section 16.1 and reads the record as of that exchange.

**4. Grant, never revoke — and pay the points.** The catch-up may grant several titles at once; that is the correct result for a career that ran its whole length with the section switched off. Each granted title adds its **grade's stat points to the unspent pool** — +2, +5, or +10 — at the assessment that granted it, exactly as an on-time assessment would have. This is the one place where the adoption changes a capability number, and it is deliberate: the points were owed at rungs the Bearer actually crossed, and withholding them would make the catch-up a partial settlement rather than the assessment it stands in for. **They arrive unspent** — no Stat, maximum, pool, or Stat Passive Rank moves at adoption, and nothing is allocated on the Bearer's behalf (Section 4.2). Nothing is granted twice, nothing already held is re-granted, and no title, once granted here, can be revoked by a later assessment.

**5. Equip nothing.** The adoption grants titles and opens slots; it fills none of them. `system_state.title` holds whatever it held before — `null` for every Bearer, since no title has ever been grantable — and the Bearer chooses through `/system titles` (Section 15.3.4), free and immediate, in play. **A Runtime never equips on the Bearer's behalf**, and no passive is active until he does.

**6. Nothing else moves.** Beyond `titles`, the widened `title` field, and the unspent points step 4 awards: no pool, Stat, maximum, XP total, level, System Rank, mastery counter, use count, quest counter, deadline, streak, gold balance, cash balance, item, or binding is touched. No past scene is reread, no past roll is reinterpreted, and no resolved outcome changes. The System's behavioral contract is otherwise unamended: no trigger tier moves, Section 14.4's information boundary is untouched, and the System still never lies, never resolves an action, and never surfaces for anyone but its Bearer.

**7. The blocks render once, one per missed rung.** A catch-up assessment is a real System event and the Bearer perceives it: each rung's `TITLE ASSESSMENT` block fires at the campaign's current anchor, in level order, not silently and not backdated into a scene that has already been narrated. They consume no fictional time and interrupt nothing.

Record adoption and the assessments it runs as campaign Events at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.46-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.47 at readiness.
