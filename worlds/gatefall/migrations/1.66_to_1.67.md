## What 1.67 changed

**Three content systems sized their populations to the Bearer's System Rank, and System Rank is not a measure of the Bearer.**

Section 6.6 has said so since 1.6, in its own words: System Rank *"is not his carded rank, not a Stat, and not a substitute for his actual body… Stats and equipment determine Health, Mana, physical magnitude, and Bearer-versus-Rank margins; System Rank only selects the scale of authored content."* For rewards and labelling — threat colour, Stat Passive ceilings, XP and shop Rank floors — that is exactly right and 1.67 leaves it untouched.

For content authored to **fight** him it was never enough, and `F-023` measured the gap. The Level 25 Class Quest, billed by Section 18.2 as *"a sealed instance, fully lethal, with no retreat… a gauntlet,"* populated at C-Rank: commons at 250, elites at 500, a boss at 1,000. Played in full, three commons, two elites and the boss died, and **the Bearer took a single landed hit for 13 damage across the entire trial.** One Rupture critical dealt **2,283** into a 1,000-Health boss.

**This is not "the Bearer is strong for his Rank."** Section 19 already has language for that — anomalous performance, re-measurement, exposure — and the divergence between card and capability is the premise that whole section is built on. The defect is that **Section 18.2 read the Rank label while Section 19 exists precisely because label and capability diverge.** A C-Rank population is calibrated against an ordinary C-Rank crew, several hunters together with none individually overwhelming. It was never calibrated against one Bearer whose single skill critical exceeds a full Rank's boss pool.

**The same read governed the penalty zone**, which is why this edge is not a local patch to Section 18:

| Consumer | Sized at, through 1.66 | Consequence at investment |
|---|---|---|
| Class trial (Section 18.2) | System Rank | the one trial authored to test him tests nothing |
| Penalty zone (Section 8.3) | System Rank | a punishment that does not punish |

**Two and no more, and the boundary is a rule rather than a stopping point.** These are the instances the System **imposes**: one the Bearer cannot retreat from, one he did not choose to enter. Everywhere else he selects his own difficulty, and 1.67 deliberately leaves that alone — an instant dungeon runs at its key's own recorded Rank (Section 17), and a public Gate at the Rank BGM assessed (Section 9), a posted Gate being contracted against, staffed to a legal minimum and insured on that number, so it cannot bend around one hunter without the assessment becoming a fiction. **The System owes an honest fight only where it picked the fight.**

**New Section 6.7 authors the Combat Tier.** It is derived at each use, stores nothing, and reads only figures the Bearer's ledger already carries:

```text
offense_tier = lowest Rank whose common Rank Health > best standard single-resolution damage
incoming(R)  = (Rank_Health(R) ÷ 4) × (1 − standing_worn_reduction)
defense_tier = highest R where maximum_health ÷ incoming(R) ≥ 5
combat_tier  = max(System Rank, min(offense_tier, defense_tier)), capped at S
```

**Two axes and the lower of them**, because out-damaging a Rank is not out-living it: scaling on offense alone would drop a high-damage, low-Health build into a population that kills it in one exchange, which is a shorter trial rather than a harder one. **Once-per-exchange and once-per-scene reductions are excluded** from `standing_worn_reduction` — they are tactical resources the Bearer may spend, hold, or waste, not standing protection.

**System Rank is a floor, so the derivation can only ratchet upward.** The defect is content being too easy and never too hard; nothing anywhere becomes easier under 1.67, and an under-invested Bearer meets exactly the content he met under 1.66.

**Each consumer snapshots at its own instant.** The class trial takes the tier at **entry**, not at offer — Section 18.1 lets the offer stand open indefinitely, so sizing it at offer would let a Bearer buy a trivial trial by waiting, which is the same defect wearing a delay. The penalty zone takes it at the 00:00 transfer. A key takes it when created, and an existing key keeps the Rank it already carries.

**`F-023`'s own proposed remedy was rejected on inspection, and the reason is worth keeping.** The flag asked whether Section 18.2 should read the Bearer's *"Section 19 measured-output tier."* Section 19.2's instrument reads **maximum Mana and nothing else**, and states of itself that its result is *"a mana-capacity classification, not a complete combat verdict."* A Bearer at 166 maximum Mana falls in that table's `120+` band and classifies **S-Rank**: 4,000-Health commons dealing ≈420 through his reduction into a 444-Health pool. That is not a harder trial, it is an execution — and the proxy is structurally wrong besides, since maximum Mana is `2 × effective Intelligence` and therefore measures the one Stat least connected to how a physical Bearer kills things.

**The flag's prose and its own worked numbers also disagreed**, and the numbers were right. It asked for *"roughly two full Ranks above his System Rank"* and then named **B-Rank** figures for both Health and incoming damage; C→B is one Rank. Section 6.7 derives the answer rather than fixing a step, so neither reading is hardcoded.

## The trial was also underspecified, and Rank alone does not fix that

Sizing was half the defect. The other half is that **Section 18.2 authored almost nothing about the trial itself.** Set beside Section 17's instant dungeon, which specifies a population formula, an archetype roll, a two-hour clock, exact loot and two named closing conditions, the trial said *"a gauntlet — waves and a final foe"* and stopped. The one piece of content in the world authored to **examine** the Bearer was **less specified than a shop-bought dungeon**.

So it was resolved with the only population machinery the profile has — a Gate roll — and produced **three commons, two elites and a boss**: six enemies, met one at a time, with healing available throughout. Against that population:

| Signal | Instrument present? |
|---|---|
| Kill efficiency | yes, repeatedly |
| Battlefield control | **no** — six sequential foes never form a crowd |
| Weapon breadth | **no** — nothing resisted the first answer |
| Endurance | **no** — one Mend restored him; resources never bottomed |
| Positioning | **no** — no ground was authored to matter |

Section 18.3 then tallied five signals of which **one** could be seen, and offered a class "matching the demonstrated style." **From inside that result there is no way to tell a correct read from the only read available**, which is what makes it an authoring defect rather than a tuning complaint. Raising the Rank does not touch it: a higher-Rank population met one enemy at a time is still one enemy at a time, and the kills merely take more rolls.

**Sections 18.2.1–18.2.3 replace the rolled population with five authored phases and a final foe**, each phase built so that one signal *can* be demonstrated in it — a converging swarm one Rank below the trial that overlaps rather than queues; a grind of elites that trade rather than burst; a foe carrying a resistance keyed live to whichever method has dealt the most damage so far, unannounced; an elite contesting space on ground that fails behind the Bearer; and a six-exchange clock whose overrun arms the boss, bounded by Section 4.3's ±3 net cap. A **single resource budget with no refill** runs across all of it: no potion functions, no Status Recovery, no pending reward, and no elapsed-time recovery, while his own Mana-costed skills work normally — so healing is a transfer within one budget rather than an escape from it, which is the decision Endurance exists to measure.

**A phase does not require its signal.** It creates the conditions under which that signal can appear; the Bearer solves it however he likes and the System scores what he actually did. Brute-forcing the swarm is a legitimate answer and scores Kill Efficiency rather than Battlefield Control. This is deliberate — a trial with right answers would be the menu Section 18.2 says it is not.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.66 → 1.67.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.67"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, Stat, level, XP, System Rank, Card Rank, skill, mastery level, mastery progress, counter, scope floor, magnitude floor, title, item, gold, cash, quest, candidate, or key is touched. The Combat Tier is **derived at each use and stored nowhere**, so this edge adds no field to any ledger and no `system_state` path gains a value.

**3. Nothing retroactive.** No instance resolved under 1.66 or earlier is re-Ranked, no resolved kill is re-scored, no XP award is recomputed, and no drop is re-rolled. Every penalty zone, instant dungeon, and Gate already cleared stands exactly as promoted, at exactly the Rank it ran at.

**4. No key in any inventory changes Rank, and Section 17's orphan is struck.** Through 1.66, Section 17 carried a Rank rule for an *"own-tier"* key — a category the profile named exactly once and **no section ever created**. The three real sources are a boss drop (Gate Rank, Section 11.2), a shop purchase (the explicit Rank bought, Section 12.5), and a Daily Premium Key (its own d100 Rank roll at rotation, Section 12.5); none of them produces a key sized to the Bearer. Section 6.6 separately asserted that *"a shop key opens at his System Rank,"* which **contradicted Section 12.5** — that section sells keys at a chosen Rank off a price table and states that a purchased key *"is fixed at the Rank bought."* Both defects entered at Profile 1.4 and neither has ever been reachable: `own-tier` appears in no campaign ledger, checkpoint, or chronicle in the repository. Section 17 now names its three real sources and Section 6.6's claim is removed. **Nothing stored is touched** — every key in every inventory keeps the Rank it already carries, and no instance opened under any earlier profile is re-Ranked.

**5. An offered-but-unentered Class Quest is unaffected as stored state, and is sized at entry.** `class_quest.status` does not move on adoption. A quest standing `offered` under 1.66 stands `offered` under 1.67; when the Bearer enters, the trial sizes to his Combat Tier at that instant under Section 18.2. This is prospective rule application, not a state transformation — the offer itself carries no Rank to migrate.

**6. An unentered Class Quest gains the phase structure; an entered one is never re-run.** Sections 18.2.1–18.2.3 are prospective and apply to a trial entered from adoption forward. No campaign in this repository has a promoted Class Quest clear, so nothing is re-adjudicated — but the rule is stated for the general case: **a trial already resolved stands exactly as promoted**, with the class it granted and the signals it tallied, and no Bearer re-enters a trial to be re-measured under a later profile. A class is held once for the campaign (Section 18.1) and this edge does not reopen one.

**7. No campaign owes a re-audit.** Nothing consumed a stored Combat Tier at resolution time, because none existed. No promoted Event, counter, or progression audit derives from either section.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.66-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.67 at readiness.
