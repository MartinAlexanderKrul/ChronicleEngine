## What 1.64 changed

**Section 9.4 gated every high-Rank contract in the world on a word it never defined.** The party-minimum table has read `8 (B-Rank-capable, incl. ≥1 B)` since 1.6, and the prose above it says only *combat-rated hunters of adequate rank*. **Adequacy was never authored.** For eighteen months of campaign time that cost nothing, because no campaign had a party large enough to test it.

**Gatefall: Pendragon produced one.** A crew of five B-Ranks and six C-Ranks, formed at the first crew training, asked the obvious question: can it legally take a B-Rank Gate? The profile could not answer. Read one way the minimum needs eight carded B-Ranks and the crew is three short; read the other it needs eight hunters *capable* of B-Rank work with at least one carded B, and the crew clears it immediately. **Nothing in the file chose between them**, and the difference is the entire ceiling of a campaign.

**The `≥1 B` clause is the evidence, and it decides it.** That clause is redundant under the strict reading — if all eight must be carded B, saying *including at least one B* adds nothing. It carries meaning only if the other seven need not be. The A-Rank row uses the identical construction (`16 (incl. at least one A-Rank)`), and the C-Rank row, needing no such protection, omits it. The table was already written for the permissive reading; only the definition was missing.

**Ruled: a hunter counts toward a Gate's legal minimum when their card Rank is at the Gate's Rank or one Rank below it, and nothing else.**

- A **C-Rank** counts toward a **B-Rank** Gate's eight.
- A **D-Rank** counts toward a **C-Rank** Gate's four.
- An **E-Rank** counts toward neither.

**Capability is a card threshold, not a judgment.** No board, regulator, or party leader assesses whether a particular hunter is *really* ready. A legal minimum resting on opinion would be unenforceable at the mouth and unprovable in a hearing afterward, which is exactly why the law counts cards and not competence — the same reason Section 19's re-assessment procedure measures a Mana pool rather than asking anyone how good they are.

**The `≥1` clauses are what stop an all-lower-tier party.** Eight C-Ranks cannot lawfully take a B-Rank Gate; seven plus one B can. **C-Rank deliberately carries no such clause**, so four D-Ranks may lawfully enter a C-Rank Gate — C being the trade's ordinary working tier and D-Ranks doing C-Rank work being the common career rather than the exception, as Section 9.4's own support-tier paragraph already describes. The law's floor rises where the beasts stop being survivable by the tier beneath; it does not rise merely because a Gate is worth more.

**One Rank and not two, and the reason is structural.** Section 2 fixes Rank at Awakening and Section 4 makes Rank the whole of an awakened's capability. A two-Rank gap is therefore not a hard fight but a different weight class, and a minimum permitting it would be a casualty report the law had signed in advance. One Rank below is the stretch the trade already makes daily; two is the thing a minimum exists to forbid.

**It is a legal threshold and not a survival guarantee.** Meeting the minimum makes an entry lawful and nothing more. Section 9.7's population formula does not soften for a party that scraped its floor, and Section 20.4's lethality rules are untouched.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.63 → 1.64.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.64"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, Stat, level, XP, System Rank, Card Rank, skill, mastery, counter, title, item, gold, cash, quest, or candidate is touched. No NPC's card Rank moves, and no hunter becomes more or less capable than they were — this edge authors what an already-existing number *counts*, not what anyone can do.

**3. Nothing retroactive, and no past entry is re-adjudicated.** Every Gate entry resolved under 1.63 or earlier stands exactly as promoted, lawful or otherwise. In particular this edge does **not** retroactively legalize an under-strength entry: a party that was short of its minimum under the old ambiguity is not cured by the new definition, and any campaign holding such a finding keeps it. Where a past entry's legality was never adjudicated, it remains unadjudicated rather than being settled here by implication.

**4. The rule is prospective and applies from adoption forward**, to entries audited after the adopting campaign's anchor.

**5. No campaign owes a roster re-audit.** Existing party records need no recomputation; the definition is read at the point an entry is assessed, not stored on any roster.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.63-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.64 at readiness.
