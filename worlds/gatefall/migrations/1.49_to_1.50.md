## What 1.50 changed

**Section 15.3.2 named five groups and defined none of them.** `/system gear` renders the dimensional inventory as **Gear · Consumables · Keys · Materials · Special**, and each group's rule carries its live line count — a number the profile prints without ever saying which holdings produce it. That is the Section 20.2 defect in its purest form: a stated figure resting on an unstated rule, decided fresh at every render by whoever is rendering.

**The kinds are now defined, with a deterministic precedence order.** Keys, then Consumables, then Special, then Gear, then Materials — first match wins, and a holding sits in exactly one group. The order is what makes the boundaries decidable rather than tasteful:

- A **key** is a consumable by nature and is still a **Key**, because Section 17 makes instant-dungeon keys their own economy.
- An **unidentified core** is **Special**, not Materials, because its Rank is precisely what is not yet known. It moves to Materials on the identification that resolves it — a state change with an Event behind it, not a re-reading of unchanged facts.
- A stored **weapon** is **Gear** while the **crystals** from the same Gate are **Materials**.

**Special absorbs the unresolved.** Anything whose worth is not mechanical — evidence, documents, quest-bearing objects, named artifacts (Section 11.6) — and **any item whose function, Rank, or provenance is unresolved**. An unidentified thing is Special until identification moves it, which gives every ambiguous holding a defined home instead of a coin flip between Gear and Materials.

**`system_state.inventory` is restructured to match**, holding one list per kind. A panel now renders five groups from five lists and takes each count from its list's length. Nothing about the grouping is inferred at render time, no holding can appear in two groups, and no holding can sit in a kind the table does not name.

**This closes the second half of a defect whose first half was repaired at `EVT-000366`.** A `/system gear` call had silently omitted a real possession — the Gate Direction Finder — because the field it renders from was prose a renderer had to interpret twice: once to ask whether an entry named an item at all, and again to ask which group it belonged to. `EVT-000366` fixed the first question by normalizing the field to one holding per entry. 1.50 fixes the second by making the answer structural.

**No holding, quantity, Rank, provenance, or owned object changes.** This is a reshaping of how holdings are stored and grouped, not of what is owned.

## Migration procedure

**Required 1.49 → 1.50 migration.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Group the existing inventory by kind.** Replace the flat `system_state.inventory` list with one list per kind — `keys`, `consumables`, `special`, `gear`, `materials` — and assign every existing holding by Section 15.3.2's precedence order. **Every holding's text, Rank line, provenance, and quantity carries over verbatim.** A holding is moved, never rewritten, and the total count before and after must be equal; a migration that changes what is owned while claiming to regroup it has failed.

**2. Assign by the order, not by impression.** Test Keys, then Consumables, then Special, then Gear, then Materials, and take the first match. Where a holding's kind is genuinely arguable, the Special rule decides it: if function, Rank, or provenance is unresolved, it is Special. Do not leave a holding in two lists and do not invent a sixth kind.

**3. An empty kind is still a kind.** A list with no holdings is retained as an empty list rather than omitted, because Section 15.3.2 requires a group with no live holdings to render `none` — a group that vanished from state cannot render anything.

**4. Nothing else moves.** `equipment` is untouched: worn slots stay separate from stored holdings exactly as Section 12.9 distinguishes them, and the `/system gear` panel still draws WORN from the slot keys and STORED from these lists. No pool, Stat, level, System Rank, skill, title, counter, gold, cash, quest, or deadline is touched, and no Event is reopened.

**5. Disposal and history stay where they belong.** A disposed holding does not enter any kind: Section 15.1 keeps it out of every panel, and it is recorded in the campaign's ownership ledger. Session narrative belongs to the campaign chronicle. Neither is a holding and neither may be stored in `inventory` under any kind.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.49-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.50 at readiness.
