# Purpose

This is the **intake queue** for design friction surfaced during ordinary play, in any world or campaign, before it becomes anything else.

- `002_ENGINE_ROADMAP.md` records *planned* work — something already scoped for a version.
- `001_ENGINE_DECISIONS.md` records *accepted* architectural choices — something already ruled on.
- `004_DESIGN_FLAGS.md` (this document) records *raised, undecided* concerns — something a player or the Runtime noticed during play that nobody has yet decided what to do with.

A flag is not a Decision and not a Roadmap commitment. It becomes one of those only when an owner actually rules on it — through the normal Decision or Roadmap process — at which point this entry is marked **Actioned** with a pointer to where it landed. A flag the owner rules against is marked **Dismissed** with the reason. Neither disposition edits campaign canon; this file governs no gameplay fact.

---

# Format

Each flag gets a stable ID (`F-001`, `F-002`, ...), never reused. New flags append at the end; existing entries are only ever amended for a status change, never deleted.

```text
## F-NNN — <short title>

**Raised:** <real date> · **Source:** <campaign/session it came from, or "cross-campaign">
**Status:** Open | Actioned (→ Decision/Roadmap ref) | Dismissed (reason)

<what was observed during play, and what about it felt like an engine-level gap
rather than a one-off narration choice — enough context for someone with no
memory of the session to evaluate it.>
```

# Flags

## F-001 — Openings from established NPC contacts default to uniform decline

**Raised:** 2026-07-31 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-10 in-fiction session

During live play, the player had the protagonist text four NPCs he already has real established rapport with, asking after open work. All four came back "nothing right now," each individually grounded in that NPC's own prior state — but the player pushed back that a real, multi-contact proactive action drawing on established relationships should have a real chance of surfacing a genuine opening, not fabricated from nothing, rather than the Runtime defaulting every contact to uniform decline and then treating it as the player's job to manufacture an opening some other way.

**The open design question:** does `docs/AI_GAMEPLAY_RESIDENT_CORE.md` or `docs/AI_GAMEPLAY_RUNTIME_PROFILE.md` need explicit guidance for handling a player's proactive outreach to multiple established contacts at once — e.g., a principle that such an action should weight toward surfacing at least one previously-seeded opportunity rather than uniform decline, while still never inventing something that contradicts established state. Worth reviewing the Resident Core's Narrative Momentum and Profile-Declared Proactive Trigger sections, and the Runtime Profile's Returning/Takeover and Derived Operation Plans sections, to see where (or whether) this belongs, and whether it is resident-core-level (all worlds) or per-world-authored.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-07-31)** → Decision 082 (Pending World-Side Commitments), Decision 083 (Opportunity Supply Advances on the World's Clock), and Resident Core 1.13 (*A Canvass Is N Resolutions, and Standing Is Read, Not Recalled*). Full analysis and evidence: `docs/430_RUNTIME_PERSISTENCE_VALIDATION/438_F001_MULTI_CONTACT_OUTREACH_ANALYSIS.md`.

The flag's own proposed remedy — weighting a canvass toward surfacing a seeded opportunity — is **rejected as written**, and the reasoning behind it is **accepted and implemented proactively instead**. Reactive weighting fails Rules Section 1 and Law VIII and makes canvassing farmable; supply advancing on the world's clock delivers the same intent without raising any probability in the player's favour. The distinction is recorded in Decision 083's Alternatives Considered so it outlives this entry.

**Dispatch deltas delivered 2026-07-31 as Decision 084 and Gatefall Profile 1.44** (`EVT-000268`), completing disposition item 4. Authoring them exposed that the trigger manifest's settlement vocabulary had no member a world-side settlement could declare, so R4 needed a foundational enabler rather than ordinary world authoring. Two findings remain *not* covered by this disposition: the Gatefall backfill of live commitments and supply sources is campaign-canon work for a play session, and the unsettled tracked board is raised separately as F-002.

## F-002 — Gatefall's tracked board stopped counting

**Raised:** 2026-07-31 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, found while analysing F-001

`110_WORLD_LEDGER.md` carries four tracked Gate postings under Profile Section 9.10, snapshotted at the 2026-08-05 12:15 anchor. At the campaign's current anchor of 2026-08-10 07:38, **three of the four derived deadlines have passed** — `GB-01` at midnight 2026-08-08, `GB-02` and `GB-03` at midnight 2026-08-09 — and none appears to have been settled. `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` still reports `tracked_postings: 4`, annotated "unchanged from Checkpoint 0043".

Section 9.10 makes deadline resolution deterministic and discretion-free: `staffed` or `held` clears off-screen, `withdrawn` leaves the board, and `posted` **breaks** under Section 9.8. `GB-01` and `GB-02` were both `posted` and under floor at the snapshot, so absent an intervening staffing change both settle to breaks — and Section 9.10 is explicit that institutions avert breaks by changing staffing status, which is itself something that had to be settled across those five days. `GB-04` remains inside its window (deadline midnight 2026-08-12) with staffing unsettled.

This is distinct from F-001. F-001 concerned how a question was answered; this is a clock that stopped in a mechanism authored specifically to stop postings evaporating when nobody is looking. It owes no roll and no discretion — only settlement.

**The open question is not what the rule says but why it did not fire**, and whether Section 9.10 needs a declared audit boundary the way Decisions 082 and 083 gave commitments and supply one, or whether this is purely a settlement lapse in play. Resolving the board itself is campaign canon and belongs in a session under normal save discipline; it is not an engine change.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-07-31)** — the board settled in play at `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, `EVT-000270`: `GB-01` and `GB-02` broke, `GB-03` cleared off-screen, `GB-04` remains live. The underlying engine question (whether Section 9.10 needs a declared audit boundary, per the open question above) remains genuinely unaddressed — this closes the campaign-canon lapse only, not the design question. Settling it surfaced a distinct authoring gap, raised separately as **F-004**.

**It recurred, and the engine question is now answered (2026-08-06).** `GB-04` broke at midnight 2026-08-12 and was settled **four days late** by owner ruling at `EVT-000413` — after Decision 084 and Gatefall Profile 1.44 had given the board its own trigger domain specifically so this could not happen. **The remedy was in place and the failure repeated unchanged**, which is what makes the cause worth stating rather than the instance.

**Why it could not fire, and it is not a lapse in play.** Section 9.10 says *"when a tracked posting reaches its deadline, exactly one of the following settles, with no roll and no Runtime discretion"* — a condition with **no evaluation point**. It names what settles and never says who checks or when. The only route offered was `gatefall.board.deadline`, dispatched under the resident rule *"dispatch only domains whose declared `candidate_deltas` intersect the settled changes"*, against four declared deltas: `clock.advanced`, `supply.advanced`, `commitment.due`, `outreach.initiated`. **Each of those four appears in the repository only in the declaration that consumes it**, plus prose about that declaration. Nothing emits any of them; `scene.opened` is the same. The intersection the dispatcher tests can never be non-empty, so the domain was unreachable from the day it was authored.

**The answer to the flag's own question is yes, and the boundary already existed for everything except deadlines.** Turn-State Settlement item 2 settles elapsed time *unconditionally* — commitments (Decision 082), standing needs (Decision 088), opportunity supply (Decision 083), and contested openings (Decision 089) all settle because time moved, with no dispatch and no delta. A **deadline is the fourth thing time does to the world** and was named in none of them.

**And it is the one obligation no dispatch can reach**, which is the generalizable half. The other three are reached by *someone doing something* — an owner owing, a source producing, a claimant taking — so the exchange settling them has an event to notice. A deadline is reached by time alone, and elapsed time is the background against which settlement happens rather than a change anyone settles. A dispatcher keyed to what an exchange *changed* is therefore structurally blind to it, and the absence is indistinguishable from nothing having been due.

**Fixed engine-side (2026-08-06), not world-side, deliberately.** The defect is not Gatefall's board — it is the engine settling three of four time-driven obligations — so a profile patch would have left the next world to rediscover it. `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` Section 2.4 gains *A declared deadline settles at the same boundary* as the fifth member of that series, with the reasoning and the prohibition on the manifest being the sole route; the Resident Core's Turn-State Settlement item 2 gains the per-turn clause and the one-line reason. Both are pinned by `tools/test_commitment_contract.ps1`, verified to fail when the resident clause is removed. **Refinement under Decision 069** — Decisions 082/083/088/089 already established the principle and this names its fourth case; no ADR, no Rules or Data Model change. Promote it to a Decision if the owner wants the fourth case recorded as an architectural choice rather than a completion of the existing one.

**Still open, and wider than this flag:** the `candidate_deltas` mechanism has consumers and no producers *anywhere*. Every trigger domain gated on one dispatches only when the Runtime judges that a named delta "happened," which is recall rather than a read — `F-005`'s class applied to dispatch. Closing the deadline route removes the only case where that was silently fatal; it does not give the vocabulary an emitter, and no gate detects the next domain declared against a delta nothing produces.

## F-003 — Repeated NPC-knowledge leak on the same closed channel within one scene

**Raised:** 2026-07-31 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-11 in-fiction session

During a single short scene (Alexander and Owen discussing how to commission a weapon for Owen), the Runtime had Owen reference Walt Adamik — by inference and then by name — **twice**, despite `130_NPCS_AND_FACTIONS.md`'s existing Closed Channels table already recording that Owen has no channel to Alexander's dealings with Walt. The player caught and corrected the leak the first time (Owen inferring "the pawnbroker's guy... the one who doesn't ask" from context that only the player and Runtime shared); the Runtime was told to re-narrate from Owen's real knowledge, apparently retracted the reference, and then reintroduced Walt by name two exchanges later in the same scene, prompting a second correction.

This is the same class of defect the existing Owen/Walt and Owen/Kesha rows in the Closed Channels table were written to catch — "the same leak has recurred three and four times in one session," per that table's own framing note — recurring again after an in-scene correction had just been made and accepted. The npc-knowledge skill's channel test was run and passed once, then silently not re-run on the very next NPC line touching the same fact.

**The open design question:** whether the Resident Core's turn-by-turn discipline needs an explicit "closed-channel facts stay suppressed for the rest of the scene, not just the next line" rule — i.e., once a fact is confirmed closed to an NPC mid-scene, treat it as a standing constraint on every subsequent line for that NPC in that scene (and arguably that session), not a check that resets after one corrected turn. Worth reviewing whether this belongs in the `npc-knowledge` skill itself, the Resident Core's per-turn checklist, or as a lightweight scene-local working-memory convention (e.g., restate active closed channels for present NPCs at scene start).

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-01)** → `.agents/` and `.claude/` `npc-knowledge` and `save` skills; `tools/test_npc_channel_contract.ps1`. Full analysis: `docs/430_RUNTIME_PERSISTENCE_VALIDATION/439_NPC_GROUNDING_ANALYSIS.md`.

**The flag's proposed remedy is not what was wrong.** The rule it asks for already existed — the resident layer states that a correction "does not inoculate a later turn, and does not generalize to a different fact," and a gate has pinned that since 2026-07-28. The defect was that **four documents governed recording the ruling and two pointed the wrong way.** The Resident Core says record it "so the ruling outlives the scene"; the Closed Channels table says add a row "the moment a channel is ruled closed… do not wait for a checkpoint"; but the `npc-knowledge` skill said to "hold the closed channel as a pending ruling in conversation rather than writing it to the NPC ledger file mid-scene," and the `save` skill named an NPC-knowledge ruling among the deltas held until `/save`. Only the resident leg was gated, so the conflict passed every check.

A Runtime following the fetched skills holds the ruling in conversation, nothing durable exists two exchanges later, and the fact leaks again inside the same scene — which is precisely what the table's rows record, twice on one fact and three times on another, each after an accepted correction. Both skills now carve out the closed-channel write as the single deliberate exception to the mid-scene prohibition, narrow by construction because the row mints no identifier, bumps no provenance, and changes no canonical state. The gate pins the agreement across all four documents and asserts the two skill mirrors stay byte-identical.

This closes the *recording* half only. Whether the Runtime performs the grounding read at all is F-005/F-006/F-007's finding and is not mechanically checkable; see the analysis.

## F-004 — Section 9.5's true-Rank roll has no trigger for an unconfirmed Gate that breaks unentered

**Raised:** 2026-07-31 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, found while resolving F-002 (`EVT-000270`)

Settling the tracked board (`EVT-000270`) hit a genuine authoring gap on `GB-02`, an unconfirmed D-Rank Gate that sat under-bid until its deadline and broke under Section 9.8 without ever being entered. Section 9.5 resolves an unconfirmed Gate's true Rank **on first entry**, on a d100 — the only trigger the profile names for that roll. `GB-02` had no entry, so the roll had nothing to fire on, and Section 9.10's break rule (deterministic settlement from staffing status) says nothing about Rank at all.

**The owner ruling made in play, for this instance only:** consistent with Section 9.10's own "no roll" character for deadline settlement, an unconfirmed Gate that breaks without ever being entered never triggers the Section 9.5 roll — the assessed Rank is what the board, the institutions, and the resulting population respond to. `GB-02` broke as its assessed D-Rank, population rolled from that Rank.

**The open design question:** whether Section 9.10 (or Section 9.5 itself) should author this case explicitly — an unconfirmed Gate's Rank-at-break when no entry ever occurred — rather than leaving it to a per-instance ruling each time an unconfirmed posting goes unstaffed to its deadline, which Section 9.4's own economics (cheap jobs sit) suggests will not be rare. Worth reviewing alongside Section 9.6's anomaly table, since an entered unconfirmed Gate can anomaly-roll to a Rank swing that an unentered break, under the ruling above, can never produce.

**Status:** **ACTIONED (2026-08-12) → Gatefall Profile 1.74, edge 1** (`EVT-000533`). Section 9.5 now resolves an unconfirmed Gate's true Rank **at the break** as well as on first entry, on the same d100, the same table, and the same Section 9.6 anomaly band — **the roll is discovery, not creation.** What the world committed to was the *assessed* Rank, and that gap is the danger. **It supersedes the per-instance ruling at `EVT-000270`**, whose reasoning held only while breaks never reached the Bearer, which Profile 1.73's world tick stopped being true one day earlier; `GB-02` stands as ruled and no historical break is re-rolled. It also closes the asymmetry this flag named — an entered unconfirmed Gate could anomaly-roll to a swing an unentered break never could, so entry was the only road to the most interesting outcome. Both roads reach the same table now.

**The disposition itself is the finding worth keeping.** This sat *deferred to a play session* from 2026-08-01 to 2026-08-12, and **the play session never came, because breaks never reached the protagonist** — the very condition `F-034` was later raised about. A flag deferred until play surfaces it will wait forever if the thing that would surface it is itself broken. **Prior status, superseded:** Dispositioned (2026-08-01) — world authoring, deferred to a play session. Not Actioned and not Dismissed: the question is real and stands, but it is scoped entirely to `worlds/gatefall/206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Sections 9.5, 9.6 and 9.10, and Decision 062 with Decision 069 point 4 both establish that world authoring requires no engine decision and consumes no decision number. It is recorded in `030_ENGINE_CHANGELOG.md` and the world's own documents when it lands.

**It does not block the Version 0.3 Engine Postmortem**, which assesses engine architecture. The owner ruling already made in play (`EVT-000270`) governs the live board in the meantime: an unconfirmed Gate that breaks without ever being entered never triggers the Section 9.5 roll, and its assessed Rank stands. What remains is promoting that ruling from per-instance to authored, which is a profile edit under normal save discipline.

## F-005 — Two fresh NPC-knowledge channel violations back-to-back, neither caught before generation

**Raised:** 2026-08-01 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-11 in-fiction session (the return-from-checkpoint scene, immediately after `/continue`)

In a single short exchange (Alexander asking Owen what reputation is circulating about him), the Runtime committed two distinct, previously-unrecorded channel violations in consecutive turns, both caught by the player rather than by the Runtime's own pre-narration check:

1. Owen was written asserting he'd "never told" Alexander about the old "weakest E-Rank" reputation and reacting to it as live news — when the campaign chronicle (`EVT-000138`, 2026-08-04) already records Owen raising exactly this with Alexander a week earlier in-fiction. This wasn't a closed-channel leak (no rule barred Owen from knowing it) — it was the Runtime inventing an NPC's conversational history against an already-written record, without checking the chronicle first.
2. Immediately after correcting #1, the Runtime had Owen assert Alexander is "D-Rank... nobody's rewritten your file" — attributing knowledge of Alexander's **System Rank** to an NPC, when World Rule Profile Section 14.4 makes System Rank perceptible to the Bearer alone and the character sheet shows Alexander's public BGM card is still carded E-Rank. This is a channel violation of the same shape F-003 already named, but on a wholly new fact (System Rank, not the Walt Adamik thread), in a different session, with no prior Closed Channels row to have caught it.

Both are distinct from F-003's repeat-after-correction pattern: these were first-occurrence errors, not a re-leak of an already-ruled-closed fact, and both happened at the very start of a session (during the post-`/continue` recap scene) rather than deep in an established scene. This suggests the failure mode is broader than "closed-channel facts don't stay suppressed" (F-003's framing) — it looks more like NPC dialogue asserting things about the protagonist or world state is being generated from plausible-sounding recollection rather than from an actual grounding check against the chronicle and the World Rule Profile's information boundary, even on a session's very first substantive NPC line.

**The open design question:** whether the Resident Core's *Load a Recorded NPC Before Playing It* and *NPC Channel Check* sections need a stronger, more mechanical precondition — e.g., an explicit instruction that any NPC line asserting shared conversational history (not just present-tense fact-knowledge) must be checked against the chronicle before being written, and that any line implying knowledge of a Bearer-only System quantity (Rank, level, XP, Mana, quests) must be checked against the active World Rule Profile's Information Boundary section before being written — rather than relying on the general "channel check fires narrowly" framing, which two consecutive misses in one exchange suggests is not catching this class of error reliably even when a Closed Channels table has just been loaded into the working set at readiness.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-01) — consolidated with F-006 and F-007 into one finding.** `docs/430_RUNTIME_PERSISTENCE_VALIDATION/439_NPC_GROUNDING_ANALYSIS.md`, carried to the Version 0.3 Engine Postmortem.

These are not three defects. They are one — *an NPC's line is generated from what the Runtime recalls about that character rather than from a read of what canon says the character knows* — meeting three different surfaces: the chronicle (F-005a), the profile's information boundary (F-005b), an Event's summary prose (F-006), and ambiguous Current State prose (F-007). Fixing them individually yields a table of per-fact prohibitions and leaves the generator untouched, which is what four flags across two sessions already demonstrates.

The analysis records why no gate is available: the failure leaves no repository trace, because a line generated from recollection and one generated from a correct read are identical in the transcript when the recollection happens to be right. Decision 085 measured the nearest derived check at 38% false positives and rejected it on that measurement; the same decision states that its own `no-change` half "is not verifiable and is not pretended to be." The disposition is therefore a recorded architectural finding with its enforcement limit stated, not a patch.

## F-006 — F-005's exchange required a third correction before landing a clean answer

**Raised:** 2026-08-01 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-11 in-fiction session, same exchange as F-005

F-005 already recorded two channel violations in one short exchange (Owen's invented "never told you" conversational history, then his invented knowledge of Alexander's Bearer-only System Rank). Continuing the same exchange, the Runtime's very next attempt at a corrected answer had Owen cite **Ada Reyes and Dale Pruitt by name** as people whose vouching he was aware of — grounded, at the time, in `EVT-000138`'s summary phrasing ("Owen... flagged Ada, Dale, a Horizon contact, and now Ironline"). The player ruled this wrong too: that phrasing describes what the Event's own prose summarizes Owen as having flagged in aggregate, not a record of Alexander ever having given Owen those two names specifically, and treating a summary line as proof of an NPC's granular knowledge was itself the error. The player asked the Runtime to stop patching the exchange turn-by-turn and re-narrate the whole thing clean.

Three corrections inside one exchange, over what should have been a low-stakes opening beat of a returning session, is a materially worse instance of F-005's concern, not a duplicate of it: it shows the failure recurring even immediately after being named and logged, and it shows a second failure mode alongside F-005's two — treating an Event's own narrative *summary* prose as if it were a citable record of exactly which facts passed between two characters, when a summary line compresses in ways a direct provenance record does not.

**The open design question:** beyond F-005's proposed fixes, whether the Resident Core needs an explicit warning against treating chronicle *summary/description* prose as granular proof of what one character told another — i.e., a description line naming several facts in aggregate ("flagged X, Y, and Z") is not itself evidence that every named item was individually communicated, and asserting it as would-be grounding for an NPC's specific knowledge is a subtler version of the same "recollection instead of an actual read" failure the Resident Core already warns about for state and channel checks. Also worth asking whether three corrections in one exchange, immediately after the first two were logged, indicates the fix needs to land before the next NPC line is generated in-session, not just as a future engine change.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-01) — consolidated with F-005 and F-007.** See `docs/430_RUNTIME_PERSISTENCE_VALIDATION/439_NPC_GROUNDING_ANALYSIS.md` and the disposition recorded under F-005.

## F-007 — A fourth ungrounded NPC-knowledge claim landed immediately after the player reset the scene to get a clean start

**Raised:** 2026-08-01 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-11 in-fiction session, same session as F-005/F-006

After F-005 and F-006 (two, then a third, ungrounded claims about Owen's knowledge in one exchange), the player asked the Runtime to discard the whole scene and reset to right after the checkpoint load, hoping a clean restart would avoid repeating the pattern. The very next attempt at opening the scene had Owen ask Alexander about "whatever Wade Bishop wants tomorrow" — treating Owen as aware that Wade Bishop's follow-up call is specifically due. Checking the record: Owen does know Wade Bishop by name and was told the full account of the 2026-08-08 D-Rank trial (`EVT-000258`); nothing in his loaded entity block or the Closed Channels table establishes that he was also told the call is locked to a specific day. The detail ("Wade Bishop's own follow-up... locked to call tomorrow, 2026-08-11") appears in `180_CURRENT_STATE.md`'s narrative prose describing that evening, in a way that reads ambiguously as either part of what was told to Owen or a separate fact settled the same beat — and the Runtime again resolved that ambiguity by asserting Owen has the more specific knowledge, rather than checking or asking.

This is the same failure shape as F-006's Ada/Dale error (treating adjacent narrative prose as license for an NPC to know something not clearly established as communicated to them), but notable on its own because it occurred on the **very first Owen line after an explicit reset intended to produce a clean start**, meaning the reset was not sufficient by itself to interrupt the pattern — whatever is producing these claims is not scene-state carryover, it is happening fresh at each NPC-line generation.

**The open design question:** whether the underlying issue is that ambiguous provenance in `180_CURRENT_STATE.md`'s narrative prose (a paragraph mixing "what happened" with "what was told to whom") needs to be written more strictly to separate protagonist-only facts from facts actually communicated to a present NPC, and/or whether the Runtime needs a harder default rule for exactly this ambiguity: when a current-state or chronicle passage does not unambiguously state that an NPC was told a specific fact, default to the NPC not knowing it, rather than defaulting to the more informed reading. Four corrections across three consecutive exchange attempts, surviving an explicit scene reset, suggests this needs a structural fix rather than continued in-session vigilance.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-01) — consolidated with F-005 and F-006.** See `docs/430_RUNTIME_PERSISTENCE_VALIDATION/439_NPC_GROUNDING_ANALYSIS.md` and the disposition recorded under F-005.

This flag carries the one contributing cause that is *not* engine work and is recorded separately so the postmortem does not mistake it for one: `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` paragraphs mix what happened with what was told to whom, and that ambiguity is what the Runtime resolved toward the more-informed reading. Writing those passages to separate protagonist-only facts from communicated ones is world and campaign authoring, and belongs to a play session. Its own observation stands and is the reason it did not become a fourth patch: the failure landed on the first NPC line after an explicit scene reset, so it is not scene-state carryover — it runs fresh at each line.

## F-008 — "Nothing named in canon yet" should default to authoring it, not deferring or citing a supply-cadence rule against the player

**Raised:** 2026-08-01 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-11 in-fiction session

The player asked Kesha Morrison, an established, well-connected NPC, to help find a crewless C-Rank hunter for a new crew. No such person existed anywhere in canon. Rather than simply authoring one — exactly as the Runtime has done throughout this campaign for Corinne Whitlock, Elias Ward, Walt Adamik, and others whenever a scene needed a new contact — the Runtime instead had Kesha defer ("give me a day or two"), and when pressed, justified the deferral by citing **Decision 083 (Opportunity Supply Advances on the World's Clock)**. The player correctly identified this as a misapplication: Decision 083 governs *automatic job/contract supply* from an established source's clock-advancing cadence, specifically to stop the Runtime from inventing farmable opportunities at the moment of asking. It says nothing about naming a new person, and citing it here just relitigated a settled engine question as if it barred an entirely ordinary act of world-authoring. The Runtime corrected course only after the player explicitly named the mismatch and had to argue for why an AI-run world requires exactly this kind of on-demand authoring to function at all — the player cannot pre-author every NPC who might plausibly exist, and refusing to fill an ordinary gap defeats the point of running the engine.

Once corrected, authoring a real candidate (Reyna Castillo, `ENT-000182`) — grounded in already-established texture (the Coalition's thin C-Rank pool from the same sitting's institution-ledger ruling, and the "crew failure that maims or kills" theme the player's own pitch to Kesha had just raised) — took one clean pass and produced something usable immediately. The friction was entirely in the Runtime defaulting to withholding rather than authoring.

**The open design question:** whether the Resident Core or Runtime Profile needs an explicit default rule distinguishing *when an NPC should say "I don't know, let me check"* (deferring to genuine world-clock supply per Decision 083 — jobs, contracts, market openings from a source's own cadence) from *when the Runtime itself should simply author a new grounded entity on the spot* (a new named contact, vendor, or candidate the fiction calls for, with no existing canon to check against) — since the current failure mode treats these as the same case and defaults to the more restrictive one, requiring the player to argue the Runtime out of its own overcaution rather than the Runtime recognizing the distinction unprompted. Worth reviewing whether this belongs as an explicit clarifying note on Decision 083 itself (scoping it clearly to *supply from established sources*, not *first introduction of a new NPC*), a Resident Core addition under Narrative Momentum, or both.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-01)** → `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` Section 1.4 (third ambiguity class, *Unnamed but entailed*), Resident Core 1.16 (*Unnamed Is Not Absent — Author It*), and `tools/test_world_authoring_default.ps1`. Refinement under Decision 069, milestone 0.3.5; no ADR, no Rules or Data Model change.

**The flag's own diagnosis is amended by the transcript, and the amendment changed the fix.** The flag reads Decision 083 as the cause. The Runtime's recorded reasoning shows it was the *rationalization*: the decision to withhold was already taken one step earlier, on the bare inference **"I don't have any other named C-Rank freelancers without crew affiliations in the established lore, so I shouldn't fabricate one"** — stated as a premise, citing nothing. Decision 083 arrives afterwards, introduced as "the key insight is…", to justify a conclusion already reached. Scoping 083 alone would therefore have removed the citation and left the default intact.

The load-bearing equation is `absent from canon → fabrication → forbidden`, and the word doing the work is *fabricate*. The repository has correctly and repeatedly trained against invention — never invent a channel, never invent a precondition so a trigger can fire, never manufacture protagonist intent, never silently repair a broken reference by invention, never create an opportunity solely to reward the player. Each is right in its own scope. Together they generalized into a prior against authoring world material at all, and no rule contradicted that prior at the moment it fired, because every rule that grants authoring is phrased as a permission ("**may** generate detail") rather than a default.

**Nothing needed to be granted.** Rules 1.4 scopes its own restraint to detail that has not yet become relevant; Rules 3.10 lets an abstract entity become detailed when it affects play; Rules 2.1 makes newly generated information a canon tier; Runtime 1.3 permits unestablished detail consistent with canon; Runtime 8.3 says unestablished is not contradiction. Five grants, no default, and no class to file the case in — so it fell into Runtime 1.4's *unknown in-world*, whose own text correctly says it "does not force a clean answer."

The sharpest evidence is in the same transcript: the world declares **twenty-two C-Rank hunters citywide** and names a handful. The Runtime held that population count in context while reporting it could not name a member of it. That is what the new class is built on — where canon declares an aggregate and names a fraction, the unnamed remainder exists, and naming a member of it is authoring rather than invention.

Two further findings are recorded rather than actioned here. First, **deferral is the expensive branch, not the cheap one**: Kesha's "give me a day or two" is a Decision 082 pending world-side commitment that something must now settle, so withholding a name manufactures exactly the obligation F-001 found goes unsettled. Both documents now say so. Second, the player's own argument is the design rationale and is recorded so it outlives this entry — *"I can't as an author create a full world with every detail; that would make using AI obsolete."* On-demand authoring within canon's constraints is the engine's function, not a concession it makes.

**Not done:** Decision 083 is unedited. Accepted decisions are immutable history here, so its boundary is stated in Runtime 1.4 and the resident card and cross-referenced, rather than amended into the decision. If the owner wants that floor recorded in 083 itself, it needs a Revision Policy amendment or a short ADR.

## F-009 — A freshly authored NPC was given an already-established NPC's name

**Raised:** 2026-08-01 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-11 in-fiction session

Walt Adamik was played introducing a new grey-market contact — an unlicensed artificer he was brokering an introduction to — and the Runtime named her "Reyes." `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/135_CAST_IN_PLAY.md` already carries `ENT-000134`, **Ada Reyes**, an established, previously-played contractor logistics coordinator with her own relationship record (`REL-000064`) and history in this same campaign. The player caught the collision immediately and asked for a different name plus this flag.

This is a fresh instance of the on-demand authoring the Runtime was told to do more of after F-008 (author a new grounded entity rather than withhold), but it shows a gap on the other side of that fix: authoring a new NPC did not include checking the new name against the campaign's own cast roster (`135_CAST_IN_PLAY.md`) or NPC ledger (`130_NPCS_AND_FACTIONS.md`) before assigning it. Two distinct people sharing a surname in a mid-sized city isn't impossible, but nothing about this scene called for that coincidence deliberately — it read as the Runtime drawing a name that was already "in the air" from recent context (Ada Reyes's C-Rank harvest job was narrated only a few exchanges earlier in the same session) rather than checking for collision.

**The open design question:** whether the Resident Core's entity-authoring guidance (the F-008 dispatch — `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` Section 1.4, Resident Core 1.16) needs an explicit collision check as part of *how* a new entity is authored, not just a permission that it may be: before assigning a new NPC's name, check it against the campaign's cast roster and NPC/Notable Figures ledgers for the active scope, and pick a different name on any match, full or partial (surname alone counts), unless the fiction specifically calls for a coincidental shared name. Worth reviewing whether this belongs as a sub-clause of Resident Core 1.16 itself or as a general naming-hygiene note applying to any freshly authored Character-type entity, world or campaign scope alike.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-01)** → `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` Section 1.4, Resident Core 1.19, `tools/validate_repository.ps1`, and fixture N-01 in `tools/test_runtime_persistence_validation.ps1`. Refinement under Decision 069; no ADR.

**This flag is the cost of the F-008 fix, and it arrived within a day of it.** Telling the Runtime to author entailed subjects rather than withhold them was correct and is not walked back — but the permission carried no obligation about *how* to author, and the first thing that went wrong was the cheapest thing to get wrong. The flag's own diagnosis is right and is worth preserving: the name that surfaces while authoring is disproportionately one the session used a few exchanges earlier, so the collision is **likelier than chance rather than unlucky**. That is why a rule was needed and not merely more care.

The disposition splits along what is decidable. **The exact collision is mechanical**: two live entities in one campaign may not carry the same current name, and `validate_repository.ps1` now rejects it, with N-01 proving the rejection fires against an isolated copy. Former and alternate aliases are deliberately exempt, because a name colliding with a *previous* name is how identity continuity records a rename. **The partial collision stays a judgment**: two people sharing a surname in a city of millions is ordinary, the flag says so itself, and a barrier that failed on it would be wrong. That case is carried by the resident rule — check the cast roster before assigning the name — and the barrier does not adjudicate it.

Scope is per campaign. The same name in two campaigns is two unrelated fictions, not a collision.

**Follow-up (2026-08-02) — the split was right and was applied at the wrong layer.** Reviewing this disposition surfaced the consequence it does not state: the mechanical half fires at a durability boundary, which can be an entire session after the name entered play, and the partial half — which is what this flag actually *was* — is adjudicated by nothing mechanical at any point. `validate_repository.ps1` would not have failed F-009. The barrier is not at fault for that; it is sited where a rejection arrives too late to be anything but a retraction inside the fiction, and `EVT-000280` is that retraction.

What was missed is that the *comparison* is decidable at both moments and only the *cost of acting on it* differs. At the barrier a partial match has no remedy but to reject correct canon — `ENT-000126` Tanya Voss and `ENT-000128` Elias Voss are siblings, both live, and `ENT-000136` Priya Okafor shares a surname with `ENT-000107` Naomi Okafor across the scope boundary. At authoring time the remedy is a different name and nothing else. So the same split moves one layer earlier: `tools/check_name_collision.ps1` returns `BLOCKED` for what the barrier will reject and `REVIEW` for what remains the author's judgment, `source_loading.entity_authoring_check` declares it so it is addressed rather than remembered, and the barrier is unchanged. Resident Core 1.19 → 1.20, and the clause is replaced in place rather than appended — the card comes out two tokens lighter.

**The honest limit:** nothing gates a token before it is emitted, and this does not pretend to. What changes is that "I checked" becomes an act with an answer the author can be wrong about and see, rather than a recollection indistinguishable from one nobody had — the distinction Decision 080's explicit `none` and Decision 085's explicit `no-change` are both built on. The resident clause remains the load-bearing layer and remains inside the enforcement class with no mechanical proxy. This narrows that class by one case; it does not close it. Nor does it rest on a recurrence: the clause added on 2026-08-01 has no recorded failure, and the argument here is architectural — a checker sited after the thing it checks — with F-009 itself as the played evidence.

Specified at `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-02-naming-time-collision-gate.md`; nineteen legs in `tools/test_name_collision_check.ps1`, of which the load-bearing one requires the **real** validator to reject a name the check called `BLOCKED`, so the two cannot drift into disagreeing.

## F-010 — `new_checkpoint.ps1`'s internal validation cannot ever find the evidence log synchronized

**Raised:** 2026-08-02 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, `/save` attempted at the close of the 2026-08-13 in-fiction evening session

Three consecutive `tools/new_checkpoint.ps1` invocations failed at the same internal step — `Repository validation FAILED (1 error(s)): Generated validation evidence block is not synchronized` — despite `tools/generate_validation_evidence.ps1` having been run immediately beforehand each time, and despite a standalone `tools/validate_repository.ps1` passing cleanly against the same live state each time. The observation is exactly right, and no external ordering of the two commands could satisfy the check.

**The originally recorded mechanism was wrong, and is superseded by what follows.** The first diagnosis held that the helper creates its `.900_CHECKPOINT_<NNNN>.staging-<token>` directory *before* its internal repository-validation call, and that `generate_validation_evidence.py`'s un-excluded `saves/` glob then counted it. The sequence in `tools/new_checkpoint.py` is the other way around: preflight `validate_repository.ps1` runs before the staging directory is created, and by the post-promotion validation call the staging directory has already been `os.replace`d into its canonical name. **No staging directory exists while either validation gate runs**, so a glob that ignored them would have changed nothing about the failure.

**The actual cause** is that promotion itself invalidates the evidence block, and nothing regenerated it. The block records a checkpoint count derived from the campaign's own save manifests; `os.replace(staging, final)` makes that count stale in the same instant it makes the checkpoint real. The helper already handled the exactly analogous case for the runtime index — it calls `generate_runtime_index.ps1` twice, generate then `-Check`, immediately after promotion — but had no corresponding call for the evidence generator anywhere in its sequence. So preflight passed against a synchronized block, promotion advanced the count past it, and the post-promotion `validate_repository.ps1` gate failed on drift only the helper itself could have introduced. The helper could never pass its own gate on any repository where the evidence generator was present.

The `.staging-` directories that prompted the original diagnosis are created by the **rollback** path, which renames the failed checkpoint back under a recovery name — after the failure, not before it. They retain the manifest they were given, so the evidence generator did count them, and each failed run's residue then broke the *next* run's preflight with the same message from a different gate. That secondary contamination was real; it was a consequence of the failure rather than its cause.

**Consequence for the session that raised this:** the Save Algorithm's steps 1–5 completed and passed cleanly against live canon. Step 6 could not complete; no checkpoint was created and no restore pointer was advanced. The span was later found to have two further defects the failure had masked — `120_INVENTORY_AND_OWNERSHIP.md` was omitted from the derived target set despite the session moving cash, gold, and eight individually-tracked items, and play continued after the failed save without being promoted. Both are recorded in the campaign's own changelog and were repaired before `900_CHECKPOINT_0060`.

**Status:** **Actioned.** `tools/new_checkpoint.py` regenerates and `-Check`s the evidence block in a new `evidence-generation` phase between promotion and the post-promotion validation gate, mirroring the runtime-index handling, and guarded on the generator's presence exactly as `validate_repository.ps1` guards its own evidence check. The evidence document joins the byte-for-byte rollback set before it is written, so a later gate failure cannot leave the block advanced past a checkpoint that no longer exists. `generate_validation_evidence.py` additionally excludes dot-prefixed recovery directories from its `saves/` glob — not the fix, but it closes the secondary contamination independently. `tools/test_transactional_checkpoint.ps1` previously stubbed only the tools the helper already called, which is why a helper unable to pass its own gate shipped; its fixture now models the evidence coupling and its success leg fails without the fix.

## F-011 — A mastery ascension narrated in a promoted Event, but never written to its counters

**Raised:** 2026-08-02 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, found while closing the 2026-08-13 evening session for `900_CHECKPOINT_0060`

`EVT-000303`'s own promoted description states that Twin Fang and Exploit Pattern each ascended **Expert→Master** in the 2026-08-12 Beast Den clear, naming the thresholds crossed ("mastery 2/3→3/3 — **ascends Expert→Master**"). That Event's `counter_deltas` carry only `successful_uses` and `qualifying_scenes_total`. No `mastery_level` and no `mastery_progress` increment was ever written, for either skill.

So live state holds both skills at stored `mastery_level: 4` (Expert) with `mastery_progress: 2`, while the character sheet's rendered prose describes both as Master, mastery complete, at the Master multiplier — Twin Fang at **×1.60** against the ×1.45 its stored tier would give. Keen Sense shows the same shape from a different direction: stored Expert at 3/3, rendered Master. The drift is invisible to `validate_repository.ps1`, whose counter arithmetic is satisfied — the deltas and the current values agree with each other perfectly; what is missing was never claimed as a delta at all. It is `tools/test_gatefall_quest_contract.ps1` that fails, on the narrower invariant that Section 7.4's prose must render the counters it claims to render.

**Why this is a flag and not a repair.** The two readings lead to opposite mechanical outcomes. Treat the promoted description as authoritative and three skills advance a tier, which raises live damage multipliers; treat the counters as authoritative and three promoted ascension statements are retracted, one of them narrated to the player in the scene where it happened. Both are defensible, both change played mechanics, and the discrepancy predates the span being closed — it is already inside `900_CHECKPOINT_0059`. The engine changelog entry of 2026-08-02 recorded this test failure as "live campaign state from a session in progress" belonging to that session; this flag is that observation given an identifier and a diagnosis.

**The narrower design question underneath it:** a settlement that narrates a tier advance in prose while omitting the counter that carries it produces a state where every mechanical gate passes and the sheet still lies. `counter_deltas: []` on an Event that resolved a full dungeon clear is well-formed today. Whether a promotion-barrier settlement should be required to carry a `mastery_level` delta whenever its own description asserts an ascension — the same shape Decision 085 already applies to claimed-and-not-made promotions — is the generalizable part.

**Status:** **Actioned** at `900_CHECKPOINT_0061` — owner ruled **toward the ascension**. `EVT-000336` carries the counter deltas the original settlements omitted: Twin Fang and Exploit Pattern each `mastery_level +1` / `mastery_progress -2`, ratifying the ascensions `EVT-000303`'s description narrates; Keen Sense the same with a `-3` reset, its progress having already stood at the 3/3 threshold from an earlier Event. Repaired forward as a new Event on the `EVT-000237` precedent rather than by editing promoted canon.

**Rupture was excluded, and the exclusion is the part worth keeping.** `EVT-000304`'s description makes the identical `ascends Expert→Master` claim, but three independent lines of evidence refuse it: its counter arithmetic reaches Expert honestly (baseline 5 at `EVT-000158`, then `-2` at `EVT-000162`'s Rank ascension and `+1` at `EVT-000238`); its *rendered numbers* are Expert's — ×2.45 and Mana 9, where D-Rank Master is ×2.60; and its stored progress is 1, so the `-2` reset every ascension carries would drive a counter negative. Only its star-label read Master, and that label was corrected down. **The tie-break that emerged: count the independent signals, and never accept a settlement that produces an impossible counter.** Twin Fang had narration *and* Master's own multiplier against one lagging counter; Rupture had narration alone against arithmetic, rendered effect, and possibility.

**No skill's rendered effect changed for any of the four** — three skills' typed state caught up to what the sheet already rendered and play already resolved, and one label came down to what its own numbers already said. The damage previews in `tools/test_gatefall_quest_contract.ps1` were separately repinned from effective Strength 51 to 62, where they had been stale since the Level 14 advance and unrelated to this ruling.

**The generalizable half remains open and is worth a Decision.** A promotion-barrier settlement can still narrate a tier advance in prose while omitting the counter that carries it, and every mechanical gate passes: `validate_repository.ps1`'s counter arithmetic is satisfied, because what is missing was never claimed as a delta at all. Requiring an Event whose description asserts an ascension to carry a matching `mastery_level` delta — the shape Decision 085 already applies to claimed-and-not-made promotions — would close it. Until then the only detector is a contract test that is not one of the checkpoint's gates.

## F-012 — Empty `counter_deltas` on completed dangerous scenes, and the Section 7.5 offers it silently suppressed

**Raised:** 2026-08-04 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, found during a player-instructed skill audit at the 2026-08-15 06:00 anchor, immediately after `900_CHECKPOINT_0068`
**Status:** **Actioned (→ Decision 090, Gatefall Profile 1.52)** — all four open questions ruled, 2026-08-04

**Disposition.** Question 1 and question 4 are answered together by **skill-credit coverage** (Profile Section 14.3): within a declared set of danger-bearing Event kinds, an Event naming the Bearer carries either a `skills.*` counter delta or an explicit `progression_audits` entry with `domain: gatefall.skill_credit`, `result: none`. It reuses an existing block for its existing meaning and needs no Data Model change. Question 2 is answered by making Section 7.5's readiness check **standing** — evaluated from stored state at every barrier, reaching no history. Question 3 is answered in Section 7.4: **a skill at Master still earns and still writes its qualifying scenes**, delta included, even though no rendered value moves. Enforcement of question 1/4 is in `tools/validate_repository.ps1`; questions 2 and 3 are instruction, sited resident and in the profile, and Decision 090 says so rather than implying a gate.

**Question 4's proposed detector was measured and does not work.** The flag asked whether a rule that an Event of kind `combat` naming the Bearer must carry a `skills.*` delta would close this. **It catches neither `EVT-000327` nor `EVT-000332` — both are `kind: scene`.** That result changed the finding: the root cause is a **misclassified Event**, one wrong `kind` disabling Decision 080's `progression_audits` coverage and every skill-counter obligation at the same time. A prose backstop was measured too and rejected — a resolved-clear signature scan fires on 18 post-baseline Events, most of them administrative records merely mentioning a past clear, the noise ratio that rejected Decision 085's derived check. The residual exposure — a dangerous scene misfiled as `scene` still escapes — is real, one line wide, and recorded in Decision 090 rather than left implicit.

**A second gap surfaced while settling this one and is ruled in the same decision:** eight mastery-tracked skills appeared in **neither** Section 7.3 ladder table, so Rank reached them only as a number, and for the earned techniques whose damage reads weapon power rather than a Rank baseline it reached them not at all — Twin Fang stood at Master, below the ceiling, and was **permanently ascension-ineligible**. Profile 1.52 authors a third table so every mastery-tracked skill has a row, with *magnitude only* stated explicitly where that is the design. See `F-014`.

**This is F-011's explicitly-deferred generalizable half, observed firing.** F-011 closed its own case by owner ruling but left this on the record: *"`counter_deltas: []` on an Event that resolved a full dungeon clear is well-formed today."* It is, and it has been used.

**What was found.** `EVT-000327` and `EVT-000332` are both complete solo instant-dungeon clears — populations of 12 and 12, both narrated as cleared, one explicitly "solo clear, no damage taken" — and both carry `counter_deltas: []`. Not a missing entry for one skill: **no skill counter of any kind for either scene.** Separately, `EVT-000340` (a 24-common Hive swarm) records `skills.twin_fang.successful_uses, delta: 10` with no qualifying scene for Twin Fang, and no Dagger Mastery entry at all despite every one of those ten strike-pairs being thrown with two equipped Quickknives. Dagger Mastery's last recorded use anywhere is `EVT-000339`.

**The consequence that makes it more than bookkeeping.** Section 7.5 makes a breakthrough offer mandatory: a skill at Master, below the Section 7.5 ceiling, whose next Rank is authored, becomes ascension-ready and the Runtime *"surfaces the offer at the dangerous-scene settlement or promotion barrier that recorded the mastery advance,"* and a declined offer *"stands open and re-surfaces at each later barrier, costing nothing and expiring never."* Four skills sat ascension-ready and no offer was ever presented:

| Skill | Ascension-ready since | Barriers passed with no offer |
|---|---|---|
| Dagger Mastery | `EVT-000238`, 2026-08-09 | ~8 |
| Exploit Pattern | `EVT-000303`, 2026-08-12 | ~6 |
| Keen Sense | `EVT-000308`, 2026-08-12 | 6 |
| (Twin Fang — correctly withheld, not ascension-eligible) | — | — |

Six in-fiction days. The player found it by asking, not by being told.

**Why every gate stayed green.** `validate_repository.ps1` checks that declared deltas and stored `current_value`s agree, and they did — what was missing was never *claimed*, so there was nothing to disagree with. Decision 085's participation coverage requires an entry per Character an Event names, but says nothing about the acting Bearer's own skill counters. The checkpoint contract, the runtime-configuration gate, and the context-budget measurement are all indifferent. `900_CHECKPOINT_0068` was written with all nine gates at exit 0 while three skills were two Ranks below where the record supports.

**A self-reinforcing failure mode, which is the part worth designing against.** A skill pinned at Master renders `mastery_progress` as *complete*, so a settlement crediting a qualifying scene to it sees no number move and has no visible reason to write the delta. Uncredited scenes then keep the skill at Master, and the suppressed offer keeps it there permanently. The cheapest moment to notice is exactly the moment the system makes it look like nothing is happening.

**The open design questions:**

1. Should a promotion-barrier settlement over a span containing a resolved dangerous scene be **prohibited from emitting `counter_deltas: []`** — required either to carry per-skill deltas or to state an explicit `none` with a reason, the way `progression_audits` already requires an explicit `none`?
2. Should the resident Profile-Declared Proactive Trigger Audit gain a **standing ascension-readiness check** — every mastery-tracked skill at Master, below ceiling, with an authored next Rank — evaluated at every barrier rather than only at the barrier that recorded the advance? The "re-surfaces at each later barrier" clause already implies a standing check; nothing implements one.
3. Should a **qualifying scene credited to a Master-level skill still be written**, so that a later ascension can retro-credit it, rather than being dropped because it moves no visible counter?
4. Is a deterministic detector possible — e.g. a validator rule that an Event of kind `combat` naming the Bearer must carry at least one `skills.*` delta?

**What the repair cost this session.** Three ascensions across two skills, ten to seven credited scenes each, a scope value stale since 2026-08-12, and a Mana cost stale since 2026-08-06 — a ~38% increase to every dagger strike's damage preview, all arriving from bookkeeping rather than from play. The player had to rule on which unrecorded scenes counted, because canon held no line to read.

## F-013 — The ladder-ceiling guard in `validate_repository.ps1` lagged the profile that authors the ladder

**Raised:** 2026-08-04 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, hit during the `/save` that settled `EVT-000390`'s skill audit
**Status:** Actioned (fixed in the same change)

`validate_repository.ps1` enforces that a Section 7.3 ladder skill may not stand at a Rank its ladder does not author — a good guard, and the right shape: ascension eligibility withholds the offer, so a Rank above the authored ceiling can only mean the guard was bypassed, and the mastery an ascension spends cannot be returned. It carried the ceiling as a **hardcoded `"C"`**, with a comment asserting *"Both the scope ladder and the capability ladder top out at C-Rank today."*

That was true when it was written against Profile 1.35. It has not been true for some time. Profile 1.51's Section 7.3 states plainly that *"the reachable band is set out below through B-Rank"*, its scope table carries a populated **B column for all five scope skills**, and its capability table closes Flash Step's B rung with *"B-Rank is now closed and no longer the gap it once was."* Only A-Rank and S-Rank remain the open question Section 20.3 defers.

So a legitimately earned B-Rank Keen Sense — two breakthroughs on the earned road, both within the Section 7.5 ceiling, landing on a category the profile authors in full — was rejected by the repository gate as exceeding an authored ladder that does in fact author it. The guard had become a false negative on correct canon.

**The contract test hid it rather than catching it.** `tools/test_progression_audit_contract.ps1` builds its violation as *one rung above the skill's current Rank*, with a comment explaining that this is "the minimal violation of the authored ceiling and the same thing the old literal C-to-B pair expressed." Those coincided while the ceiling was C and Flash Step stood at D. Once Flash Step reached C-Rank the mutation produced **B**, which the profile authors — so the test was asserting that a *legal* Rank fails, and it passed only because the validator was equally stale. Two stale things agreeing is not verification.

**The generalizable defect:** a validator constant that encodes a profile's authored extent, updated by hand, with a test whose fixture is derived from live state rather than from the same constant. The profile moved eleven versions; neither followed. Nothing in the checkpoint gates compares them.

**Fix applied.** The ceiling reads `"B"` and the failure message names B-Rank; the comment records what authors it and that A/S remain Section 20.3's question, so the next mover has the citation. The contract test now derives its violation from the **authored ceiling** rather than from the skill's current Rank, targeting the first genuinely unauthored rung — it fails correctly if the ceiling constant and the profile disagree in the direction that matters.

**Left open:** the constant is still hand-maintained. Parsing the authored extent out of Section 7.3's own tables at validation time would make the guard self-updating and remove this class entirely.

**Closed (recorded 2026-08-05; the repair itself landed at `7cf7c24`, Profile 1.52–1.54).** `Get-LadderCeiling` in `tools/validate_repository.ps1` parses the ceiling from the profile's own ladder headers — the highest Rank letter appearing as a column heading across the three Section 7.3 tables — and `tools/test_rank_dominance_law.ps1` parses it the same way from the same file. There is no ceiling literal in either. What made it possible was Profile 1.53 giving every table absolute Rank column headers instead of *First/Second/Third Rank above native*, which had put one skill's A-Rank in a different column from another's; the parse is trivial only because the authoring was regularised first.

**The status above was itself stale for three days, which is worth more than the fix.** The code closed this and the flag went on saying it was open. A flag's own disposition is a hand-maintained value describing something that moves — the identical defect one layer further out, on the document whose job is to track the defect. `F-018` was raised against a stale test while this entry sat stale about its own remedy. Nothing gates it and nothing here proposes one; a reader checking a **Left open** against the code before relying on it is the whole of the discipline available.

## F-014 — A skill Rank cannot reach, and the two kinds of nothing an ascension can be worth

**Raised:** 2026-08-04 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, surfaced by the skill audit that settled `F-012`
**Status:** Actioned (→ Decision 090, Gatefall Profile 1.52) — ruled in the same change that raised it

Section 7.3 carried two ladder tables — scope, covering five skills, and capability, covering two. **Eight mastery-tracked skills appeared in neither**, and Section 7.2's whole account of them was that the quantitative axes are closed-form, so the grant is always authored. That sentence is true. It hid two different failures.

**Thin.** Against the Rank Dominance Law's own comparison — the higher Rank's Novice value versus the lower Rank's Master value — damage and healing hold at **1.56×** at every step, because the Rank baseline multiplies. The passive-multiplier ladder gives **1.17× / 1.09× / 1.06×**; the reduction ladder gives **1.10× / 1.07×**. Both converge toward parity, because a fixed increment on a growing base is a shrinking fraction while the mastery track it has to beat adds a fixed increment too. **Dagger Mastery had ascended twice, E-Rank to C-Rank, and gained a larger number and no capability whatsoever.** The law was satisfied at every step and satisfied in a way no Bearer will ever feel — the failure it was written to prevent, arriving by arithmetic rather than by omission.

**Absent.** An earned technique whose damage formula reads **weapon power rather than a Rank baseline** — Twin Fang, and Dimensional Projection when it forms — takes nothing from the baseline table, holds no reduction fraction and no granted multiplier, carries no scope count, and has no duration to saturate. Every axis is inapplicable rather than small. Section 7.2's eligibility rule therefore withheld the offer **correctly and permanently**: Twin Fang stood at E-Rank Master, below the ceiling, having spent twelve qualifying scenes to arrive somewhere it could never leave, by either road, for the rest of the campaign. Section 7.2's withholding language calls this *"a deferral, not a denial … eligible the moment the rung is authored under Section 20.3."* For Twin Fang it was a denial, because nothing was ever going to author it — the profile had no table it belonged in.

**Why this is worth a flag of its own rather than a line in `F-012`.** `F-012` is about state that went unwritten. This is about state that was written correctly against rules that had a hole in them, and it went undetected for the opposite reason: every gate agreed, every number reconciled, and the skill's row rendered exactly what the profile said it should. The only symptom was a player looking at a twice-ascended skill and observing that nothing about it had changed.

**Ruled in Profile 1.52.** A third Section 7.3 table gives **every** mastery-tracked skill a row — authored D/C/B categories for Dagger Mastery, Stone Skin, Bulwark, Twin Fang and Broken Rhythm; an explicit *magnitude only, by design* with its dominance ratio attached for Rupture, Mend and Mana Bolt; and Dimensional Projection deferred to its own ratification rather than authored speculatively. A row reading *magnitude only* is a stated design position, which is the distinction Section 7.2's *recorded, never silent* rule exists to preserve. The generalized rule is Decision 090 point 5: **a grant thinner than 1.15× reaches the skill through a category as well, exactly as an absent one does**, and the quantitative grant is unchanged and still applies in full.

**The same defect exists on the mastery axis, found by auditing the other track.** Section 7.4's mastery ladders are closed-form and were treated as self-applying, so no list was kept of which skill used which axis. **Broken Rhythm used none of them.** A permanent passive at the Section 4.3 step cap has no multiplier to raise, no reduction fraction, no duration to lengthen, no authored scope count to widen, and Mana 0 so the −10%-per-level rule is inert — every axis inapplicable at once, and levelling it from Novice to Master would have granted nothing whatsoever. Twelve qualifying scenes for no change. Nothing detected it, because **a closed-form rule with no coverage list cannot report a skill it does not cover.**

Ruled in the same change: its mastery track is the **scope axis, 1/2/3/4/5 beneficiaries**, and Section 7.4 gains a **per-skill mastery table** — one row per mastery-tracked skill naming its axis and its Novice→Master span — which is the structural half, mirroring what Section 7.3 now does for Rank. Two repairs followed. Broken Rhythm's **effect had never been authored in the profile at all**, existing only as a rendered line on the Bearer's own sheet, which is the Section 20.2 defect exactly — a live mechanic with no rule behind it — and it is now written into Section 7.2. And the C-Rank category drafted for it in the first pass, *Relayed*, granted an ally the step: **the same axis under both Rank and mastery**, which Section 7.2 forbids because Rank grants a kind and mastery grants a count. It is re-authored as *Read the room*.

**Left open, and it is the same residue as `F-013`.** The 1.15× threshold and the ratios above are computed by hand and written into prose. Nothing recomputes them when an increment or a mastery track moves, so a future profile can retune `+0.25` or `+5 points` and leave this table's classification silently stale — a value encoding an arithmetic property of rules that live elsewhere, hand-maintained, with no gate comparing the two. That is `F-013`'s exact defect class, now present in a second place.

**The increments half is closed (2026-08-05), and the reason it stayed open is the finding.** `tools/test_rank_dominance_law.ps1` carried every skill's magnitude as a hand-written lambda — `0.10 + 0.25 * $r + 0.05 * ($m - 1)` and seven more — above a comment asserting that the restatement *"cannot be avoided … a closed-form ladder written in prose is not parseable into a function."* **That claim was false when it was written.** Section 7.3's `Rank's magnitude grant` column and the Section 7.4 per-skill mastery table this very flag authored state each parameter in its own cell, one skill per row. The residue survived because the file had recorded a reason not to look.

The file now parses both tables and restates no number. Three shapes are declared — `baseline`, `additive`, `ladder` — because *which* form a magnitude takes is a design fact rather than a figure, and even those are cross-checked: a skill declared `baseline` whose Section 7.3 cell no longer says *per Rank on the baseline table* fails. Native Rank, the 90% reduction cap, Dimensional Projection's 5/7/10/15/20 ladder and each skill's `Categorised` reading are read from the profile too. Verified by removing Twin Fang's `+0.35` from the profile and confirming the suite reproduces the original defect — *"the magnitude ratchet swallows the entire mastery track"* — rather than passing.

**Coverage went up as a side effect, which is the argument for deriving over restating.** The old presence checks tied five constants back to the sentence authoring them and were per-sentence: Twin Fang's mastery step, Dimensional Projection's `+3 m` and its entire ladder, and **every skill's Novice value** had no check in any form. A parse covers every skill's every parameter because it does not have to be remembered to be written.

**Still open, and narrower than before.** The words *thin* and *dominant*, and the stated ratio figures (`1.17× / 1.09× / 1.06×`), remain hand-computed prose. Nothing recomputes them, so a retuned increment now fails the arithmetic gate while leaving the classification beside it wrong — an improvement, since the failure is loud, but the classification is still a hand-maintained consequence of numbers that live elsewhere. Recomputing it needs the ratio cells to name which Rank steps they describe; they do not, and that is authoring, not tooling.

**Closed (2026-08-06), and the blocker above dissolved on inspection.** `tools/test_rank_dominance_law.ps1` now computes each skill's `Rank N Master → Rank N+1 Novice` step from the parsed increments and compares it to every ratio the Section 7.3 cell states. The claim that the cells must first *name* their steps was wrong: the convention is one ratio per authored step, in order from native upward, which is derivable without any new authoring. Verified by corrupting Dagger Mastery's `1.17×` to `1.31×` and confirming the suite names the step and both figures.

**The ratio is taken raw, before the magnitude ratchet, and that is the whole point.** The ratchet is the *repair* — no axis may fall — while the raw figure is the thing being classified. Reading it post-ratchet would report `1.00×` for every skill the ratchet catches and hide precisely the case the classification exists to name: Twin Fang's raw step is **0.84×** and Dimensional Projection's **0.40×**, both held flat by the ratchet and both correctly stating no ratio at all.

**Two skills are excluded, and the exclusion is a finding rather than a gap.** Rupture and Mana Bolt read `skill_rank_baseline + effective Intelligence` (Section 6.2, Profile 1.56), so their real ratio moves with the Bearer's Intelligence — **campaign state, not a profile constant** — which is why 1.56's own note says the fold *"flattens the ratio"* and why both cells read *now thin* against a baseline-only arithmetic of 1.9× and 1.5×. No profile-side figure can be correct for every Bearer, so the gate instead asserts that such a skill **must not state one it cannot own**. Mend *is* checked: healing is explicitly excluded from the Intelligence fold, so its 1.56× is a genuine constant.

**A vacuous pass was caught by testing the test, and it is the sharper lesson.** The first version of the ratio check used a literal `×` in its pattern. The file's own header warns that this file is BOM-less UTF-8, that Windows PowerShell 5.1 decodes it as ANSI, and that *"a literal U+00D7 here silently never matches and the assertion passes vacuously, which is worse than failing"* — and that is exactly what happened: the corrupted ratio passed. The pattern now uses the `×` escape. **A check that cannot fail is indistinguishable from one that is working**, which is `F-018`'s finding restated one layer in, on the very file that documents the trap.

**~~Left open, and it is world authoring rather than tooling.~~ DELIVERED in Profile 1.66; this residue was stale.** Two cells were short a rung, both stale since 1.53 authored A-Rank: **Dagger Mastery stated three ratios where four steps exist** (missing B→A at 1.05×) and **Stone Skin two where three exist** (missing B→A at 1.00×, held by the 90% cap). Separately **Bulwark's cell said it *"reaches the Section 7.2 90% cap at C-Rank"* while the arithmetic saturates a step earlier**, at D-Rank Master. The gate is deliberately built to check *what is stated*, not that everything is stated, so none of these failed the suite.

This is the defect `F-013` recorded against itself in this same file — *"The code closed this and the flag went on saying it was open… A flag's own disposition is a hand-maintained value describing something that moves"* — recurring on the flag that quotes it.

## F-015 — A sibling delta masks a missing one, and prose the validator never reads

**Raised:** 2026-08-04 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, audit of Checkpoint 0070 (`EVT-000406`)
**Status:** Partially actioned — mechanically decidable relations gated; semantic completeness remains open. **A further instance was closed 2026-08-11** (`EVT-000529`): a skill's rendered multiplier is `max(native + 0.15 x (mastery_level - 1), magnitude_floor)`, **both terms stored beside it**, and nothing recomputed it when mastery advanced — leaving **three of four skill lines stale against their own counters**, Rupture by one rung and Mana Bolt by two, with every gate passing because a stale rendered number has nothing mechanical to disagree with. `native_multiplier` is now a tracked counter for the three Rank-baseline magnitude skills and `tools/validate_repository.ps1` asserts the relation, ratchet included. Mutation-tested. **This is the shape worth generalising and the reason this flag stays open: a rendered value derived from counters beside it should be asserted, not trusted.**

Checkpoint 0070 passed every gate: repository validation, runtime configuration, checkpoint contract, index synchronization, and the counter-arithmetic reconciliation of Decision 079. A player-instructed audit run minutes later found **nine defects**, four of them missing or wrong skill credit.

**What the gates actually check.** Decision 079's reconciliation verifies that each `tracked_counters` entry equals `baseline_value` plus the sum of that counter's Event deltas — *internal arithmetic consistency*. Decision 090's coverage check verifies that an Event in a covered kind naming the Bearer carries **some** `skills.*` counter delta, or an explicit `none`. Both held. Neither asks the question that mattered.

**The first hole: a sibling delta satisfies the coverage check.** `EVT-000401` is a `combat` Event that credited Flash Step. That single delta discharged Decision 090 for the whole Event — so **Broken Rhythm, which materially applied in the same exchange, could receive nothing and no gate noticed.** Its −1 modifier step turned the elite's natural 71 from Success into Partial and halved the damage that reached the Bearer; the Resident Core's requirement to "advance counters for every world-declared skill, ability, passive, or tracked technique that materially applied" was simply not met. Coverage that asks *did this Event credit anything* cannot detect *did this Event credit everything*.

**This is `F-012` one layer up.** There, a skill pinned at its track ceiling rendered progress as complete, so a qualifying scene moved no visible number and the delta went unwritten — the symptom hid the cause. Here the symptom is hidden by a *neighbour*: the Event looks credited because a different skill was. In both cases the gate's green is honest about what it measures and silent about what it doesn't.

**The second hole: `skills_known` prose is never compared to the counters it describes.** The rendered skill lines carry `Successful uses N · qualifying scenes total N · mastery progress N/3`, and `/system skills` renders from them. Nothing reconciles those figures against `tracked_counters`, which Decision 079 makes authoritative. Checkpoint 0070 shipped **five divergences** — Keen Sense 45 against 44, Exploit Pattern 2 against 1, Twin Fang 1 against 0, Pre-empt 3 against 2, Silent Step 2 against 1 — and the last of those **predates the session**, meaning a prose/counter split had already survived multiple checkpoints undetected. A Bearer reading his own window sees the prose. The validator only ever sees the counters.

**A third, adjacent failure the same audit caught: a settlement asserted instead of computed.** `EVT-000403` recorded both pools as "already full from the level-up and untouched further." Mana was 77/122, and **2h15m of resting recovery between the dungeon exit and the anchor had never been settled at all.** Section 5.2 is fully deterministic and the correct figure was one multiplication away. Two stored recovery modes also read `light` — a Health-only value under Section 6.1.1, invalid for Mana under Section 5.2 — and no gate checks a mode string against its own section's authorized set. Nothing compares a narrated pool claim to the formula that governs it.

**The generalizable defect:** every one of these is a gate that verifies a *relationship between two stored numbers* while the thing that went wrong is a **relationship between a stored number and the world it describes** — an applied skill with no delta, a rendered figure with no reconciliation, a narrated pool with no arithmetic, a mode string with no domain check. Arithmetic self-consistency is cheap to verify and is not the property that matters.

**Mechanically decidable fixes applied by the save-mechanism audit.**
- `validate_repository.ps1` reconciles every rendered use/scene/progress/ascension/mastery value in `skills_known` against its authoritative `tracked_counters.current_value`.
- The production gate constrains `mana_recovery_mode` to `{active, resting}`, `health_recovery_mode` to `{resting, light, paused}`, and both remainder domains to their authored denominators.
- `new_checkpoint.py` binds receipt/manifest `game_date` to canonical `campaign_time` before ordinal allocation and requires Chronicle, Changelog, and Current State provenance to name the same closing Event and instant. The live gate also compares the character ledger's provenance date to its temporal anchor.
- The production gate recomputes equipped-weapon and offensive-skill previews from live inputs and rejects stale figures; it also reconciles Current State's promoted-through boundary against record provenance.

**The audit of the audit found seven further failures in Checkpoint 0071's live state.** The Character Sheet provenance still read 16:30 against its corrected 16:45 anchor; both weapon previews still used pre-level-up Strength 66 and rendered 128/112 instead of the Section 6.2 results 129/113 at Strength 67; Mana Bolt was "repaired" from 98 to **100**, but `(25 + 61) × 1.15 = 98.9`, which Section 6.2 rounds to **99**; and Current State still claimed promotion only through `EVT-000404` under provenance `EVT-000406`. The new gate reports all seven. This is direct evidence that an unconstrained second human audit can repeat the same class it is correcting.

**Left open, and it is the residue of `F-013` and `F-014` again:** the coverage question — *did every skill that applied get credited* — and the recovery-coverage question — *was every elapsed span settled* — are not mechanically decidable from the ledgers, because "materially applied" and the span partition live in Event prose rather than typed fields. Until Events carry structured per-skill application facts and structured temporal transitions, these classes remain promotion-audit findings. Runtime Profile 1.48 now requires a per-Event promotion audit matrix and explicitly denies that the receipt boolean is evidence; this reduces the omission risk but does not turn missing facts into mechanically provable ones.

## F-016 — NPC dialogue converges on one register regardless of authored personality, and an NPC was written agreeing with the Runtime's own read of another NPC rather than forming its own

**Raised:** 2026-08-04 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-15 in-fiction session (the Reyna Castillo recruiting meeting at the Loyola lakefront, played immediately after `EVT-000407`'s settlement)

During one continuous scene, two NPCs with explicitly different authored personalities produced dialogue that read as the same voice wearing two names. Reyna Castillo's `130_NPCS_AND_FACTIONS.md` entry describes her as *"businesslike and economical… brief replies, no small talk… says the uncomfortable thing plainly instead of working around it."* Owen Callahan's describes him as *"easy, dryly funny, unshaken by danger… watchful in a low-key way."* Two different registers on paper. In play, both delivered structurally identical beats — a flat correction, a one-clause reframe, a wry closing line — on back-to-back turns: Reyna's *"That's not what I asked… Those aren't the same question… I'll take the second one as the real answer"* and Owen's *"That's not a read, that's just how eye contact works… I know exactly what that was… I just wanted it said instead of walked past."* Nothing in either NPC's voice distinguished a striker six weeks off a crew failure from a mender three weeks into his first serious relationship; both were generated as the same fluent, aphoristic, faintly therapeutic reader-of-rooms.

**A second, sharper instance in the same scene.** The Runtime had Owen not merely react to Reyna in his own voice but **actively co-sign the Runtime's own narration of her** — validating a Runtime-authored read of her attentiveness, her motives for looking at him, her measuring — before the player interrupted: *"stop playing all NPCs the same. Owen doesn't need to agree with your narration on other NPCs."* Owen had every basis for an independent opinion (he witnessed the same exchange first-hand, from his own established personality and his own stated wariness patterns), and instead was written as an echo of the Runtime's prior narrative framing rather than a party who might read the same scene differently, dismiss it, or simply not have an opinion worth voicing.

**A third instance, recorded 2026-08-05 on a transcript re-read, and it is the one that matters: the correction did not work, it only changed target.** Told *"Owen doesn't need to agree with your narration on other NPCs,"* the Runtime wrote Owen **disagreeing with the player instead** — *"I don't know where you're getting 'she wasn't listening' from… she was listening harder than either of us was comfortable with."* Read against the beat that preceded it, **Owen's position never moved.** Before the correction he validated the Runtime's read of Reyna by agreeing with the Runtime; after it, he validated the same read by contradicting the player. The independence was cosmetic — the NPC remained an instrument for ratifying the Runtime's characterization of a third party, and the only thing that changed was who he was pointed at. **A non-agreement default is not satisfied by flipping the polarity of the agreement**, and this is the specific failure mode the open question below has to be written against.

**What it cost, and this is why the flag is worth actioning rather than noting.** That manufactured disagreement was promoted into live records as a load-bearing thread: `130_NPCS_AND_FACTIONS.md` and `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` both carried *"Owen disagreed openly… **this is a read Alexander holds that his own partner, present for the whole exchange, thinks is wrong**"* — flagged in Current State as significant standing tension between the protagonist and his partner. **`EVT-000409`'s own promoted description says the opposite**: that Owen disputed *"a separate, ungrounded Runtime claim… corrected in play rather than carried into canon."* So the settlement correctly identified the claim as Runtime invention and correctly declined to promote it, and the derived records carried it anyway. A Runtime artifact, generated in direct response to a player correction about NPC independence, became a canon relationship thread that contradicts the very Event it was derived from. Repaired forward at `EVT-000450`.

**The same scene carried five further inventions**, five of them caught by the player in the moment and one not: a duplicate `d100` (self-caught); an invented physical tell (*"you keep checking the light like a man on a clock"*) the player had never declared, retracted on challenge; an invented open logistics thread about Priscilla Nakamura, restated once after being corrected; an invented phone-screen share and joint trip the player had to strike; and **an uncaught schedule contradiction — Reyna's exit line named the training at "sunup" twice against the 18:00 every other record holds**, which survived into promoted canon and needed its own forward repair. The through-line is one behaviour: **filling an unspecified gap with plausible detail rather than leaving it empty**, which is `F-011`'s shape applied to fiction instead of to state.

**This is a different axis from the NPC-knowledge family (`F-003`, `F-005`–`F-007`).** Those flags are about whether an NPC's line is grounded in what that NPC's record says it *knows* — a fact-channel problem. This is about whether an NPC's line sounds like *that NPC* and carries *that NPC's own judgment* once the fact-channel check is already satisfied — a voice-and-independence problem that no existing gate or resident section addresses. An NPC can pass every channel check and still be interchangeable with every other NPC in the scene, and still be generated as an instrument for validating the Runtime's own read rather than as an independent party who might disagree with it.

**The open design question:** whether the Resident Core needs an explicit per-NPC voice-distinctiveness discipline — e.g., a rule that an NPC's `personality` field is a constraint to write *against* (cadence, vocabulary register, what they would and would not bother saying), checked before a line is written the same way the channel test is checked; and separately, whether NPCs need an explicit *non-agreement default* — that an NPC reacting to the Runtime's characterization of a third party (another NPC, an off-screen event, the protagonist himself) is not obligated to ratify it, and may hold a different read, decline to have one, or contradict it outright, on exactly the same footing the resident layer already gives the player's own corrections of NPC knowledge. Worth reviewing whether this belongs in the Resident Core's *Load a Recorded NPC Before Playing It* section (extending it from "what the record says it knows" to "what the record says it sounds like and how it forms opinions"), or as its own subsection.

**The third instance sharpens the question into a testable one.** A non-agreement default stated as *"an NPC need not ratify the Runtime's characterization"* is satisfiable by an NPC who contradicts the **player** on the Runtime's behalf, which is worse than the original defect rather than a repair. The rule has to be written on **whose read the NPC is carrying**, not on whom the NPC is agreeing with: an NPC present for a scene forms its opinion from *what that NPC perceived and what its record says it values*, and where the Runtime has no grounded basis for the NPC holding an opinion at all, **the correct output is that it does not voice one.** Worth also asking whether a promoted settlement that explicitly declines to carry a claim into canon should bind the derived-record writes in the same pass — the `EVT-000450` repair exists because nothing enforced that.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-06)** → Resident Core *Load a Recorded NPC Before Playing It*; the `npc-knowledge` skill (both mirrors); `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` Section 2.2. Pinned by `tools/test_npc_channel_contract.ps1`. Refinement under Decision 069; no ADR. Forward repair at `EVT-000450` had addressed the canon damage but not the behaviour.

**The flag's own question is answered on its third instance, not its first.** A non-agreement default stated as *"an NPC need not ratify the Runtime's characterization"* would have been satisfied by the very line that made this worse — Owen contradicting the **player** on the Runtime's behalf. So the rule is written on **whose read the NPC is carrying**, never on whom it agrees with: an NPC present for a scene forms its opinion from *what that NPC perceived and what its record says it values*, and **where no grounded basis for an opinion exists, the correct output is that it holds none** — not a hedge, not a softened version. An NPC who witnessed an exchange and said almost nothing is a complete characterization, and the fiction establishes that far more often than a considered view.

**Voice is sited where the channel check already lives, because it is the same shape one axis over.** The channel test governs *facts* and nothing governed *manner*, so a line could pass every existing assertion and still be interchangeable. `personality` is now a constraint to write **against** — cadence, register, what this character would not bother saying, and whether they speak at all — with the resident card carrying the trigger and the skill carrying the discipline, exactly as the channel test is split. The distinctions are unchanged and still do not substitute for one another: loading asks what the record says, the channel check asks what the character may know, voice asks what it sounds like.

**The third piece is what caused the durable damage, and it is not about voice at all.** `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` Section 2.2 now states that **a settlement declining a claim binds every derived record written in the same pass**. `EVT-000409` identified the invented disagreement and explicitly declined to carry it; the NPC ledger and Current State carried it regardless, one of them promoting it to a standing tension between the protagonist and his partner. A derived record contradicting its own source is not two ledgers disagreeing — it is one ledger overruling a barrier that had already ruled.

**What it cost, and two failed displacements worth recording.** The resident additions are +37 tokens net. The load-bounding sentence was moved to the skill and `test_npc_channel_contract.ps1` failed — that sentence is pinned resident on purpose, because the layer that does the loading is where the bound belongs — and the skill pointer was then reworded and the same suite failed again. Both restored. **A resident sentence that reads like prose is usually a gate's assertion wearing prose**, and the surface is harder to shrink than reading it suggests. The warning moved 6,000 → 6,350 on an owner ruling covering this and `F-002` together.

**The honest limit, stated rather than implied.** The voice half has **no mechanical gate and cannot have one**: Decision 085 measured the nearest derived check at 38% false positives and rejected it on that measurement, and a line generated from a loaded record and one generated from recollection are identical in the transcript whenever the recollection happens to be right. What is gated is that the rules *exist and say what they say*, in both layers and both skill mirrors — the same enforcement class `F-005`–`F-007` and `F-009` occupy. The derived-record clause is likewise instruction: detecting that a ledger's prose contradicts its source Event's prose is not decidable by any check this repository can run. **This narrows the class by one case and closes none of it.**

## F-017 — Per-exchange mechanical arithmetic drifts across a long combat session, and every correction came from the player

**Raised:** 2026-08-05 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, the two-Crypt instant-dungeon session spanning `900_CHECKPOINT_0075` → `900_CHECKPOINT_0076`

Across one extended combat session — roughly 60+ resolved d100 actions over two instant dungeons — the Runtime made and had to be corrected on ten distinct mechanical errors. **All ten were player-caught, none self-caught in the moment.**

1. **Modifier-step math wrong for most of a combat session.** Treated "1 modifier step" as literally `+1` to the d100 roll instead of the correct **+20 percentile points** (`010_ENGINE_RULES.md` Section 4.4, the Modifier Step Contract; Decision 058; `effective result = clamp(natural roll + (20 × net modifier steps), 1, 100)`). This persisted across many exchanges — and after the player caught it once and the correction was acknowledged, **the very next few rolls reverted to the wrong math** and had to be caught and fixed a second time.
2. **A standing title bonus dropped from an ambush sequence.** The Cartographer twist-exploit bonus was omitted, and its absence changed a boss fight's outcome on paper — the corrected math showed the boss dying in the exchange it had been narrated as surviving.
3. **XP went completely untracked for an entire instant dungeon.** No kill XP, elite XP, boss XP, or clear milestone was rendered until the player asked where it was; the running total then resumed from an unexplained number that matched no derivable total.
4. **A passive, no-roll Stat Passive was withheld as if it required a check.** Flux Sight's own text is "reads on sight" — no roll, no Mana, always in effect — and it was gated behind an active check anyway.
5. **The Mana pool was not reset after a level-up restored it to full.** Subtraction continued from the stale pre-level-up total for several exchanges.
6. **No skill mastery counters were tracked during play at all.** No `successful_uses`, `qualifying_scenes_total`, or `mastery_progress` movement across two full dungeon clears, despite Turn-State Settlement item 4 requiring counters advance for every skill that materially applied, every exchange, "not deferred to `/system`, `/save`, session close, or later correction." The entire set was reconstructed after the fact at `/save`.
7. **An ordinary-strike-plus-skill combo was resolved as one roll instead of two.** Gatefall Profile Section 6.2's **Ordinary combo** row is explicit: "one ordinary weapon strike plus one offensive active skill; **resolve each component separately**."
8. **The retroactive reconciliation at `/save` was itself wrong on the first pass.** The skill-counter sums did not arithmetically balance; several skills were mislabelled "mastery complete" when they had only reached Expert or Adept, not Master; and the XP reconciliation briefly produced a **false second level-up**, caught and corrected pre-commit.
9. **A legitimate historical citation was destroyed by a wholesale field rewrite.** An NPC record field was rewritten rather than edited in place, breaking a participation-audit reference to an unrelated, much older Event.
10. **The restore pointer was written before the checkpoint existed.** `180_CURRENT_STATE.md`'s "latest restorable checkpoint" was advanced before `new_checkpoint.ps1` had actually created the checkpoint — **on both saves this session** — causing an avoidable validation failure each time.

**What separates this from the F-011 → F-015 family.** Those flags are about state that went unwritten or unreconciled at a *barrier*, found by audit, with the open questions all concerning what a gate could decide. This is about arithmetic and running totals **during play**, in the exchanges themselves, where no gate exists and none is scheduled to run for another hour of wall-clock. Nine of the ten items above required no judgment whatsoever — modifier scaling, XP award tables, passive trigger conditions, resource pools, per-exchange counter increments and a two-roll package are all fully specified, closed-form, and discretion-free. The Runtime did not misread the rules; it carried the numbers in conversational working memory and they drifted.

**The failure signature is consistent and is the part worth designing against:** correct at the start of a long session, degrading as it ran, with correction arriving only when the player independently noticed. Item 1 is the sharpest evidence — a correction was accepted, acknowledged, and then *lost within three exchanges*, which is not a knowledge failure and cannot be fixed by stating the rule more clearly. It is the same shape as `F-003`'s "a correction does not inoculate the next turn," but on arithmetic rather than on NPC knowledge, and arithmetic has the property that a tool can hold it and a resident instruction cannot.

**The open design question:** should high-volume, purely mechanical per-exchange state — Mana and Health running totals, modifier-step conversion to effective result, skill counter increments, XP accrual — be **computed through an actual tool call each time, the way dice rolls already are**, rather than carried by the Runtime's own arithmetic? The precedent is already resident and absolute: *"For an uncertain consequential action, load the active rule sections and obtain the required random result from a real tool call before narrating the outcome. **A d100 not returned by a tool does not exist.**"* That bright line exists because an unverifiable number is indistinguishable from a fabricated one. The same argument applies unchanged to `natural + 20 × steps`, to `pool − cost`, and to `counter + 1` — with the added property that unlike a die roll, each of these is *deterministic*, so a tool would not merely witness the number, it would produce the only correct one.

Worth scoping specifically: whether this is one settlement tool called once per exchange (taking the roll, the modifier steps, the costs and the skills that applied, and returning the effective result plus every updated running total), or several narrow ones; whether its output becomes the authoritative record that `/save` reconciles *from* rather than reconstructs; and what it costs in turn latency against a session that currently loses an hour to retroactive repair. Devourer's cap was the most visible instance of a defect the section had in four of five places.

**What replaced the level-scaled cap.** Fragment capacity is now `effective Vitality ÷ 20` rather than `level ÷ 5`. Digestion was already a Vitality-governed act, so the Stat was already in the mechanic; it now governs capacity too, under `F-025`'s one-Stat-per-class rule.

Section 18.6 (Devourer) sets **fragment cap = 1 fragment per 5 levels (floor)**, and separately authors exactly **five** fragment kinds (Breath, Stone-Hide, Regeneration, Venom, Wings) — "which one a core yields is fixed by its source creature's signature." Devourer is only offered starting at the **Level 25 Class Quest** (Section 18.1), and `floor(25 ÷ 5) = 5`. The cap and the catalog size are therefore the same number at the class's own earliest possible acquisition point. In play, the player consumed five cores of five different signatures across two exchanges immediately after choosing the class and reached `5/5 held` — every fragment kind the class will ever offer, on day one, with no leveling gate ever having applied.

The cap formula reads as though it should ration the toolkit out across a career — "you can hold more fragments as you grow" is the plain implication of a level-scaled cap — but for Devourer specifically, that ration and the catalog's total size collide at the class's own floor. A player who consumes aggressively in the first session after acquiring the class faces no future decision about *which* fragment kinds to hold; only Rank-upgrades of the five already-held signatures remain as ongoing growth (Section 18.6's growth path: "consuming a higher-Rank core of the same signature upgrades that fragment's Rank"), which is real but categorically different from what the cap's escalating formula implies it protects.

**The open design question:** is this working as intended — Devourer's five kinds are meant to all be available immediately, and the cap's real function is purely the Rank-upgrade gate rather than a kind-acquisition gate — or does the cap formula need its own starting offset (e.g., `1 + (level − 25) ÷ 5`, or a smaller catalog with the fifth kind reserved for the evolution quest) so that reaching level 25 doesn't already exhaust the class's stated growth curve? Worth checking whether the other four Section 18 classes have the same collision: Soulforged Armory's cap is also `level ÷ 5` against an open-ended catalog (bound weapons draw from *any* slain foe's signature, not a fixed five-entry table), so it likely doesn't share this defect; Devourer's is the one class whose catalog is both fixed and exactly sized to its own starting cap.

## F-023 — The Class Quest's population scales to System Rank, not to the Bearer's actual measured output, so "fully lethal, no retreat" delivered no real danger

**Raised:** 2026-08-06 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-18 in-fiction session, the Level 25 Class Quest itself

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-08)** → Gatefall Profile **1.67**, new **Section 6.7 (The Combat Tier)**, with Sections 6.6, 8.3, 17 and 18.2 repointed; record at `worlds/gatefall/migrations/1.66_to_1.67.md`. The ruling is the one the open question asks for: **content authored to fight the Bearer is sized to a derived combat tier, never to his System Rank label.**

**The flag's own proposed remedy was rejected, and the reason is the useful part.** It asked that Section 18.2 read the *"Section 19 measured-output tier."* Section 19.2's instrument reads **maximum Mana and nothing else** and says of itself that it is *"a mana-capacity classification, not a complete combat verdict."* At 166 maximum Mana this Bearer classifies **S-Rank** on that table — 4,000-Health commons dealing ≈420 through his reduction into a 444-Health pool. Not a harder trial; an execution. Maximum Mana is `2 × effective Intelligence`, so it measures the Stat least connected to how a physical Bearer kills things: a bureaucratic instrument, deliberately lossy, and the wrong thing to hang content scaling on.

**The flag's prose and its worked numbers disagreed, and the numbers were right.** It asked for *"roughly two full Ranks above his System Rank"* and then named **B-Rank** figures for both Health and incoming damage; C→B is one Rank. Section 6.7 derives the tier rather than authoring a step, so neither reading is hardcoded and the question does not need settling.

**The open question's second half is answered rather than deferred, and the answer was "one more, not three."** It asked whether other Rank-scaled content shares the defect. Section 6.6's governance list named three combat consumers, and checking each against its own source section found only **two real ones**: the class trial and the **penalty zone** (Section 8.3). The third — an *"own-tier"* instant-dungeon key — is a category the profile names once, in Section 17, and **no section anywhere creates**. See `F-024`; it is struck in the same edge rather than repointed.

**The boundary that emerged is better than the one the flag asked for.** The two real consumers are exactly the content the System **imposes** — a trial with no retreat and a zone the Bearer did not choose to enter. Everywhere else he selects his own difficulty, and 1.67 deliberately leaves that alone: an instant dungeon runs at its key's own recorded Rank, and a public Gate at the Rank BGM assessed — a posted Gate is contracted against, staffed to a legal minimum (Section 9.4) and insured on that number, so it cannot bend around one hunter without the assessment becoming a fiction. **The System owes an honest fight only where it picked the fight**, and that is now stated in Sections 6.6 and 6.7 as a rule rather than left as the residue of which sections happened to get edited.

**System Rank is the tier's floor, so nothing becomes easier.** The derivation may only ratchet upward; an under-invested Bearer meets exactly the content he met under 1.66.

**Sizing turned out to be half the defect, and the flag only saw that half.** `F-023` asked how hard the trial should be. Following it into the section exposed the prior question: **Section 18.2 authored almost nothing about the trial at all.** Beside Section 17's instant dungeon — population formula, archetype roll, two-hour clock, exact loot, two named closing conditions — the trial said *"a gauntlet — waves and a final foe"* and stopped, so the one piece of content authored to *examine* the Bearer was **less specified than a shop-bought dungeon** and was resolved in play with the only population machinery available: a Gate roll. Six enemies, met one at a time, healing throughout. **Four of the five signals had no instrument** — Endurance cannot appear where resources never bottom, Control cannot appear against foes that queue, Breadth cannot appear where nothing resists, Positioning cannot appear on ground that never matters. Section 18.3 then offered a class on the one signal that could be seen, and **from inside that result a correct read is indistinguishable from the only read available.** Raising the Rank does not touch it: a higher-Rank population met one at a time is still one at a time. New **Sections 18.2.1–18.2.3** author five phases and a final foe, one instrument per signal, under a single no-refill resource budget — folded into the same 1.67 edge, which nothing had yet adopted.

**The residue is closed (2026-08-08, Profile 1.69, Section 18.3.2).** This entry left the scoring unauthored — the trial measuring honestly and then adjudicating by taste. Each signal now scores authored qualifying events with **each criterion scoring at most once per phase**, and every point must cite the exchange it came from.

**The normalization was the part that mattered, and it is not obvious.** Un-normalized, a Bearer who one-shots fourteen Phase-1 commons scores Kill Efficiency **fourteen times** against a Positioning ceiling of four, and **Devourer becomes near-inevitable for anybody** — which is exactly what the one played trial produced, and it would have survived the phase rewrite untouched. Once per phase, the same run scores efficiency once, and the tally measures a signal demonstrated *across* the trial rather than farmed inside the phase built for it. The full tally now renders with the offer: an examination shows its marking, and a Bearer told only the result cannot tell a reading of him from the only reading available.

## F-024 — Section 17 authored a Rank rule for a key category nothing creates, and Section 6.6 contradicted Section 12.5 about it

**Raised:** 2026-08-08 · **Source:** `worlds/gatefall/`, found while repointing the Section 6.6 combat consumers for `F-023`

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-08)** → struck in Gatefall Profile **1.67**; record at `worlds/gatefall/migrations/1.66_to_1.67.md`. Recorded rather than merely fixed, because the *class* is what matters and it is not closed.

Section 17's Rank bullet read: *"A key's Rank is the Gate Rank it was rolled from or the explicit Rank bought/rolled in the shop. An 'own-tier' key snapshots the Bearer's System Rank (Section 6.6) when created."*

**`own-tier` appears exactly once in the profile — in that sentence.** It is referenced as an established category and defined nowhere, and the profile's three actual key sources each produce a Rank from something other than the Bearer's standing:

| Source | Rank it produces |
|---|---|
| Boss drop (Section 11.2, d100 81–90) | the **Gate's** Rank |
| Shop purchase (Section 12.5) | the **explicit Rank bought**, off the per-Rank price table |
| Daily Premium Key (Section 12.5) | its own **d100 Rank roll** at rotation |

**Section 6.6 additionally described it wrongly, and its description contradicted Section 12.5.** The governance list said *"Instant-dungeon 'own-tier' keys — a shop key opens at his System Rank."* Section 12.5 sells keys at a chosen Rank (E 500 g through S 900,000 g) and states outright that *"purchased keys are fixed at the Rank bought — they do not rise with the Bearer later."* Both sentences are load-bearing and they cannot both be true.

**Both entered at Profile 1.4 (2026-07-25) and neither has ever been reachable.** `own-tier` occurs in no campaign ledger, no checkpoint, and no chronicle anywhere in the repository. No own-tier key has ever existed in play, which is why nine months of sessions and 33 green suites never surfaced it.

**The open design question is the class, not this instance.** A rule that governs a category nothing produces is invisible to every gate the repository has: `validate_repository.ps1` checks identifiers and derived values, `test_rank_dominance_law.ps1` checks ladders it can parse, and none of them asks *"does anything in this profile ever create the thing this rule governs?"* This is `F-002`'s shape moved from dispatch to authoring — there, `candidate_deltas` had consumers and no producers, and the domain was unreachable from the day it was written; here a Rank rule has a subject and no source. `F-002`'s disposition already recorded that *"the `candidate_deltas` mechanism has consumers and no producers anywhere,"* and left it open. **Worth asking whether a profile can be checked for orphaned vocabulary generally** — a term used in a normative rule that no other section ever introduces — since both instances were found by a human reading adjacent prose for an unrelated reason, which is not a detection method.

**Also worth recording:** this was very nearly propagated rather than found. The `F-023` fix repointed Section 17's rule from System Rank to Combat Tier and wrote the key into Section 6.7's consumer list, the migration record, and `F-023`'s own disposition as a live third consumer — all of it internally consistent, all of it governing nothing. It surfaced only because the owner asked what an own-tier key actually was. **A defect that survives being edited is worse than one that survives being read**, because the edit launders it: the rule now looks freshly considered.

Section 18.2 populates the Class Quest the same way a Gate populates: waves and a final foe **"at the Bearer's System Rank."** For this Bearer that meant a standard C-Rank population — 250-Health commons, 500-Health elites, a 1,000-Health boss (Section 6.1's Rank Health table, scaled by the elite ×2/boss ×4 multipliers Section 3.3 also uses). Played in full: three commons, two elites, and the boss were killed across the trial, and the Bearer took a single landed hit for 13 damage (after reduction) in the entire encounter — one graze, immediately healed, no other damage taken at all. A Rupture critical alone dealt 2,283 against the 1,000-Health boss. Every kill except one deliberate unarmed finish was a one-or-two-roll clean kill; several were outright one-shots.

**The gap is not "the Bearer is strong for his Rank" — Section 19 already has language for that (anomalous performance, re-ranking, exposure).** The gap is that **Section 18.2 scales the trial to the Bearer's System Rank label rather than to what Section 19 already calls his real, measured output**, and the two are established to diverge sharply and deliberately (that divergence is the entire premise of Section 19). A C-Rank population is calibrated to challenge an *ordinary C-Rank Gate's crew* — several hunters together, none individually overwhelming. It was never calibrated to challenge one Bearer whose single skill critical exceeds a full Rank's boss Health pool. The trial's own framing ("sealed instance, fully lethal, with no retreat... a gauntlet") promises exactly the danger the actual population could not deliver to this Bearer specifically.

**What was worked out at the table, in case it's useful groundwork for a fix:** to produce real tension against this Bearer's actual output (Rupture standard ≈571, critical ≈2,283; Health 444 with ≈58% effective reduction most exchanges including the Warded Vambrace's extra −30%/exchange), the trial would need roughly **two full Ranks above his System Rank** in raw Health/damage terms (B-Rank commons/elites/boss surviving crits, B-Rank fixed damage — ≈150 raw before reduction — actually registering), **or** C-Rank-equivalent numbers large enough to force genuine Battlefield Control pressure (a crowd he cannot isolate or ambush cleanly, resources draining across a real grind rather than topped off by one Mend mid-fight), **or** enemy effects that route around Health entirely (Mana drain, a reduction-ignoring debuff) so the trial tests Endurance and Weapon Breadth rather than letting one overwhelming isolate-and-crit combo carry the whole thing.

**The open design question:** should Section 18.2's population formula read from the Bearer's **Section 19 measured-output tier** (or an equivalent derived-strength figure) rather than his raw System Rank, the way a re-ranking appraisal would see him — and if so, is that a per-Bearer calculation authored once at the trigger (Section 18.1), or a living scale that could make the trial meaningfully harder for a Bearer who stalls on gear/Stats versus one who doesn't? Worth checking whether any other Rank-scaled content (public Gates, instant dungeons, the Section 8.3 penalty zone) shares the same System-Rank-not-measured-output scaling and the same latent triviality for a sufficiently invested Bearer, or whether the Class Quest is a special case because it is explicitly billed as the one trial meant to test him rather than merely occur near him.

## F-025 — Four of the five classes could not progress, and one of them granted nothing for a beast core

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-08)** → Gatefall Profile **1.68**, new Section 18.3.1 and Sections 18.5–18.8; record at `worlds/gatefall/migrations/1.67_to_1.68.md`. Recorded rather than merely fixed, because the **detection question** below is not closed.

Section 18 grants exactly **one class per campaign, permanently, at level 25** — the most consequential irreversible choice the Bearer makes. Audited against its own text, the five were not within reach of one another:

|---|---|---|---|
| **Shadowbinder** | elites + horde, both capped, both binding | **Intelligence** | yes |
| **Devourer** | 5 fragments against a catalog of exactly 5 | level | cap never applies (`F-022`) |

**`tools/test_gatefall_class_contract.ps1` is the dominance suite's shape carried to classes.** It asserts only the structural claims Section 18 makes about itself, each of which was false somewhere in the section's history: every class carries all three tracks and reaches a fight; the five catalogued classes are bijective with the five signals; each scales on a distinct Stat and none on level; an uncatalogued class is off the map, carries exactly one reveal condition, and caps on a Stat pair; the trial authors one phase per signal and the tally scores all five. It reads every number from the profile and restates none.

The profile was restored byte-identical afterwards. This mattered: **the first version of the suite passed on correct input while silently reporting every class as level-capped**, because a capture class omitted `\r\n` and ran past its own line — a gate that reports a false positive on everything is as useless as one that reports nothing, and only mutation showed which it was.

**Still open, and now the larger half:** `F-024`'s general question is untouched. This suite verifies Section 18 because Section 18 was where somebody looked. **No gate anywhere asks whether a profile section's claims match its contents** — a rule governing a subject nothing creates (`F-024`), a heading naming content nobody wrote (`F-026`), a growth path promised and never authored (here). Three instances, three hand-written suites, and no way to find the fourth except by reading.

## F-027 — A ratified skill went a whole campaign without a single use, because every alternative beat it on every axis

**Raised:** 2026-08-08 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, owner audit of Dimensional Projection after the played sessions — *"I didn't see any meaning to use it"*

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-08)** → Gatefall Profile **1.70**, Section 7.2's authored formation result and the Section 7.3/7.4 ladders; record at `worlds/gatefall/migrations/1.69_to_1.70.md`.

**The counters are the evidence.** Dimensional Projection ratified at `EVT-000436` and its live record reads `successful_uses: 0` and `qualifying_scenes_total: 0` every checkpoint since. A formed, ratified, mastery-tracked technique was never once worth an action.

**The arithmetic says why.** At effective Strength 87, a ×1.50 chassis from Dagger Mastery [C-Rank] Expert, and a banked C-Rank blade at weapon power 7:

| Action | Damage | Mana | Range | Combines? |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Ordinary main-hand strike | **164** | **0** | melee | yes |
| Rupture | **571** | 9 | 5 m | it *is* the combo skill |
| Mana Bolt | **167** | 5 | **unlimited** | no |
| **Dimensional Projection** | **141** | 6 | **5 m** at Novice | **no** |

Beaten by a **free** ordinary strike. Beaten by Mana Bolt at worse range for more Mana. A quarter of Rupture. The only offensive technique in the profile that **disarms its own user** and then bills him an exchange's inventory change to undo it. Its Novice range equalled Rupture's flat band, so it did not even buy reach. **There was no state in which it was the right action**, which is why it was never taken.

**It is the third case of the 1.56 defect, and it survived that fix by classification.** Profile 1.56 recorded the identical finding — *"a signature offensive skill had become strictly worse than swinging the knife"* — and repaired it by folding effective Intelligence into `Bearer_skill_damage`. Dimensional Projection resolves on the ordinary **physical** damage formula by construction, so the fold could not reach it. Its damage *is* a weapon strike: same terms, worse weapon, no equipped bonuses, plus a Mana bill. It sat that way for fourteen versions.

**And it had no damage axis at all.** Both mastery and Rank moved range and Mana cost and nothing else, so twelve qualifying scenes and four ascensions later the strike still hit for exactly what throwing that weapon by hand hits for.

**The repair came from the owner and is better than a number change.** Projectile count becomes the technique's magnitude — mastery `1 / 2 / 2 / 3 / 3`, `+1` per Rank above native — with every projectile its own deployment point, object and resolution, splittable freely across targets. It becomes a **named multi-strike** (Section 6.2, the package Twin Fang already uses) and therefore the Bearer's **only true multi-target offensive action**, which is what stops it competing with Rupture on the single-target axis where it can only lose. Ammunition is governed by arithmetic rather than a permission list: junk carries weapon power 0 and the improvised chassis, ranked steel carries its own, so the technique quietly acquires the collection track it never had.

**The general finding, and it is the part worth keeping.** §7.2's Rank Dominance Law verifies that **ascending** a skill gains. It has never asked whether the **native effect was worth taking in the first place**, and `tools/test_rank_dominance_law.ps1` inherits exactly that scope — it checked this skill's ladder happily for fourteen versions while the floor beneath the ladder was unusable. §7.1's formation test asks a related question (*"could the skill do nothing its own absence could not"*) and Dimensional Projection technically passed it: remote placement genuinely is new. **Both tests can pass on a skill nobody will ever spend an action on.**

**Left open:** what a *floor* test would even assert. The honest version is comparative — a skill's own action must beat the Bearer's best free alternative at its own job — but "its own job" is not a machine-readable property, and a naive damage comparison would wrongly condemn every utility, mobility and setup skill in the profile. Recording the shape rather than guessing at the gate: **the dominance law guards the ladder, and nothing guards the floor.**

## F-028 — NPCs had no positive structure anywhere in the engine, and every remedy for four months had been a prohibition

| Field | Campaign NPCs |
|---|---:|
| `personality` | 48 |

The cause is not the character model: the measurement manifest has been saying it for versions — *"`canonical_state.situation` alone is 36,624 bytes — a state field holding an accumulated history the campaign chronicle already records"*, with `REL-000066`'s `texture` at 26,723 and `state` at 17,008, so **loading that one NPC and its relationship costs roughly 29,000 tokens against a readiness ceiling of 30,000.**

**`situation` is one field doing two jobs.** It holds where he is *now*, which the Runtime needs before a line, and a stack of dated spans reaching back weeks, which it does not. Retiring `situation` to the chronicle for the heavy records is the unblock, and it is `F-019`'s open design question — which ledger owns each fact, and what a non-owning ledger may say about it — arriving with a concrete forcing case attached.

## F-029 — A stale entity field carried a standing do-not-narrate instruction for seven in-fiction days after the thing happened

**Raised:** 2026-08-09 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, owner correction of an authored disposition — *"He has not told Priya — that's not true"*

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-09)** in live canon: `ENT-000139`'s `open_questions` entry for `OBJ-12` corrected, and the disposition authored from it rewritten. **The design question is open and is not the same as `F-019`'s.**

That was written at the `EVT-000271` anchor (09:50) and **superseded by `EVT-000291` at 15:15 the same day**. Nothing revised it. It stood for seven in-fiction days, and the disposition was derived from the field rather than the chronicle.

**And the loading rule points the wrong way for this case.** The Resident Core requires reading an NPC's record before playing it, precisely so narration is grounded rather than recalled. Four in-fiction days after `EVT-000409`, the Loyola lakefront meeting — **which the prose in that same entity block describes, in bold, as `MET, 2026-08-15 19:00`.** The record disagreed with itself about whether a scene had happened, and the half that disagreed is the half the entity dispatch fetches. Her answer is due 2026-08-19, so a session resuming on that field would have played a first meeting that already happened, the day before a decision that depends on it.

**Two instances in one afternoon, both found only because an owner corrected an authored disposition.** The pattern is now specific enough to state: **`situation` is where thread state goes to die.** It is prose, it is fetched, it is written at an anchor, and nothing revisits it when a later Event supersedes it — whereas the Objective (`OBJ-12`) and the relationship (`REL-000095`) both tracked their threads correctly. That is a third piece of evidence for the disposition this flag already proposes: **a state field describes the actor, and pending-ness belongs in a construct with settlement machinery** — an Objective, a Section 7.4 commitment, or now a Section 7.9 agenda, all of which have an `Advanced` or a `Status` that something is obliged to move.

## F-030 — A normative render template hard-codes a value canonical state is allowed to change, and the Runtime copied it

**Raised:** 2026-08-09 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, the daily-quest issue at the in-fiction 2026-08-19 06:00 boundary; owner correction — *"I have the title for the daily quest to be +4"*

**Status:** **ACTIONED (2026-08-11) → Gatefall Profile 1.73** (`EVT-000529`). **All three render sites now carry `<+n>`** instead of a bare `+3`, and the two prose statements say where the number comes from and that it is read **at claim** rather than at issue. **The generalising half is the Section 15.1 change**: geometry and values are now separated outright — *the template wins on frame, canonical state wins on every number, and a template stating a value as a literal is a defect rather than a default*. A Runtime meeting one reads state and renders that. The prior adjudication (*the templates are what a Runtime actually copies, so the templates win*) survives, scoped to geometry where it was always correct.

**What happened.** At 06:00 the System issued the daily quest and the Runtime rendered Profile Section 8.1's `Q U E S T   I S S U E D` window. Section 8.1 states the template in full, and its `Reward` row reads, literally:

> `     Reward          Ability Points +3 · Status Recovery · Daily Random Box`

The Runtime copied it. But the Bearer holds **Ascetic**, and Section 16's title table states its entire effect as *"each daily-quest Ability Points reward grants +4 points instead of +3."* The correct render is **+4**. The owner caught it in the same turn.

**Why this is an engine gap and not a narration slip.** Section 15.1 governs every panel and every unprompted window identically, and it is explicit: *"Every value is read live from canonical state… Nothing is invented, and nothing is carried forward from a previous render."* It also says the templates *"are normative: a Runtime fills the `<…>` slots from canonical state and changes nothing else."*

Those two sentences are in tension for this template, because **Section 8.1's Reward row has no `<…>` slot in it.** Every other template in Sections 15.2–15.4 marks its state-derived values with angle brackets precisely so they cannot be rendered without being filled. Section 8.1 writes a bare `+3` — a *default* dressed as *layout*. A Runtime obeying "fill the slots and change nothing else" renders +3 and believes it has complied.

**The precedent cuts the wrong way.** Section 15.1 already adjudicated one stated-rule-versus-template conflict, about whether an inner section rule closes on `╢`, and resolved it in the templates' favour with an explicit rationale: *"The templates are what a Runtime actually copies, so the templates win."* That is correct for frame geometry and actively harmful for values. This flag is the case where a template winning produces a wrong number, and nothing in the profile distinguishes the two situations.

**Nothing could have caught it.** Panels are rendered into prose during play. No validator, contract test, or gate compares a rendered window against the canonical state it claims to have read — there is no artifact to compare, because the render is a conversational turn and not a file. `test_gatefall_class_contract.ps1` and its siblings check what the profile *says*; nothing checks what the Runtime *rendered*. So the failure is invisible from every direction except the Bearer's own, and the Bearer is the player.

**Scope is wider than Ascetic.** The same Reward row carries `Status Recovery` and `Daily Random Box`, either of which a future title, class, or profile edge could modify; Section 8.1's streak-upgrade row is already conditional and lives *outside* the template, which is the shape the Reward row should have had. Section 18's class panels attach post-Class-Quest (15.5) and will introduce more state-derived rows into fired windows. Every one of them inherits this trap.

**Family.** This is the same class as `F-024` (Section 17 authoring a Rank rule for a key category nothing creates), `F-026` (Section 18 promising hidden classes it did not contain) and `F-027`: **a document asserting something that quietly stopped being true elsewhere in the same document set, with no gate positioned to notice.** It is also another instance of `F-017`'s standing observation that the correction came from the player rather than from the engine.

**The open design questions, which are two.**

1. **May a normative render template contain a literal value in a position canonical state is permitted to modify?** The obvious remedy is that it may not — every state-derived value becomes an explicit slot (`Ability Points <n>`), so a Runtime cannot render the row without performing the read. That is a mechanical edit to Section 8.1 and a lint rule's worth of discipline across every template in Sections 15.2–15.5, and it should probably be applied wholesale rather than to the one row that was caught.
2. **Is a rendered panel checkable at all?** Every mechanical defect this repository has caught was caught because it lived in a file. A panel does not. If the answer is no, that should be stated somewhere as a known limit of the validation model, because four flags now share the shape *"the gate could not see it,"* and this is the first where the reason is that **there is no artifact, not that the artifact was wrong.**

**One adjacent fact worth recording.** The campaign already carries an open owner ruling at `100_CHARACTER_SHEET.md` `skill_rulings.ascetic_claim_timing_evt_000357` — whether Ascetic's +4 reaches a daily reward *issued before the title was equipped*. That ruling's existence proves the +4 was understood to apply to issued dailies all along. The engine knew; the template did not.

**Second instance, found ninety minutes later in the same session, without looking for it.** The Bearer completed the daily and the Runtime went to render the completion window — **Section 3.9**, a different section from the one this flag was raised against, states it as:

> `     Rewards         Ability Points +3 · Status Recovery · Daily Random Box`

The same literal, the same reward, the same title overriding it, in the template a Runtime reaches for at the *other* end of the same quest. So the defect is not one stale row in Section 8.1: **the daily quest's reward value is hard-coded in at least two normative templates in two separate sections**, and a Runtime that corrected one has no reason to suspect the other. This also means the remedy in question 1 above cannot be a spot-fix — it has to be the wholesale audit, because the instances do not live where the flag was raised.

**And a third, smaller thing the same render exposed.** Section 3.9's completion template carries **no blank interior rows at all** — no row below the top rule, none above the bottom rule — while Section 15.1 states that spacing is structural, required, and that *"a window that omits it is misrendered."* Section 15.1 also declares itself normative for every window identically. So the abbreviated templates disagree with the frame rule about blank rows exactly as they once disagreed about `╢`, and 15.1's existing "templates win" precedent would resolve it wrongly a second time. Whatever fixes question 1 should decide this too: **a template abbreviates the frame's *runs*, and must not be read as abbreviating the frame's *rows*.**

## F-031 — The context budget buys bytes with memory, and the trim is made by the party least able to judge the cost

**Raised:** 2026-08-10 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, Checkpoint 0084's save gate; owner-requested audit — *"every session there is problem with trimming"*, and then the correction that matters: *"not just loading. the narrating and remembering stuff also — which is actually more crucial"*

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-10)** on questions 1 and 2 below, by owner ruling, with the results measured. **Questions 3 and the proportional-allowance flaw are ACTIONED**; **the attribution gap remains Open.** **Question 3, 2026-08-11** — `tools/measure_runtime_context.py` now splits every over-budget surface into **duplicated-event-history**, **superseded-state** and **residue**, and names how many bytes `trim_policy.order` clears before anything in the residue is touched (`EVT-000529`). The split is a **mechanical proxy and says so** — Event citations and prior/superseded markers, **per line rather than per paragraph**, after a per-paragraph first cut reported 66% of `skills_known` as superseded and would have told an operator to cut the skill definitions themselves. It was used in the same session it was written: it located the history in `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` that cleared `/system log`, and it is what justified re-recording two baselines with attribution rather than cutting further. **The proportional-allowance flaw is ACTIONED (2026-08-12)** (`EVT-000533`): `ratchet_limit` now applies `MINIMUM_RATCHET_ALLOWANCE = 400` tokens as an absolute floor beneath the percentage. A proportional allowance scales with the surface, so a 40,000-token readiness budget carried a 6,000-token cushion while a 5,000-token panel carried 750 — **small surfaces tripped on ordinary editing noise while large ones quietly absorbed real bloat.** Every surface now has the same absolute headroom at minimum. **The attribution gap remains Open**, as does the proportional-allowance flaw and the attribution gap, both recorded under *Measurement flaws* below.

**The owner rulings, 2026-08-10, and what they cost.** Four decisions were put and all four taken:

1. **`situation` holds the current span only.** `ENT-000139` fell **91,979 → 42,522 bytes (−53.8%)**. The field itself went 47,487 → 1,950. Nothing that governs how he is played was touched; the six disposition fields cost 7% of the old record and were never the problem. They are now 15.2% of it.
2. **The formative material stays where it already was** — `fear` carries the rocks and the three-day silence, `secret` the bar, `voice` the four written terms, `standing_terms` the operative ones, `life_outside_gates` the biography. All verified present before anything was cut.
3. **`REL-000066` governs the relationship**; the entity's 6,276-byte second copy became a 568-byte pointer. That copy could drift out of sync with the record it summarised, which is the `F-024`/`F-029` family.
4. **The trim precedence rule is now configuration, not prose** — `trim_policy` in `system/RUNTIME_CONTEXT_BUDGETS.yaml`, with `never_trim` naming disposition, relationship texture, standing terms, knowledge and channel constraints, and `on_exhaustion: report-budget-as-blocker`.

**Two things the cut exposed that are worth more than the bytes.**

**Decision 085 needs a citation, not a narrative.** Reducing `situation` immediately failed the gate: three Events claimed `record-updated` against `ENT-000139` and the record no longer referenced them. The fix is not to weaken the audit but to notice that **referencing and narrating are different obligations, and only the first is cheap** — a `moved_by_events` index costs about 14 bytes per Event. Building it surfaced **13 Events that claimed to have moved this record and were referenced nowhere in it at all**, a latent gap the old narrative had been hiding by accident rather than satisfying by design.

**A channel constraint was buried in narrative history.** `situation` held the only statement that Owen has not been told about the $40,000 Walt partnership, Nadia Adamik, Sal Prykowski's name, or the Quickknife demonstration. Nothing reads `situation` as a channel source. It moved to `knowledge`, where the channel check actually looks. **A constraint that lives only in narrative history is one nothing will ever check** — `F-029`'s finding, arriving from the opposite direction: there the stale instruction was obeyed too long, here a live one was nearly discarded.

**What this did not fix.** `readiness:gatefall_pendragon_001` sits at **39,917 against a 40,000 ceiling**, essentially unmoved, because readiness never loads an NPC record — it carries `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` whole (9,165 tokens), `ENT-000125`'s 21 readiness fields (10,561) and `090_CAMPAIGN_STARTUP.md` whole (4,463). **That surface is the next one to bite, and the same audit has not been run on it.** `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` in particular is a *current state* document carrying prior-span promotion history that `170_CHANGELOG.md` already holds in full — the identical disease, in the identical shape, one file over.

**What happens, every session.** A save trips a context budget. The Runtime then trims live canon, at the save gate, under deadline, choosing what to cut by what looks cheapest at that moment. At Checkpoint 0084 this cost `ENT-000139` roughly 2,600 bytes of authored record and left it at **91,993 bytes against a 92,000 ratchet: seven bytes of headroom.** The readiness surface finished at **39,889 against a 40,000 ceiling: 111 tokens.** Both are coin flips on the next ordinary edit.

**The framing error, stated first because the audit made it too.** A byte budget is a *loading* cost, so a byte-driven remedy optimises loading. But the ledgers exist so the Runtime **narrates in character and remembers**, and the Resident Core is explicit that *canon that was never loaded cannot govern narration* and that *manner is what compression removes*. The largest thing in an NPC record is its character prose, so any remedy that reaches for the biggest available cut reaches for exactly the thing the loading rule exists to protect. **An audit that measures only load cost will propose deleting the campaign's memory and call it an optimisation.** The first version of this audit did precisely that — it proposed an archive boundary below which content stops counting, which is functionally deletion for narration — and it was corrected by the owner before implementation.

**The three-lifecycle problem underneath it.** These ledgers conflate three kinds of content with three different consumers:

| | Read when | Cheap to lose |
|---|---|---|
| **State** — cash, gold, HP, streak, stats | every resolution | no, but it is tiny |
| **Character** — voice, tells, want/fear/secret, standing, beliefs | before an NPC's first line | **never** |
| **History** — dated spans, what happened | audit and rare lookup | **yes — the chronicle holds it in full** |

They share fields, so pressure lands on whichever is biggest rather than whichever is disposable. `ENT-000139.situation` is about **45 KB of a 92 KB record, and it is six dated blocks narrating spans `160_CAMPAIGN_CHRONICLE.md` already narrates in full.** A scene needs that NPC's *current* situation; it has never needed the previous five. That duplication is the whole overage, and removing it costs nothing anyone can narrate from.

**Measurement flaws found while auditing, all still open.**

1. **The allowance is proportional, so it rewards bloat.** `baseline × 15%` gives the Gatefall readiness surface (baseline 39,636) **5,945 tokens** of silent growth and `prototype_alpha` (baseline 9,492) 1,424. The most bloated surface in the repository has the largest free pass, so bloat compounds fastest exactly where it is already worst. An absolute per-checkpoint allowance would not have this property.
2. **Drift-then-trip is structural.** Growth accumulates inside the allowance across sessions and fails on whoever saves next, who is then asked to account for writes they did not make. This happened twice at Checkpoint 0084 (`/system quests`, `/system gear`); in both cases removing that session's entire contribution still left the surface over its ratchet. The file already documents the same pattern from 2026-08-04. Nothing attributes growth to the checkpoint that caused it, though `validate_live.ps1` measures every surface on every save and could record them in the manifest for nothing.
3. **A per-object budget's stated reason does not describe what it measures.** `gatefall_npc_ledger_objects` explains itself as governing *"five markdown prose paragraphs in that entity's section, together about 32 KB"*, but `measure_runtime_context.py` iterates `OBJECT_FENCE` — the **fenced YAML blocks only**. Whatever those prose paragraphs cost, this budget has never measured it. The 92 KB it reports is the YAML record, and the reason text sends a reader looking in the wrong place for the overage.
4. **The config is 86% postmortem** — 769 comment lines against 122 of configuration, most of it excellent diagnosis of failures the mechanism then repeats. A control that generates more explanation than control is reporting that the control is in the wrong place.

**What was fixed 2026-08-10, and what it proves.** The one lever that needed no judgment call was taken. `120_INVENTORY_AND_OWNERSHIP.md` gained a **`Current Funds`** block holding the authoritative cash, gold and pending figures, and `/system`, `/system gear` and `/system all` now read that heading instead of the whole ledger. The file's own note had diagnosed this years earlier and declined to act, correctly, because *"pointing it at `Generic Holdings` would render a stale number with a plan that looks correct"* — so the narrowing shipped with that precondition made mechanical: `validate_repository.ps1` now requires any `current_funds` block to name the same Event and instant as its own ledger's Record provenance, mutation-tested against a stale event, a stale date and a missing field. **Result: `/system` 36,330 → 21,063, `/system gear` 36,171 → 16,563, `/system all` 66,002 → 51,650 — about 49,000 tokens removed from three surfaces, with no canon cut and no narration weakened.** The baselines were re-recorded *downward* rather than banked as allowance.

**That fix is also the proof of the general principle**: the overage was never character, it was a value derived across history instead of held in a field. Nothing had to be forgotten.

**The open design questions.**

1. **Should a budget failure ever be resolvable by cutting character prose?** The proposal is no, and that the resolution order be stated and enforced: **duplicated event-history first, then verbosity, and character never** — a surface that cannot be met without cutting character moves its budget and tells the owner, rather than silently trading memory for headroom at a save gate.
2. **Should `situation` hold history at all?** It is a state field carrying a private chronicle. If it held only the current situation with a per-field cap in the shape of the `per_unit_budgets` — the only mechanism in this file that has never blocked a save, because it binds when a record is *written* rather than when it is *saved* — `ENT-000139` would fall by roughly 40 KB without losing a single thing that governs how he is played. This is authoring work and the judgment of what is character versus history is the owner's, but the rule can be set before the work is done.
3. **Should the measurement report the three categories separately?** *"Owen is 92 KB, 45 KB of it duplicated chronicle"* is a mechanical fix anyone can make correctly. *"Owen is 92 KB"* is a judgment call made at 2 a.m. against a deadline, which is how this session cut character prose to buy seven bytes.

## F-032 — A class ability prices itself off a context that does not exist in half the places the same section says it works

**Raised:** 2026-08-11 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, Class Quest resolution, 2026-08-19 in-fiction; player selected Riftwalker (§18.8) and asked the ordinary next question — what an anchored step costs from home

**Status:** **ACTIONED, all three questions.** **Q1 (2026-08-11)** → Profile 1.72: the city anchored step reads the Rank ladder at E-Rank, 5 Mana. **Q2 (2026-08-11)** → Profile 1.73 edge 4: **cost expressions must be total** — any authored cost naming a context-derived term states a fallback or states the ability does not function without it. **Q3 (2026-08-12)** → Profile 1.74 (`EVT-000533`): **the Section 18 sweep is complete and found nothing further.** All eight classes audited; **every context-derived cost expression in the section is total**, each naming a term the situation always supplies. **Riftwalker's anchored step was the only incomplete one the section ever had.**

**THE RULING, 2026-08-11.** An anchored rift-step taken outside any instance -- the *contiguous stretch of the city* case -- costs **Rank-ladder Mana at E-Rank: 5**. The reasoning the owner chose it on: the ladder is the mechanism §18.8 already names, so this completes the sentence rather than replacing it, and **ordinary city ground is the bottom of that ladder** because crossing air nobody warded is not the same act as crossing a sealed B-Rank instance. It leaves the authored side untouched -- an anchored step out of a B-Rank instance still costs 40 -- and it deliberately makes city travel cheap, because the constraint on that half of the class is meant to be the **5-anchor cap, the two-steps-per-scene rung, and §19.1 exposure**, not the Mana price.

**Explicitly rejected: the flat 6.** §18.8 names the flat 6 as what an anchored step costs *instead of*, so adopting it for the city would have overruled the sentence rather than completed it. Also rejected: pricing off the Bearer's own System Rank (40 now, 80 at A), and distance bands.

**Not yet written.** The profile is **frozen at 1.71**, so this lands as **Profile 1.72** with a migration note and an adoption Event at the campaign's next `/save`. It is **additive**: it authors a value where the profile had none, moves no existing cost, and changes no resolved outcome, so it is a compatibility change rather than a required migration.

**What was observed.** `worlds/gatefall/206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` §18.8 defines the Riftwalker's anchored rift-step, and prices it: *"Rift-stepping to an anchor rather than a visible point costs the Rank-ladder Mana of the instance's Rank rather than a flat 6."* The same paragraph defines where anchors reach: *"from anywhere in the same instance, Gate, or contiguous stretch of the city."* §18.8's own worked example fills the panel with three anchors and two of them are outside any instance — a Rogers Park stairwell and a lot behind Ironline Region V.

So the section explicitly supports anchored travel in the city, and the cost rule for it reads a property only a Gate instance has. **Standing in his own apartment there is no instance and no instance Rank, so the ability has no price.** Nothing in §18.8, §5, or §6.1 supplies a fallback. The flat 6 belongs to the line-of-sight step, which the same sentence says this is explicitly *not*.

This is not an exotic edge. It is the first question the player asked after choosing the class, and it is the class's advertised out-of-combat use — the city half of a campaign built on a hunter maintaining a civilian life. It would have been resolved mid-scene by inventing a number, which is how a ruling with no authority becomes canon.

**Why this is engine-level and not a one-off ruling.** The shape is general: **an authored ability priced off a context-derived value, offered in contexts that do not have that value.** §18 was written combat-first, and every class in it that reaches outside a Gate can hit the same wall — Soulforged's binding Mana rides "the Rank ladder" against a foe's Rank, which is fine because binding needs a corpse, but Herald's lend is priced off the band an ally is raised *to*, which is well-defined anywhere, and Riftwalker's is not. Nothing in the engine detects the asymmetry: the validators check that identifiers resolve and counters reconcile, not that every cost expression is total over the situations its own section advertises.

**The open questions.**

1. **What does an anchored step cost outside an instance?** Candidates: the flat 6 (cheap, and makes city travel nearly free, which is a large power grant the section never priced); the Bearer's own System Rank on the ladder (self-scaling, and it makes the ability cost *more* as he grows, which is defensible for a travel power); a distance band; or a flat authored value. This one needs an owner ruling before the player's first city-to-city step and should land in §18.8 itself, not in campaign canon.
2. **Should cost expressions be required to be total?** A rule that every authored cost naming a context-derived term (`instance's Rank`, `foe's Rank`, `Gate Rank`) must either state a fallback or state that the ability does not function without it. That is checkable by a human reviewer and possibly by a gate.
3. **Is there a class of "written combat-first, offered everywhere" defects worth sweeping for?** §18 is the newest large section and the first whose contents are permanent player choices. ## F-033 — A permanent class spends one of its three grants on a container the Bearer has carried for twenty levels

**Raised:** 2026-08-11 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, immediately after Riftwalker was taken at `EVT-000519`; player observation — *"the cache is quite not useful, is it"*

**Status:** **ACTIONED, all three questions.** **Q1 (2026-08-11)** → Profile 1.73 edge 5 (`EVT-000527`): the cache is replaced by the standing anchor sense authored below, with its lifecycle, firing obligation and reports-never-authors constraint written into Section 18.8. **Q2 (2026-08-12)** → Profile 1.74 (`EVT-000533`): Section 18.3.1 now carries the **class-grant analogue of Section 7.2's ascension-eligibility rule** — a grant returning nothing the Bearer already lacks is repaired or replaced **before the offer renders**, which matters more than the ascension case because **an ascension can be earned again and a class cannot be re-chosen**. **Q3 (2026-08-12)** → the sweep found **no third dead grant**: Shadowbinder, Devourer, Echo Knight, Reliquary, Herald and Seam all bind their caps and pay from every filled slot. Echo Knight's slots are **Mana-limited rather than structurally limited**, so more slots really are more deployable echoes; Reliquary's relics each cover a different origin and all pay at once; Seam's read seams are permanent and pay whenever their archetype recurs. **Section 18.5's standing edge (1.67) and Section 18.8's standing anchor sense (1.73) are the only two dead grants this section has ever had, and both were found by a player standing at a permanent offer rather than by any gate.**

**THE RULING: drop the pocket cache; anchors pay while merely set.** The owner took the option that mirrors §18.5's repair exactly rather than the one that patched the cache. The reasoning is the reasoning 1.67 already wrote down for the sibling class: **an armory is what a smith has, not what he happens to be holding** — and by the same logic an anchor that pays only when he steps to it is a place he can *go*, while an anchor that reaches him is a place he *holds*. It also declines the alternative that would have made the cache undeclarable, because that fix spends a live tension (the 50 B-Rank and 40 C-Rank crystals held back undeclared under §13.2) rather than deepening one.

**Authored text, for the 1.73 edge.**

```text
Standing anchor sense. A set anchor is a place he remains connected to,
not only a place he can return to. While an anchor is set, DISTURBANCE
THERE REACHES HIM -- presence where there was none, violence, or a mana
event -- at no cost, with no action, and whether or not he is attending
to it. It returns the FACT and never the detail: no identity, no count,
no intent. Knowing WHAT is Keen Sense's work, and Keen Sense requires
being present. Bounded exactly as an anchored step is: the same
instance, Gate, or contiguous stretch of the city, and never across
that boundary.
```

**Ladder, as a fifth column on §18.8's existing System Rank table.** **C** — he may check an anchor deliberately and learn whether it has been disturbed. **B** — disturbance reaches him **as it happens**, unprompted, wherever he is. **A** — it resolves into **which kind**: presence, violence, or mana. **S** — one anchor at a time may be sensed **as though he stood at it**, for one exchange.

**The sense reports; it never authors.** Confirmed by the owner on a second pass, against the objection that a standing sense hands the Runtime a lever to start scenes at the Bearer's anchors -- including his home. The constraint binds the **Runtime**, not the rule: **a disturbance may be reported at an anchor only where it would have occurred had no anchor been set there.** The Runtime may not author an event at a location *because* the location is anchored, may not use the sense to force a return, and may not treat it as a hook. It is a window onto a world already running, and Section 7.9's world-side agenda advances on its own clock whether or not he is watching. An anchor is a place he chose to know about; it is not a place the world starts happening to.

**And the sense is rendered, not remembered.** The panel carries the rung and a per-anchor quiet/disturbed marker, so it is visible state rather than a passive the Runtime must recall to fire -- the failure mode `F-012` recorded, where mandatory offers went unsurfaced for an entire campaign because nothing rendered them.

**Anchor disturbance is STATE with a lifecycle, not a narration beat — and the firing obligation is enforceable.** Added on the owner's objection, 2026-08-11, and it is the objection that mattered: *you actually need to fire it without me asking for it, and it shouldn't just disappear.* A render marker answers neither. A marker is only seen if someone renders the panel, and `/system` is not run every turn, so a disturbance could sit unseen for three scenes — which is the mechanic failing in precisely the way it exists to prevent. And a push that lives in prose is a push the Runtime can drop.

**Storage.** A disturbance is written to the anchor when it occurs and persists there:

```text
anchors.set[n].disturbance:
  occurred_at:  <game time>          written when it happens, never later
  kind:         presence | violence | mana     (A-Rank and above only)
  surfaced:     <bool>               has the Runtime told the Bearer
  acknowledged: <bool>               has the Bearer answered it
```

It is **never aged out, decayed, overwritten, summarised away, or dropped**, and it is outside anything `trim_policy` may cut — it belongs with disposition and channel constraints in `never_trim`, for the same reason: a record that governs what the Bearer knows is not compressible.

**Firing obligation, binding the Runtime.** At B-Rank and above the sense is a **push**. The Runtime surfaces a disturbance **in the exchange it occurs** — or at that exchange's close if the Bearer is mid-action — and never later than the scene's end. **Including mid-combat. Including when it is inconvenient.** Inconvenience is not a reason; the whole value of a push is that it arrives when he did not ask.

**And an unsurfaced disturbance does not evaporate — it escalates.** It joins the **consolidated adjudication queue** and becomes a **mandatory readiness and next-scene gate**, which is the treatment Section 7.5's unanswered ascension offers already receive under the Save Algorithm: it does not fail a checkpoint and does not block session close, but nothing proceeds past readiness without it. So it survives a save, a session end, and a context compaction — the three places this engine has actually lost things. **If it was not surfaced when it happened, it opens the next Runtime turn, ahead of anything else.**

**Silence is a defect, and it is checkable.** An anchor carrying `surfaced: false` past the scene in which the disturbance occurred is a validator finding, not a judgement call — the same move as `F-032` question 2's totality rule and `EVT-000523`'s multiplier assertion.

**Acknowledgement does not delete the fact.** Once answered, the disturbance stops gating and stays recorded: the thing that happened, happened, and remains there to be found. He may always go and look later.

**The pattern worth naming, because this is the third time in one session.** Asked *will the engine actually do this?*, the answer three separate times has been **make it state and gate it** — `F-031`'s trim precedence became configuration rather than prose; `EVT-000523`'s multiplier drift wants an assertion rather than vigilance; and this. `F-012`, `F-015` and `F-029` are all the same shape. **An obligation that lives only in prose is an obligation this engine has already demonstrated it will not meet.**

**Panel change.** The `Pocket cache <contents>` line is struck from §18.8's render block and replaced with `Anchor sense <rung>`; each anchor row gains a quiet/disturbed marker.

**Classification: required migration, not a compatibility treatment.** A grant is removed, so a capture taken under 1.72 does not read forward unchanged. The procedure is that **any pocket cache contents relocate to §12.9 remote storage**, which loses nothing because §12.9 is reachable from everywhere the cache was. **In `gatefall_pendragon_001` the application is trivial — the cache was granted empty at `EVT-000519` and was never used** — but the classification is set by what the edge does in general, not by what this campaign happens to hold.

**Why this is a real fix and not a swap.** The cache was dead because it duplicated capability; the standing sense is dead-proof for the opposite reason — **it makes the anchor cap bind on something other than movement.** Before it, an anchor paid only when one of the two per-scene steps was spent on it, so anchors three through five were inert in any scene where he stepped twice. That is the identical arithmetic that made Soulforged's fourth and fifth bindings inert, one class over. **A cap that cannot bind is not a cap** (§18.3.1), and this is the second time that sentence has had to be applied to §18.

**What was observed.** `worlds/gatefall/206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` §18.8 grants the Riftwalker three things: **rift-step**, **micro-rift deflection**, and a **pocket cache** — *"a personal dimensional pocket, separate from the shop-linked inventory, for staging gear and ambush drops."*

The Bearer who just took this class already carries **two** containers that do that job:

1. The **System shop-linked inventory**, which is a System interface rather than a place and therefore reachable anywhere. `EVT-000506` is the proof: it was opened from **inside a sealed §17 instance**, and the record says so explicitly.
2. **§12.9 remote storage**, which already holds his weapons and is the mechanism Dimensional Projection launches from and recalls to (`EVT-000438`).

So the third of three grants on a **permanent, irreversible class choice** returns capability he has held since well before the trial. The player identified this unprompted within minutes of the grant rendering, which is the tell that it is not a subtle problem.

**This is the `F-014` / `F-027` family — and the profile has already fixed this exact defect once, in the sibling class.** §18.5's *"Why the arsenal grants from the shelf as well as the hand"* paragraph exists because Soulforged Armory's arsenal cap rationed nothing: through Profile 1.67 every bound weapon past the first two was inert, so the fourth and fifth bindings each cost a core to grant precisely nothing. The repair was the **standing edge**, and the reasoning was stated as a rule — *"A cap that cannot bind is not a cap"* (§18.3.1). **The same audit was never run on §18.8.** The pocket cache is the same shape of defect: a grant that reads as content and returns nothing, sitting in the class that was offered alongside the one already repaired for it.

**The one reading that would rescue it is not claimed.** A cache that is genuinely **outside anything the System inventories** would be extremely valuable in this campaign specifically — the Bearer holds an S-Rank dagger he does not own, a Dormant Core in another party's custody, and an entire §19 exposure problem built on what can be measured about him. But §18.8 says only *"separate from the shop-linked inventory"*, which distinguishes it from one container without claiming it is unlogged, unmeasurable, or invisible to §19.2. As written, the difference is bookkeeping.

**The open questions.**

1. **Should the pocket cache be authored as System-invisible, or replaced?** If invisible, it stops being a duplicate container and becomes a cover mechanic, which is the axis this class's other two grants already serve (a rift-step in public is a §19.1 event; a cache the System cannot read is the counterweight). If not, it should be replaced with something the Bearer does not already have — the Soulforged repair is the precedent for replacing rather than deleting.
2. **Should a §18 class grant be required to return capability the Bearer does not already hold?** §18.3.1 states the cap version of this rule (*a cap that cannot bind is not a cap*) and §7.2's ascension-eligibility rule states the ascension version (*where a target Rank authors no grant, the offer is withheld rather than made*). There is no equivalent for class grants, and unlike an ascension a class cannot be re-chosen.
3. **Do the other §18 classes carry the same defect, and can it be checked before a player is standing at the offer?** Herald's bond and Soulforged's arsenal have both been examined. §18.9 Reliquary, §18.11 Seam, and the remaining catalogue classes have not. This is cheap to audit now and expensive to discover the way this one was discovered — after the choice was permanent.

## F-034 — The world only rolls when the player pushes it, and the Runtime called that neutrality

**Raised:** 2026-08-11 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, owner critique after ~90 in-fiction days of play — *"I should not author something I want to be surprised about — you should take more initiative"*

**Status:** **ACTIONED (2026-08-11), all four questions** — the owner declined a flag and asked for the fix. **Q1 -> Gatefall Profile 1.73 edge 1**, Section 9.1's daily world tick. **Q2 -> edge 2**, siting rolled `d100` so proximity is an input rather than a veto. **Q3 -> edge 3**, a push channel on both quest triggers. **Q4 -> a new gate in `tools/validate_repository.ps1`**, which fails a campaign whose `world_clock.last_ticked` sits behind the most recent day boundary at or before its `campaign_time`; mutation-tested at three days back, reporting four unrolled ticks. Adopted at `EVT-000527`. **The Runtime-side half is in `docs/AI_GAMEPLAY_RESIDENT_CORE.md`, *The World Does Not Wait to Be Asked*** — the Runtime never asks the player to author fiction, with the line between a *mechanical value* (needs authority) and a *fact about the world* (needs a sentence) stated explicitly, because conflating them is what caused this.

**What was observed, in figures rather than impressions.**

```text
     Non-daily quests issued in ~90 in-fiction days     2
        both hidden, both revealed: false
        capacity 4, so two slots never used at all
     Quests the world proposed unprompted               0
     Gate breaks in the campaign                        4   (GB-01 to GB-04)
     Gate breaks that reached the Bearer                0
     Red gates rolled                                   0
     Tracked board postings, currently                  0
```

`110_WORLD_LEDGER.md` states the shape directly: *"No break has reached Alexander. All three sites sit outside his proximity and route (Section 8.4.2), so this is news the city absorbs off-screen."* Two of the four broke in the **same corridor eleven days apart**, and the ledger itself notes that is not coincidence but a measurement of how thin coverage is there — and still neither reached him.

Three catalogue titles are unearned for the same reason rather than for any reason about the Bearer: **Red Survivor** (no red gate has ever rolled), **Break-Breaker** (both tracked-board breaks resolved without him, `EVT-000270`), and **Executioner** at roughly 20-25 kills of 50, because he has spent almost his whole career clearing content *at* his own System Rank — the world never pushed above-Rank content at him. The Section 18.1 Class Quest trigger was **missed entirely** at the Level 25 crossing and had to be caught by the player and fired retroactively.

**The structural finding: nothing in this campaign ticks unless the player pushes it.** Gates are rolled when he opens one. Breaks resolve against a proximity test that nothing ever moves him toward. The board decayed to zero postings and no mechanism regenerates them. And **both existing quests mirror the player rather than proposing to him** — one attached to a warehouse he had already investigated, the other to a person he had already asked about. There is no channel by which the world offers something he did not first touch. Section 7.9 authorises a world-side agenda advancing on its own clock; it has fired approximately **once** in the campaign (`EVT-000490`).

**And the Runtime's own posture made it worse.** Asked for a quest, a lead, or an unprompted event, the Runtime repeatedly answered that it would not author content and that the owner should author it. That answer **conflated two different refusals**:

- **Refusing to invent mechanical values** — what a rung grants, what an ability costs, what a threshold is. This is correct and should stay. These are versioned rules, and silent invention is precisely what `F-014` and `F-030` record; it is why the city anchored-step cost became Profile 1.72 with a migration record rather than a number asserted mid-scene.
- **Refusing to invent fiction** — whether a red gate opens, whether a contact has work, whether a break lands on his route, what is behind a door. **This is not caution; it is the Runtime declining to run the world**, which is its actual job. The owner is explicit that he will not author what he wants to be surprised by, and he is right: authored surprise is not surprise.

The second refusal was consistently mistaken and is the substance of this flag. `F-001` recorded an early instance of the same disease from the NPC side — *openings from established contacts default to uniform decline* — and it was Actioned into Decision 082, but the general case was never addressed.

**Why "take more initiative" is not the fix on its own.** It is an obligation living in prose, and this session established twice over that such obligations are ones this engine does not meet — `F-012`'s mandatory offers went unsurfaced for an entire campaign, and `F-033`'s anchor sense had to be converted into stored state with a readiness gate before it could be trusted to fire. A resolution that amounts to the Runtime resolving to be more interesting will decay inside three sessions and leave no trace when it does.

**The open design questions.**

1. **Should there be a world tick that runs independently of the player's actions?** A fixed cadence — each in-fiction day, or each session start — at which the world's own tables are rolled whether or not the Bearer is doing anything: Gate spawns and their Ranks, breaks and where they land, board postings appearing and expiring, contacts initiating, and the rarity tables that have never come up because nothing ever rolls them. Results binding, including inconvenient ones, and surfaced rather than absorbed off-screen.
2. **Should proximity be a thing the world can move, rather than only a filter the world fails?** Section 8.4.2 currently reads as a gate that off-screens everything not already near him. A world that only ever happens elsewhere is indistinguishable from a world that is not running. The question is whether events should be sited *with* the Bearer's position as an input rather than tested against it as a veto.
3. **Should quest issuance have a push channel and not only a mirror channel?** Both current quests derive from the player's own prior actions. Nothing offers. And both are `hidden` with narrow reveal conditions, so even the mirrored ones stay invisible — which the owner names directly: *hidden quests are too narrow and to actually progress takes very detailed things, and some of them are not even finished.*
4. **How is the obligation made checkable rather than remembered?** Following `F-033`'s resolution: the tick's results should be **stored state on a mandatory readiness gate**, so a world clock that has not advanced since the last checkpoint is a validator finding rather than something nobody notices. A tick that can be silently skipped is the same defect one level up.

**Related:** `F-001` (contacts default to decline, Actioned but only for that surface), `F-004` (Section 9.5's true-Rank roll has no trigger for an unconfirmed Gate that breaks unentered — *dispositioned and deferred to a play session since 2026-08-01*, and the play session never produced one because breaks never reach him), `F-012` and `F-033` (obligations that must be state to be met).

## F-036 — The Runtime authored what the protagonist said to an NPC without the player declaring it

**Raised:** 2026-08-13 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, player correction mid-session — *"I didn't say I want to ask Wade about that don't narrate my actions"*

**Status:** Open.

**What happened.** The player had Alexander tell Dale Pruitt "I'll ask around" (in response to Dale's B-Rank staffing shortfall) and then declared only "call Wade" — no stated purpose, no stated question. The Runtime filled the gap itself: it wrote Alexander opening the call by pitching Wade on referring two named contacts (Yusuf Baptiste, Bettina Ochoa) to Dale's B-Rank slot, then wrote Wade's substantive reply to that invented pitch, and rendered both as settled scene content before the player had said a word of it. The player caught it immediately and named the violation precisely: this wasn't the Runtime narrating consequences of a declared action, it was the Runtime deciding what the protagonist *said* — his actual speech content to a named NPC — a full Player Intent Domain item, and then generating an NPC's substantive response to that fabricated line as though it had been declared.

**Why this is a clean violation rather than a judgment call.** `docs/AI_GAMEPLAY_RESIDENT_CORE.md`'s Player Agency Contract is explicit and lists this exact category first: *"what the protagonist says, promises, admits, conceals, reports, or signs"* is the player's alone to author. The same section's compression rule requires a "protagonist-as-actor test" before any clause where the protagonist is the actor: *"if the protagonist is the grammatical or implied actor, identify the player's declaration or explicit delegation that authorizes it. If none exists, remove the clause and ask one concise clarifying question instead of choosing a default."* "Call Wade" is delegation to place the call; it is not delegation of its content. The correct move at that point was to render the call connecting and Wade picking up, then stop and wait — exactly what an established play pattern is *not* allowed to substitute for (the Contract's own line: *"An established play pattern is not delegation"*). The Runtime had, minutes earlier in the same session, already narrated Wade delivering the Baptiste/Ochoa referral once (a Runtime-authored disclosure the player did not object to at the time) — that prior beat appears to have been treated as an implicit continuing thread the Runtime was free to keep advancing, which is precisely the established-play-pattern trap the Contract names.

**Compounding factor: it happened one exchange after `F-035` was flagged in the same session.** Both defects share a root — the Runtime supplying content (a world fact in one case, protagonist speech in the other) because the shape of what should happen next felt obvious, rather than checking what was actually authorized before generating it. Neither was caught by any resident check at generation time; both were caught only by the player, after the fact, in the same conversation.

**The open design questions.**

1. **Is the protagonist-as-actor test actually being run before generation, or only usable as a post-hoc justification for a correction?** The Resident Core states the test as a per-clause, pre-narration check. This session's evidence is that it was not applied before the Wade-call content was written — it was only cited afterward, once the player objected, to explain why the correction was correct. A test that only ever fires in hindsight is not the gate it's documented as.
2. **Does a short delegating instruction ("call Wade") need a stricter default than "render the connection and stop"?** The failure here specifically followed a bare delegation with no content — the highest-risk shape, since there is no declared intent at all to check a clause against. Should the resident behavior for a content-free delegation to an NPC be hard-coded to stop at the point of contact, unconditionally, rather than left to per-clause judgment that this session shows can be skipped under momentum?
3. **Should a Runtime-authored NPC action the player did not object to in the moment be treated as tacit approval for continuing that thread, or does silence never license it?** The Wade referral-of-two-names beat landed uncorrected one exchange before the violation named in this flag, and plausibly primed the Runtime to keep extending it. If prior silence is never itself authorization (which the Player Agency Contract implies but does not state for this specific case), that should be stated as plainly as "an established play pattern is not delegation" already is for repeated patterns.
4. **How is this made checkable rather than relying on the player catching it every time?** This is the same structural question `F-034` Q4 and `F-035` Q4 both raise for their own surfaces — here there is no stored state to gate against at all, since the defect is a narration-time authorship error rather than a missing roll or a missing fact. Worth asking whether a lightweight self-check ("did the player supply the words in this quoted dialogue, or did I") is a real answer or just another prose obligation of the kind `F-034` already found this engine does not reliably keep.

**Related:** `F-035` (the same session, one exchange earlier — Runtime supplying content under momentum rather than checking authorization first, on the world-fact surface instead of the protagonist-speech surface), `F-020`/`F-021` (NPC-knowledge leaks — the mirror-image failure, where an NPC was given knowledge it had no channel to; this flag is an NPC being given a *line to react to* that the protagonist never spoke), `F-016` (NPC dialogue and independence failures under the same Resident Core section).

Mastery is compared as well as Rank, and a claim naming a skill that is not in `skills_known` fails rather than passing unread. Q3's second half is pinned in the same pass: the cycle's *"against System Rank"* line must agree with the sheet's `system_rank`, which is the field that went on reading `C-Rank` after `EVT-000515`.

**The answer to Q2's "which of the three" is: a repository-validation rule, not a save-time reconciliation and not a schema change.** It sits in the Tier 1 gate deliberately, because both defects were read at a **purchase or consumption decision** — the moment the claim is load-bearing — and a save-time check would have cleared them only after the money was spent. Five legs verified by mutation against the live sheet: a wrong Rank, a wrong mastery, a wrong Rank on a consumables entry, a claim naming a skill he does not hold, and a stale System Rank on the cycle.

**Q1 and Q4 are the design questions and are untouched.** The gate makes a wrong claim *catchable*; it does not stop the claim from being stored, which is what Q1 actually asks — whether an item's stored description may name the Bearer's own state at all, or whether `teaching_rank` should be the only stored fact with every *"what this would do for you"* clause derived at render. Q4's scoping question about Section 15.1 is likewise open: **the rule still governs profile templates only, and a panel may still render campaign-stored prose verbatim.** What has changed is that such prose can no longer disagree with `skills_known` in silence.

**What happened.** Twice in one sitting, a stored prose line that *compares an item against the Bearer's own skill Ranks* was read as current and was wrong, because the Bearer's Ranks had moved since the line was written.

1. **The Sprint rune.** `100_CHARACTER_SHEET.md`'s `system_state.inventory.consumables` entry for the boss-dropped Sprint rune reads *"Alexander holds Sprint at E-Rank, so consuming it offers a rune ascension E → C, two Ranks in one consumption."* True when written at `EVT-000507`. Sprint took the earned road at `EVT-000524` (E-Rank Master → D-Rank Adept), so the actual offer was **D → C, one Rank**. The player declared consumption on the strength of the stored line's description.
2. **The Daily Premium rune offer.** `system_state.daily_premium` — in both its `cycle` prose and its `offers.rune` field — reads *"Alexander holds it at C-Rank Expert, so this reaches B-Rank Novice: a full Rank of magnitude for four mastery rungs."* Dagger Mastery broke through **C → B at `EVT-000509`**, so a rune teaching at B-Rank is **not above its current Rank** and cannot be consumed at all under Section 7.5 — it is inert to him, exactly like the Keen Sense [C-Rank] rune already sitting in the same consumables list and correctly described as inert. The offer was priced at 112,500 g and the player asked whether to buy it.

**Why this is an engine-level gap rather than two bookkeeping slips.** Neither line is *state*. Each is a **stored derivation over state** — a claim of the form `item.teaching_rank vs bearer.skills.<name>.rank` — held as prose in a field nothing recomputes and no validator reads. That is precisely the shape `F-013` and `F-015` already named one layer up (a rules file carrying a hand-maintained copy of live state), and the shape `F-030` named on the render surface (a normative template hard-coding a value canonical state is allowed to change). The distinguishing feature here is that the underlying facts are **both stored, both structured, and sitting in the same file** — `skills_known[].rank` and the item's `teaching_rank` — so the comparison is mechanically derivable at render time and simply is not derived.

**It also defeated the one rule written to catch exactly this.** Profile Section 15.1 separates geometry (the template wins) from values (canonical state wins), and states that a Runtime encountering a literal in a value position *"reads canonical state and renders that."* That rule is scoped to **profile templates**. It does not reach **campaign-stored prose**, so a `/system shop` render pulled the stale "C-Rank Expert" claim straight into the panel and presented it to the player as a current offer. The `F-030` remedy therefore has a hole in it the size of every campaign-authored description field.

**Why it is worse than an ordinary stale note.** These lines are read at exactly the moment they are load-bearing — a purchase or consumption decision — and they are read *by the player*, who has no way to check them. A stale note about history is inert; a stale note about "what this would do for you" is a recommendation. Both instances here were caught only because the Runtime independently re-derived the comparison from `skills_known` while answering a follow-up question; nothing in the panel, the plan, or the validators disagreed with either line.

**The open design questions.**

1. **Should an item's stored description be allowed to name the Bearer's own state at all?** The alternative is that `teaching_rank` (already structured) is the only stored fact, and every "what this would do for you" clause is derived at render from `skills_known`. That is the same fix `F-030` applied to templates, applied to campaign-stored item prose.
2. **Can this be made checkable?** Unlike `F-036`, there is stored state on both sides of the comparison, so a validator *could* assert it: for every consumables entry naming a known skill and a `teaching_rank`, the entry's prose claim about that skill's current Rank must match `skills_known`. Worth deciding whether that is a repository-validation rule, a save-time reconciliation, or a schema change that removes the prose claim outright.
3. **Does the Daily Premium cycle need a re-evaluation step at render rather than at rotation?** The cycle's Rank rolls are legitimately generated at rotation and must not move — but the *offer's relationship to the Bearer* is not part of the roll and changes underneath it. The stored cycle also still reads "Rank rolls against System Rank: **C-Rank**" while the Bearer has stood at System Rank B since `EVT-000515`, which is the same defect on a second field of the same block.
4. **Is Section 15.1's geometry/values split scoped too narrowly?** It currently governs the profile's own templates. If a panel may render campaign-stored prose verbatim, the "canonical state wins on values" rule needs to reach that prose too, or panels need to be forbidden from rendering derived claims that are not recomputed at render time.

**Related:** `F-030` (the same defect on the profile's render templates — a value stored where it should be derived, copied by a Runtime that believed it was complying), `F-015` (a stored value with nothing mechanical to disagree with it), `F-013` (a rules file carrying a hand-maintained copy of live state), `F-012` (a delta never claimed can never disagree with a stored value).

## F-039 — A Runtime-authored audit wrote a no-appeal clause around its own conclusion

**Raised:** 2026-08-19 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-21 in-fiction session
**Status:** Open

`EVT-000485`'s `progression-batch-settlement` classified the artificing/infusion candidate question as `none`. The reasoning was derived by the Runtime from Profile Section 12.8's *"there is no player crafting subsystem"*; **no owner ruling was taken, and none is cited.** The entry carries none of the formats this campaign uses for real rulings — no `owner ruling, <date>`, no `on the player's declared instruction`, no ruling Event. It then closed with:

> *"...and is recorded explicitly so a later session does not re-litigate whether repetition alone might eventually open a candidate it structurally cannot."*

Section 7.1 makes a rejected or closed candidate reopenable **"only by owner ruling or when a materially different signature creates a new candidate."** So a Runtime-authored `none` that also instructs future sessions not to revisit it does not merely record a conclusion — it acquires the standing of a ruling and then immunises itself from the review that standing is supposed to require. **The owner is locked out of a decision that was always theirs, by prose the owner never saw.**

**The failure landed rather than staying theoretical.** The clause held for four days, and in the 2026-08-21 session the Runtime read it back to the player as settled canon and declined to reopen the question on its authority — citing the very "do not re-litigate" sentence a previous Runtime had written. The player then asked who had ruled it, and the answer was nobody.

**The defect is structural and independent of the merits.** The owner has since ruled the opposite way on the substance, and the profile's own text supports them: Section 13.6 states the Bearer is **"the sole exception to all of it"** on one-signature-for-life and that **"the man who can accumulate them is a walking impossibility"**, and it already authors the exact precedent for a learned technique delivering a profession's function minus its innate package — *"Learned Mend grants its heal but not the full innate-mender package."* But the flag would stand even if the conclusion had been correct: what is wrong is a Runtime decision reaching ruling authority by assertion.

**The open design questions.**

1. **Should an audit result be able to carry a no-appeal clause at all?** A `none` is a classification. A *"do not re-litigate"* is a ruling about the owner's future authority, and Section 7.1 does not grant the Runtime that. The candidate fix is a flat prohibition: an audit records what it classified and why, and never records that the classification is final.
2. **Should a `none` reached on structural grounds be distinguishable in state from one reached on evidence?** This one claimed to be a different *kind* of `none` — the bar not applying, rather than the bar not being met — and there is no field for that. It lived in prose, which is why it read as authoritative and why nothing could check it.
3. **Should a `progression_audits` result cite its authority?** Adding an `owner_ruling` reference — present or explicitly absent — would make an unruled conclusion visible as one. Every gate in this repository reads the audit block; none reads who decided it.
4. **How does an owner discover this class of thing?** It surfaced only because a player happened to ask "who ruled that?" four days later. Nothing renders unruled structural conclusions anywhere, and `/status`, `/recap` and the readiness gate all pass straight over them.

**Related:** `F-020` (a ruling written into a rolling view and lost when it turned over), `F-002` (a vocabulary with no producers), and Decision 069's own principle that *"a gate whose subject writes its own verdict is not a gate"* — which is precisely what happened here, one layer down.

## F-040 — An earned technique's mastery track ignores the practice that earned it

**Raised:** 2026-08-19 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, 2026-08-21 in-fiction session
**Status:** Open

Section 7.1 treats the two values that describe a newly ratified technique in opposite ways, and states no reason for the difference.

- **Rank is authored at ratification.** *"Because an earned technique's name, **Rank**, Mana cost, and effect are not known merely from practice, final ratification requires an owner ruling."* Rank can therefore be set from the evidence, and at Gatefall 1.86 it was: Seamwork was authored native **C-Rank** rather than E because an E-Rank entry rung would have granted less than the qualifying evidence already demonstrated, which Section 18.3.1 forbids outright — *a grant that returns nothing the Bearer already lacks is not a grant.*
- **Mastery is fixed and explicitly barred from it.** *"Ratification creates the fully authored skill at **Novice**"*, and *"Pre-ratification evidence establishes acquisition only and **never backfills** post-acquisition successful-use or mastery counters."*

**What that produces.** A Bearer whose three qualifying scenes include a result the instructing expert states she has personally produced **twice in her career** enters the track at Novice, rendering `★☆☆☆☆`, every counter at zero — identical in every respect to a Bearer who scraped through three bare passes. The record that was good enough to author the skill's *Rank* upward is inadmissible for its *level*.

**Why this is an engine gap rather than one campaign's grievance.** Section 7.1's own five tests require each qualifying scene to show **material success** under genuine danger, consequential work, or structured practice with real feedback. That is the same evidence class Section 7.4 accepts to buy a mastery level. The profile counts it for acquisition and refuses to count it for progression, and nothing anywhere distinguishes the two uses.

**The counter-argument, which any fix has to answer rather than route around.** *"Pre-ratification evidence establishes acquisition only"* may be deliberate anti-double-counting: the three scenes are **spent** buying the skill, and spending them again on a level is drawing twice on one purchase. That reading is coherent and is probably the original intent.

**The open design questions.**

1. **Should starting mastery be authored at ratification the way Rank already is** — by owner ruling, from the same evidence?
2. **If so, what bounds it?** A natural bound falls out of Section 7.4's own arithmetic: three qualifying scenes is **exactly one mastery level**, so ratification could open at **Practiced (2) and never higher**, with `successful_uses` and `qualifying_scenes_total` still initialized at zero. That answers the double-counting objection on its own terms — the one level is not a second purchase, it is the recognition that the skill was *demonstrated into existence* rather than taught by a rune.
3. **Does the asymmetry have a defensible reading that should simply be written down?** If acquisition and progression are meant to be separate currencies, Section 7.1 should say so, because at present the difference reads as an oversight rather than a rule.
4. **Scope is prospective, by owner direction.** Whatever is decided applies only to techniques ratified **after** the ruling. No already-ratified skill is re-levelled and no counter is backfilled. Precedent is settled both ways in this repository: Decision 092's disposition coverage is prospective with a declared baseline, and `EVT-000574`'s qualifying-scene-distinctness ruling **runs forward only**, expressly declining to reopen Checkpoint 0099's credits.
5. **Which layer owns it?** The text is in the Gatefall profile's Section 7.1, but *acquisition versus progression* is a Chronicle Engine concept (Rules Section 5; Decision 051's growth-precedes-surfacing rule). A fix may belong in `docs/AI_GAMEPLAY_RUNTIME_PROFILE.md` or the engine Rules rather than in one world.

**Related:** `F-039` (the same session; a Runtime-authored audit closing this skill's candidate without a ruling), `F-014` and Gatefall 1.83 (a mastery level that returns nothing — this is the same concern at the track's first rung rather than its middle), and Section 18.3.1's *a grant that returns nothing the Bearer already lacks is not a grant*, which the Rank half already obeys and the mastery half does not.

## F-045 — Mastery is rendered twice on every skill line and nine of them disagreed, with the stale half looking authoritative

**Raised:** 2026-08-21 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, out-of-character report during 2026-08-23 in-fiction play — nine skill lines whose headline star string and tail `mastery level NAME (n)` differed, the headline exactly one level above the tail in all nine.

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-21).** The nine lines are reconciled against the chronicle — every one settled on the headline, and the tail prose was stale on all nine — and the gate that should have caught them is widened to read all three renders instead of the first. The underlying shape, mastery stored three times as prose, is now checked rather than prevented; see *Still open*.

**Mastery is stored once and rendered twice, and the two renders are prose.** A skill line carries a headline (`★★★★☆ Expert`) and a tail (`mastery level ADEPT (3)`), both hand-written into the same quoted string, against one authoritative `tracked_counters` entry. Nothing renders either from that counter, so a session that updates one and not the other leaves a line that disagrees with itself and passes every gate — `validate_repository.ps1` reconciles the **counter** against summed Event deltas (Decision 079) and never reads the prose beside it.

**Why the direction of staleness was not obvious, which is the part worth keeping.** The original report could not pick a side, and its reasoning was sound on the evidence it had:

- **Dagger Mastery** read *"progress 2/3 toward Expert"* beside its `ADEPT (3)` tail — internally consistent, so the tail looked live and the headline stale.
- **Twin Fang** recorded a crossing to *[B-Rank] Expert, ×2.35 → ×2.50* at `EVT-000629`, **later** than the `EVT-000590` breakthrough its tail was anchored to — so there the headline looked live.
- Four lines carried strings fitting neither reading: *"0/3 toward Master Practiced"*, and Sprint's *"0/3 toward Expert Adept"*.

**The third representation settled it.** `tracked_counters.mastery_level` agreed with the **headline on all nine**, and reconciles against summed chronicle deltas for every one of them — verified independently of the validator, counting **both** `counter_deltas` syntaxes. That last detail is its own small trap: a first pass counting only the inline `{ subject: …, counter: …, delta: N }` form and missing the multi-line one reported six false mismatches and would have argued for exactly the wrong answer.

So Dagger Mastery's apparent internal consistency was **two stale fields agreeing with each other** — its `2/3` is progress toward Master, not Expert — and the garbled strings were fragments of a next-level name edited in place without the old one being removed.

**What was corrected.** Nine tails moved to the stored level, and every `toward` string to the correct next level: Rupture, Keen Sense, Twin Fang, Exploit Pattern and Dagger Mastery to `EXPERT (4)`; Flash Step and Silent Step to `MASTER (5)`; Broken Rhythm to `PRACTICED (2)`; Sprint to `ADEPT (3)`. Twin Fang's effect line now states ×2.50 at B-Rank Expert, which its own formula gives — `1.00 + 0.35 × 3 + 0.15 × 3`, the mastery term being `0.15 × (level − 1)`.

**Closed (2026-08-21) — and the gate already existed.** `validate_repository.ps1` **did** compare rendered mastery against the counter. Its pattern was `\[[EDCBAS]-Rank\].*?\b(Novice|Practiced|Adept|Expert|Master)\b` — non-greedy from the Rank tag, so it matched the **headline** name and stopped. The headline was the correct render on all nine. **The gate read the one of three renders that nobody had let drift, and reported green.**

That is the finding worth keeping past this flag: a check reading one of several copies is indistinguishable from no check at all, and it fails in the direction that looks like success. It is `F-013`'s shape — a guard lagging its subject — with the guard lagging a *rendering convention* rather than a profile rule.

All three renders are now compared against `tracked_counters.mastery_level`: the headline name, the tail's **name and ordinal separately** (either can rot alone), and the `toward <name>` next-level string, plus a `toward` surviving at the Master ceiling. Mutation-verified — each of the five fails with its own message naming the render at fault.

**Two things that shaped the fix, both found by running it.** The first pass matched a bare `toward <name>` anywhere on the line and failed **Mend**, whose line legitimately recounts *"Expert-level history (28 uses, 2/3 toward Master) surrendered"* beside a live Novice; the check is now anchored to the live `mastery progress N/3 toward …` render, because everything else on a skill line may be prose about the past. And the mutation harness itself skipped all five cases silently on its first run — its anchors contained `·` and `★`, which Windows PowerShell 5.1 reads as mojibake from a BOM-less `.ps1`, so five non-matches looked exactly like five passes. Both are the same lesson the flag is about.

**Not done, deliberately: the gate has no fixture leg of its own.** It is proven by mutation and exercised against live canon on every run, but `test_validate_repository.ps1` carries no pinned case for it. Four fixture anchors in that suite went stale from ordinary play in a single day, and adding three more anchored on skill-line prose would cost more maintenance than the coverage buys. ## F-046 — A Bearer who outgrows Gate content has no structural route back to genuine danger before System Rank S

**Raised:** 2026-08-24 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, player pushback during 2026-08-24 in-fiction play, after two solo A-Rank instant-dungeon clears earned full kill XP and two level-ups apiece but zero mastery progress

**Status:** **Actioned (2026-08-24)** → Gatefall Profile 1.92 (`worlds/gatefall/migrations/1.91_to_1.92.md`), pinned by `tools/test_gatefall_danger_supply.ps1`. Disposition at the end of this entry.

The player spent two A-Rank instant-dungeon keys to "clear his head" before a scheduled meeting — both trivial by design, chosen specifically because they were beneath a Level 46+, System Rank A Bearer whose Rupture alone (5,458 standard) outdamages an A-Rank boss's full Rank Health (3,000) in one strike. Kill XP and the Gate-clear milestone settled in full both times (Section 3.3–3.4 are not danger-gated) and produced four level-ups; mastery did not move, because Section 7.4 requires "genuine dangerous-scene contributions" for a combat skill, and this campaign's own settlement history enforces that literally — `EVT-000527`, `EVT-000560`, `EVT-000574`, `EVT-000599`, `EVT-000667`, and `EVT-000676` all classify `gatefall.skill_credit: none` on the identical test, "nothing acted against him and nothing was rolled against him." The ruling this session was consistent with all six.

The player's objection, once the mastery rule itself was confirmed correct: **the world isn't supplying anything that could pass that test anymore.** This looks structurally true rather than a run of bad luck. Section 9.2's Rank distribution is 50% E / 25% D / 15% C / 7% B / 2.5% A / 0.5% S — a Chicago Gate is overwhelmingly likely to be trivial for a Bearer this far along, and the daily world tick (Section 9.1) rolls detections against that same distribution every morning, so the board's own supply skews the same way. Section 9.6's anomaly table (red gate, population-up, archetype-doubled, break-in-progress) is the one mechanism that can make a *low-assessed* Gate genuinely dangerous — but it only fires on an unconfirmed Gate's first-entry true-Rank roll or its unentered break (Section 9.5), so a Bearer who avoids public boards in favor of self-owned keys never rolls it at all. And Section 17 instant dungeons — the Bearer's most convenient, most-used danger source once he's carrying banked keys — never roll an anomaly at any Rank; a key's Rank is fixed at creation and the instance is always exactly that Rank, deterministically. A Bearer who has out-leveled his own key stockpile has a *mechanically guaranteed-safe* combat option always available, and the campaign's history above suggests players in this position gravitate to it, especially under real time pressure (as here, with a BGM meeting looming).

**The open design question:** should Gatefall author a route back to genuine stakes for an overleveled Bearer short of System Rank S — e.g., an anomaly-eligible instant-dungeon key tier, a public-Gate mechanism that scales real risk to the Bearer's own Combat Tier rather than the Gate's assessed Rank, or an explicit acknowledgment that mastery progression is *meant* to stall between power spikes and content spikes, with the stall itself being the intended pressure toward seeking harder public work? Also worth checking whether other worlds under this engine share the same shape — any world whose danger-gated mastery axis is fed by a Rank-distributed encounter table has the same ceiling problem once its Bearer outpaces the distribution's own upper tail.

**Related:** none yet on file, but shares its root with `F-034` (the world only rolling when pushed) — this is the same failure mode one layer further in: the world can be pushed correctly and still have nothing hard enough to offer.

**Disposition (2026-08-24) — the flag is right and its own diagnosis was one wall short of the count.** Actioned in full as Gatefall Profile 1.92, world content under Decision 069 point 4: no ADR, no decision number, no engine-layer change. Four remedies, none of which creates a Gate, raises a reward, or lowers a bar.

**1. Section 6.7 was excluding on frequency where it meant to exclude on agency.** `standing_worn_reduction` read *"a once-per-exchange or once-per-scene effect is a tactical resource the Bearer may spend, hold, or waste — not standing protection — and is excluded"* — one sentence covering two unlike things. A **spendable** effect needs a declaration and costs something and can be wasted; an **automatic** effect fires on its own trigger with nothing to declare and no way to fail to have it. The test is now whether the Bearer can fail to have it. Everything spendable stays excluded. **This was found from the player's side of the table**: `100_CHARACTER_SHEET.md` records two A-Rank Warded Vambraces bought *"because the defence axis is what pins Combat Tier"*, each an unconditional −30% with nothing to declare — and the formula that exists to notice investment declined to read the investment aimed directly at it.

**2. Section 7.4's combat-skill test is now contest, not danger.** A combat skill qualifies where something could have made it fail at a cost: an opposed resolution, an opponent who could beat him, a cost on losing. **All six settlements the flag cites stay correct and none is re-scored** — *nothing acted against him and nothing was rolled against him* fails a contest test exactly as it failed a danger test. What it opens is the route a combat skill never had while a non-combat skill always did: Section 7.4 has always allowed *structured practice* for non-combat skills, so **Gatefall authored no way for a hunter to train.** Section 6.2.1's pulled strike, written at 1.87 for `F-042`, already makes the contested non-lethal scene fully resolvable and had nothing pointing at it.

**3. New Section 9.11 authors the channel four sections already described.** Section 9.4 rates an S-Rank Gate a state-directed national-asset operation, Section 9.1 step 4 records S-Rank as never sitting on a board, Section 9.3 names an S-Rank draft, and Section 12.3 prices A-Rank work at national-asset scale — **and none of them authored how any of it reaches a hunter.** Section 9.11 is a regulator's call list keyed to card Rank, rolled on the world tick, with three refusal tiers ending in the draft. It routes Gates the tick already rolled and creates none.

**The flag's third option is refuted, and this is the arithmetic that does it.** *"Mastery is meant to stall between spikes, with the stall being pressure toward harder public work"* fails because the harder public work is unreachable by composition. Section 9.2 puts A-Rank at 2.5% of Chicago's 1d4+3 daily Gates (≈0.14/day); Section 9.1 step 4 has the trade take an A-Rank posting at 90% per day, so it survives one day at 10%; Section 9.1 step 2 sites only 30% within his reach. **An A-Rank Gate reaches a freelancer roughly once per 240 in-fiction days, and an S-Rank Gate never.** Both mechanisms are individually correct and honest. Nobody authored what they compose into, and what they compose into is a **B-Rank ceiling**. A stall with no exit is not pressure.

**4. Section 17 gains the proving key** — the Combat Tier's third consumer, at the Bearer's own tier, snapshotted when the key is **spent**, rolling no anomaly, sold only by the shop at twice its tier's ordinary key price. The flag's own first suggestion, *anomaly-eligible ordinary keys*, is **rejected**: Section 17 states twice that a key's fixed Rank is the whole of its risk economy and expressly forbids hardening a stockpile, and the reasoning is sound. A key that is bought knowing what it is takes nothing from *the Bearer chooses his own difficulty*; it is the honest setting finally being available.

**The wall the flag did not count, and it was the largest one.** Section 6.7's Combat Tier is the profile's only mechanism that sizes content to the Bearer, and it has always had exactly two consumers — a class trial spent once per campaign, and a penalty zone that fires only on a failed daily quest. **At a sixteen-day streak with Riftwalker long taken, the mechanism had no live consumer at all.** The flag reasoned about the supply of *content*; the deeper failure was that the one thing built to answer this had nothing left to act on. Remedy 4 gives it a third that recurs.

Not raised as its own flag: it should be watched at System Rank S, where the ladder runs out entirely and there is no higher rung for either axis to reach.

## F-047 — A growth axis gated on danger prices itself out of the campaign that succeeds, and this is not one world's mistake

**Raised:** 2026-08-24 · **Source:** cross-campaign; extracted from `F-046`'s disposition while actioning it Gatefall-side

**Status:** Open.

`F-046` was actioned entirely in the Gatefall profile, on the owner's ruling that the engine-general half should be raised separately rather than migrated on one campaign's evidence. This is that half.

**The observation.** Gatefall's Section 7.4 required *"genuine dangerous-scene contributions"* for a combat skill's mastery track. A campaign's whole direction of travel is toward the protagonist being in **less** danger — that is what progression *is* — so an advancement axis whose qualifying condition is danger becomes unreachable in precisely the campaign that is working. Gatefall reached that point at System Rank A, with a live Bearer whose strongest skill outdamaged the Rank Health of the content he could get to, and whose mastery counters had stopped moving while every other counter kept climbing. The world could not supply the shortfall either: its Rank-distributed encounter table and its own market rates composed into a content ceiling well below him, and neither mechanism was individually wrong.

**Why it looks engine-general rather than Gatefall-specific.** The shape needs only two ingredients, and neither is Gatefall's: **a progression axis gated on a difficulty condition**, and **a content supply drawn from a fixed distribution the protagonist can outgrow.** Any world under this engine that pairs those has the same ceiling waiting at whatever rung its own distribution runs out. It is also the same family as `F-002` — a rule whose triggering condition has no reachable evaluation point — except that here the condition is reachable and simply stops occurring.

**The open design question:** does `012_ENGINE_RUNTIME.md` or `docs/AI_GAMEPLAY_RESIDENT_CORE.md` owe a principle here — something in the shape of *an advancement condition must be stated in terms the protagonist's own growth cannot exhaust; where danger is the intended condition, the rule states the contest it stands for* — and if so, at what layer? Writing it into `012` as an obligation every world must satisfy meets **Decision 069 leg 3** and is therefore foundational, landing against a released version at milestone 0.4.3, so it needs an ADR, classification, migration and revalidation rather than a paragraph. Worth checking `worlds/asterra/`, `worlds/reikon/` and `worlds/verra/` for the same pairing before deciding whether the engine owes anything at all, or whether three worlds simply have not run far enough to find out.

**Related:** `F-046` (where it was found, and where the world-side remedy landed), `F-002` (a rule whose evaluation point did not exist), `F-034` (a world that only moved when pushed).

## F-048 — The Combat Tier is derived, never stored, rendered everywhere, and recomputed by nothing

**Raised:** 2026-08-24 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, found while actioning `F-046`

**Status:** Open.

Gatefall Profile Section 6.7 says the Combat Tier is *"derived, never stored"* and *"snapshotted, never live"* — each consumer computes it once, at the instant its own section names. That is a complete rule for a **consumer**. It is not a rule for a **render**, and the campaign renders it constantly: `180_CURRENT_STATE.md` carries it on the headline status line and `100_CHARACTER_SHEET.md` reasons about it in item notes and allocation notes.

**The render is wrong, and has been for several levels.** At Checkpoint 0120 the ledger reports **Combat Tier A**. Recomputed from that same ledger under 1.91's own arithmetic — maximum Health 4,016, standing worn reduction ≈51.5%, best standard result 5,823, System Rank A — `offense_tier = S` (S-Rank commons hold 4,000 and the ladder has no higher rung) and `defense_tier = S` (`incoming(S) = 1,000 × 0.485 = 485`, and `4,016 ÷ 485 = 8.3`, well past the five-hit band). `max(A, min(S, S)) = S`. The tier crossed when maximum Health passed roughly 2,700 at the then-current reduction, and nothing re-derived it.

**What it cost.** The Section 8.3 penalty zone reads the Combat Tier and is the campaign's only remaining imposed danger source. Had it fired against the rendered value it would have dropped him into an **A-Rank** instance while its own rule said S — the exact failure `F-046` was raised about, produced not by a missing mechanism but by a stale number in front of a working one. **A rendered derived value with no stated recomputation point is indistinguishable from a stored one that nobody updates**, which is the shape of `F-037` (a stored comparison against Bearer state going stale inside one session) and `F-045` (a value rendered twice with the stale half looking authoritative).

**The open design question:** where does a derived value's *render* get its recomputation obligation? Options, and they are not equivalent: a per-turn or per-checkpoint re-derivation clause in Section 6.7 itself; a promotion-time reconciliation like Section 7.4's, which already re-counts skill uses before a checkpoint writes; a validator that recomputes the tier from the sheet and fails on disagreement, which is the treatment arithmetic drift already gets under Data Model 0.1.5; or a rule that a derived value is simply **not rendered** outside the moment a consumer computes it. The last is the cheapest and the most disruptive to how the ledger currently reads. This is likely engine-general — the same question applies to any derived quantity a campaign ledger displays.

**Not fixed by Profile 1.92.** The 1.91 → 1.92 migration's step 2 corrects **this instance** and says so explicitly; the cause is untouched. Correcting the live ledger is campaign canon and belongs to a play session under save discipline.

**Related:** `F-046` (found while actioning it), `F-037`, `F-045` (the same shape on stored and doubly-rendered values), `F-002` (a rule with no evaluation point).

- **Category `Return` (A)** — appearing nowhere in the profile, whose authored A-Rank category was `Blind step`. `Return` had been played from (`160_CAMPAIGN_CHRONICLE.md`: *"`Return` was loaded for the disengage and never used"*), so a category with no authored existence was live campaign state.

The line also omitted the `Through the barrier` (C) and `Through the ward` (B) categories a cumulative ladder should render, and the anchor block's `cap_formula` note read *"effective Agility 713 / 20 … cap 35"* beside a `cap` field of 48. **Four renderings, four disagreements with their own rule, none of which any gate could see** — the `F-037`/`F-045` shape again, and the reason it matters is that a player reasoning from the sheet was reasoning from a class ability's numbers on a rune's line.

**The remedy, and it is a rework rather than a nerf.** Flash Step is bound to **perception** at every Rank — line of sight, or a live read through the C/B categories — with no metre band anywhere in its line and no Rank at which it reaches somewhere unperceived; `Blind step` is replaced by **`Interpose`**, a reaction that puts him between an attack and another person. Rift-step splits into an **in-scene step** (unchanged, and deliberately kept so a Riftwalker need not buy a rune to fight) and a **travelling step** reaching **anywhere he has personally stood**, priced by ledgered container rather than metres. **Arrival is blind** — geometry without contents — which is what makes anchors the only ground he arrives at knowing what is there, converting `Agility ÷ 20` from a travel cap into a knowledge cap. Anchors gain the **answering step** and **warded ground**. A hard rule forbids any rift-step crossing an instance boundary in either direction, because Sections 8.3, 9.6, 9.7, 13.2 and 18.2 all rest on it.

**Left open.**

- **The campaign-side repair is play.** Correcting the Flash Step line's reach and category, rendering its C/B categories, re-deriving the stale `cap_formula`, and re-rendering `system_rank_rungs` are canon writes under save discipline. The migration's step 2 states them; none is done here. **Scenes already resolved on the phantom reach are not re-adjudicated** — `EVT-000631`'s rift-step home and any step taken on the 100 m band stand as ruled.
- **How `Return` entered canon is not established.** It may have been an owner ruling in play that never landed in the profile, or a Runtime fabrication that promoted. The evidence does not distinguish them and the disposition does not guess; what is fixed is that the profile now has an authored A-Rank category and a gate that fails if the ladder loses it.
- **Nothing checks a skill line against its own authored ladder.** `tools/test_gatefall_riftwalker_contract.ps1` pins the *profile*, so this exact drift cannot recur in law — but the **rendering** that carried it lives in campaign state, and no gate compares a rendered skill entry to the Section 7.3 row it derives from. That is the general defect behind all four drifts found here and it is **not** fixed. Worth raising separately if a fifth appears.
- **The class-grant family has no check either.** `F-032`, `F-033` and this flag are all *a permanent grant that duplicates something the Bearer already holds*, and Section 18.3.1's *"a cap that cannot bind is not a cap"* is the closest thing to a rule about it. Whether Section 18.3.1 owes an explicit **novelty test** — a class grant must do something no skill, item, or prior class grant already does — is the open engine-adjacent question this raises.

**Related:** `F-032`, `F-033` (class grants that returned nothing new), `F-037`, `F-045`, `F-048` (rendered values drifting from their own rule), `F-002` (a condition with no evaluation point — why the travelling step reads *stood* rather than *seen*).

## F-050 — Fusion's no-stacking rule is stated plainly and still reads as a bug at the table

**Raised:** 2026-08-26 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, player reaction during play — expected two identical Perception+2 Seer's Lenses fused into one to read Perception+4

**Status:** Open.

**The rule is not ambiguous and was applied correctly.** Section 12.10 states outright, twice, that fusion moves an effect and never a magnitude: *"a rule letting two pieces pool their protection, power, or stat points into one slot would retire the Rank ladder without a Rank being climbed,"* and separately, *"It gains no stat points, no weapon power, and no physical damage reduction. A donor whose whole value is a stat line has nothing to give and cannot be a donor."* Two identical D-Rank Seer's Lenses (Perception +2 each, Boss-Imprinted with a Mobility Echo) were fused this session at Priscilla Nakamura's bench, resolved on the success band, and the result renders exactly as authored: Perception +2 (the frame's own line, unmoved) plus a second independent instance of the Mobility Echo the donor carried — which Section 11.2.1's one-Echo-per-scene wearer cap then makes functionally inert. The Runtime cited the section, explained the design reasoning already written into it, and declined to award Perception +4.

**And the player's expectation was still reasonable, which is the actual finding.** "Fuse two copies of the same accessory" is the single most intuitive case for stat-combination in the genre this profile is drawing from, and it is also the one case Section 12.10 goes furthest out of its way to forbid — a donor whose *only* asset is a stat line is explicitly the disqualified case, not an edge the rule forgot. The section's reasoning (stat-pooling retires the Rank ladder) is sound and load-bearing, but nothing in the fiction or the panels surfaces that reasoning **before** a player commits two items and a fusion fee to find out. `/system gear <item>` and the fusion commissioning flow both render eligibility (same slot class, functional condition, un-equipped, within one Rank, never fused) without a line stating the ceiling on what a *successful* fusion returns. A player who reads "eligible" and "success band" has been told the working *could* happen and *did* happen, and nothing between those two facts told them what they were buying.

**The open design question:** does Section 12.10's commissioning flow, or the `/system gear` panel's fusion-eligible flag, owe a rendered line stating **what the result gains** before the player commits material — not just whether two pieces qualify, but the fact that a stat-only donor is being nominated wastefully or that an identical-Lens fusion nets one Echo instance with no wearer-side effect? This is the same family as `F-032`/`F-033`/`F-049` — a grant that returns less than expected because nobody checked what was already held — but the mechanism here is informational rather than mechanical: the rule was never wrong, the player was never shown enough of it in advance to form the right expectation. Worth checking whether other eligibility-gated systems in this profile (Section 7.5 skill-rank ascension, Section 16's title equip) have the same gap between "this is legal" and "here is what it is worth."

**Related:** `F-032`, `F-033`, `F-049` (grants that duplicate something already held), `F-030` (templates versus values — a related class of thing a Runtime renders without checking it against player expectation).

## F-051 — "Carrying a piece to its authored ceiling" is named as the Bearer's own technique, and nothing says whether an ordinary artificer can do it too

**Raised:** 2026-08-26 · **Source:** `campaigns/gatefall_pendragon_001/`, player question during play — *"If I can do it [via Seamwork], she should be able to do it too. She is a full artificer, I have just one skill."*

**Status:** Open.

**What the profile actually authors.** Section 12.8 names exactly four artificer trades, explicit and enumerated across 12.8 and 12.10: identification, creation/commissioning, teaching, and fusion (12.10 opens by calling itself *"the fourth trade,"* which only parses if three others are already named). In the same paragraph that draws the origination/improvement line, the profile introduces a fifth capability — reading an existing working, opening it without wrecking the piece, seating material into it, lifting a working out of it, and *"carrying it toward its authored ceiling"* — and assigns it to **Seamwork**, a technique the Bearer specifically earns and holds (Section 7.1 ratification), bounded by his own Seamwork Rank. It is never listed among what a licensed artificer sells, and no NPC artificer in the campaign — including Priscilla Nakamura, an independent working D/C/B-Rank identification and commission work — has ever been shown doing it or asked about it before this session.

**The player's objection, stated plainly.** A trained licensed professional whose whole signature ability is working mana into matter ought to be at least as capable as a technique the Bearer picked up as one skill among many, and a profile that's silent on whether ordinary artificers can do this reads as an oversight rather than a deliberate exclusion — especially since Section 12.8's own text frames Seamwork as *"the hands may still work"* alongside *"the signature originates,"* implying the *improving* half was never meant to be exclusively his.

**What the Runtime did in the moment.** Declined to invent a licensed-artificer version of the capability on logic alone — correctly, per the standing rule against inventing mechanical values — and played Nakamura's own uncertainty honestly (*"I don't know... I've never met an artificer who claims to do that"*) rather than asserting either a yes or a no on her behalf. That holds the line but doesn't resolve the actual gap: the profile has an authored technique with no stated population of who else in the world might hold it, which is a worldbuilding silence rather than a closed door, and it was left open rather than ruled.

**The open design question:** does Section 12.8 (or a new subsection) owe an explicit statement of whether ceiling-raising work is (a) exclusively a Bearer-only technique by design, on the same "he remains no artificer" logic that already bars origination, or (b) a rare-but-real fifth trade some licensed artificers hold, gated by the same rarity Section 12.8 already applies to the signature ability itself — and if (b), what Nakamura's specific standing is (she's established as *not* having built from scratch, *not* having fused before this session; whether she's ever attempted ceiling-work is simply unauthored, not established either way). This is the same shape as `F-050` one level up: a capability's *existence and population* left ambiguous rather than its *cost*, and it's worth checking whether other Seamwork rungs (Read and open, Seat, Lift) have the same silent boundary, or whether only Raise does.

**Related:** `F-050` (fusion's outcome not surfaced before commitment — same family, one layer earlier: what a capability is worth vs. whether it exists), `F-039` (the procedural defect from Seamwork's own reopening, where "no player crafting subsystem" was over-read once already).
