## What 1.77 changed

Two gaps, both found by projecting what the tick would actually produce once it started running.

### 1. The tick had no demand side, so every Gate ran to a break

Steps 1–3 rolled Gates, sited them and rolled rarity. Step 4 then ran the break timers. **Nothing in between rolled the trade taking the job.**

That is not what this world says happens. Section 9.10 is explicit: *"`staffed` or `held` → cleared off-screen. The trade did its job… **This is the ordinary case**"*, and *"the institutions act before the deadline exactly as their ledgers say they do — BGM escalates, raises the rate, or issues an emergency contract, and a guild dispatch may take it."* Chicago has four guilds, a co-op desk and a regulator competing for this work. A break is what happens when **nobody took it** — a market outcome, not a countdown.

Left as it stood, a projection of the tick produced roughly 38 Gates a week and a break for nearly every one of them, which would have made the dominant world event the rarest one the fiction describes.

**New step 4 rolls contest per posting per day**, and the rates are read off the campaign's own board rather than invented:

```text
     E-Rank      15   per day     cheap work, insulting split eight ways
     D-Rank      25   per day
     C-Rank      55   per day     worth a guild's bench
     B-Rank      75   per day     contested; auction wars start here
     A-Rank      90   per day     every major guild wants it
     S-Rank     ---              state-directed; never sits on a board
```

The four tracked postings the campaign has actually played are the derivation: `GB-01` and `GB-04`, both E-Rank Coalition postings, sat unbid to their deadlines and broke; `GB-02`, an unconfirmed D, was *"starved of bids by the Ironline/Horizon auction war"*; `GB-03`, a confirmed C, was *"won at auction, staffed from Horizon's own bench, cleared off-screen on schedule."* Section 9.4's own economics — cheap jobs sit — says the same thing.

The effect is that **break pressure sits at the bottom of the ladder**, where the money explains it, instead of spread evenly across every Rank. A taken posting is still a world event: who took it is news, and a guild clearing a Gate on his route is something the Bearer can hear about, be offered a place on, or lose an opportunity to.

### 2. Every world event was a Gate

Through 1.76 all six steps produced Gates, breaks, or postings about Gates. A city with four guilds, a regulator, a market, an underground and a press corps generated nothing else. **A world whose only event type is the one the protagonist fights in is a dungeon queue, not a city.**

**New step 8 and Section 9.1.2** roll one d100 per tick on a nine-row table: nothing (30%), guild movement, regulatory action, market movement, a non-Gate opportunity, aftermath, a private life moving, news, and a 96–00 band for something genuinely unusual. Each row is sourced from institutions and threads the world already has. Siting uses the same `01-10` / `11-30` / `31-00` bands, and an event elsewhere is still recorded — the city has a history whether or not he is in it.

**Section 8.4.2 is untouched and this does not create Urgent quests.** Its criterion 2 admits only a Gate creature, an active Gate break, or a directly perceived sealed-instance hazard. A market shift is not a threat of death and must never be dressed as one to justify an offer. What these events feed is **supply, pressure and fiction** — an opening a Section 7.5 source can now hold, a reason a contact calls, a fact the Bearer can learn, a consequence that lands later.

## What did not change

**No Rank distribution, siting band, break timer, party minimum, anomaly entry or population formula moves.** Sections 9.2, 9.3, 9.4, 9.5, 9.6 and 9.7 are byte-identical in effect. Steps 1–3 are exactly as 1.73 authored them; the old steps 4–6 are renumbered 5–7 and their text is unchanged apart from step 5 now saying it runs on *what nobody took*.

**Section 8.4.2's quest criteria are unchanged**, and 1.77 adds no new quest channel.

**The tick record is unchanged in form.** Section 9.1.1's `world_ticks` entry gains the two new steps' results — contest outcomes and the non-Gate event — under the same rule that `none` is a result and is written as one.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.76 → 1.77.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.77"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, Stat, level, XP, Rank, skill, mastery, counter, title, item, gold, cash, quest, candidate, class, fragment, supply source, standing need or tracked posting is touched.

**3. No past break is re-adjudicated.** `GB-01`, `GB-02` and `GB-04` broke under the rules in force at the time and their outcomes stand exactly as promoted; `GB-03` cleared. The contest roll is prospective and applies from the first tick under this profile. **These four are the evidence the rates were derived from, not cases to be replayed against them.**

**4. `world_ticks` is unchanged and still empty.** No tick has run. The first tick falls 2026-08-20 06:00 and writes the first entry, now covering eight steps rather than six.

**5. No campaign owes a re-audit.** Nothing consumed these steps at resolution time before this version, because the tick has never run.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.76-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.77 at readiness.
