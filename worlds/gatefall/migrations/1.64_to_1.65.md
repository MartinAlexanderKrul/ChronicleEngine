## What 1.65 changed

**Section 19.3 closed on a sentence that reads as a limit on size, and is not one.** The line was *"Reawakening explains one jump; it cannot explain a curve."* Every word of it is defensible and the shape of it is misleading: **one jump** invites the reading that the classification tolerates a *small* correction and refuses a large one — that an E-Rank card measuring at a D-Rank pool is admissible while the same card measuring at a B-Rank pool is too much to claim.

**It was misread that way in play**, which is the whole reason this edge exists. The wording did not merely permit the wrong reading; nothing else in the section contradicted it, so the wrong reading survived contact with the rest of the text.

**The rest of Section 19.3 already settles it, and settles it the other way.** The section states that reawakening is *"a correction of a card mismeasured at Awakening, not a second Awakening and not a Rank that moved"*, and that *"the only admissible account of a changed number is that the original card was wrong all along."* **A wrong measurement has no natural size.** If the claim is that the original appraiser got it wrong, then a wider gap says the appraiser got it *more* wrong — which is a more embarrassing error, not a less believable correction. The instruments are held infallible about the present; nothing anywhere requires the past to have been off by only a little.

**Ruled, and stated in the section rather than left to be inferred:**

- **The classification is not magnitude-limited.** An E-Rank card that re-measures at a B-Rank pool is exactly as admissible a claim as one that re-measures at D. There is no size at which a regulator refuses the classification *on size alone*.
- **What it cannot absorb is repetition.** One correction is a correction. A second on the same card is not — two mismeasurements of one hunter stops being an appraiser's error and becomes a pattern the instruments are not permitted to have, and a pattern is what the world would then have to explain some other way.
- **The constraint is therefore on the number of claims, never on the distance any one claim covers.** The closing line now reads: *reawakening explains a jump of any size; it cannot explain a series.*

**A wide gap is not free, and the cost was already authored.** It buys **attention** — Section 19.1's four surfacing routes, and the *how, when, witnessed by whom* scrutiny Section 19.3 already names — and Section 19.4's trade is unchanged. What a wide gap does not cost is **credibility**, and that distinction is the entire content of this edge.

**Nothing about the mechanism moves.** Section 19.2's mana-capacity re-measurement, its capacity table, and the voluntary/court-ordered split are untouched. Section 19.4's refusal costs are untouched. This edge says what an already-existing classification *covers*, not what anyone can do or what any instrument reads.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.64 → 1.65.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.65"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, Stat, level, XP, System Rank, Card Rank, skill, mastery, counter, title, item, gold, cash, quest, or candidate is touched. No hunter's card moves and no measurement is re-taken.

**3. Nothing retroactive, and no past scene is re-adjudicated.** Every re-assessment, every claimed or withheld reawakening, and every consequence promoted under 1.64 or earlier stands exactly as recorded. In particular this edge does **not** retroactively make a previously-refused claim admissible: where a campaign ruled against a Bearer's cover under the old ambiguity, that ruling stands as canon and is not reopened here.

**4. The rule is prospective and applies from adoption forward**, to claims adjudicated after the adopting campaign's anchor.

**5. No campaign owes a re-audit.** No roster, record, or NPC belief needs recomputation; the rule is read at the point a claim is assessed and is stored nowhere.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.64-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.65 at readiness.
