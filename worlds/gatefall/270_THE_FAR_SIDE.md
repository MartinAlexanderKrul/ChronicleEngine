# 1. What It Is

`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 9 already says it in one line: the beasts are *"the harvested remnants of worlds that already failed — a graveyard shipped from one exam to the next,"* and *"every dungeon is a museum of the dead."* This file is that graveyard as a place a man can stand in.

**Owner term: the Stacks.** It is not a world. It is the **seed-library** — every biosphere the process has harvested, kept, and drawn from since it began, held in mana-suspension and stored in layers. A Gate is a **core sample**: one cut, through one seam, into one slice of one dead world, seeded into a living one to test it. Every Gate the Bearer has ever cleared was a few hundred metres of somewhere that lost.

**The single fact that makes it what it is: each stratum is still in the hour it died.** Suspension is not preservation of a corpse; it is a held moment. A world harvested nine thousand years ago still has the fires of its last afternoon burning in the middle distance, still has whatever came through its punctures moving in the open, still has the light of the day it lost. Nothing there decays and nothing there finishes. **The Stacks are not ruins. They are an unending disaster, filed.**

**There is no sky.** Each stratum keeps the light it had, and above it the light stops at a seam — a surface with no depth, the underside of the next slice up. A man standing on a dead plain sees a horizon that behaves, a sun that does not move, and a ceiling made of a different world's ground.

# 2. Physical Law

**Nothing here carries a Rank, and that is not an oversight.** Section 18.13 states it: the System has never seen these things and does not price them. Rank is the Assay's *grading of a sample*, applied when a slice is cut and seeded. In the library, before the cut, nothing has been graded. A creature that would be an E-Rank common in a Gate stands here at whatever it actually was, and so does the thing that ate it.

**Resolution, therefore, is authored and never improvised.** Every entity in this file carries a stated magnitude and a statement of what it does, in **one of two forms and never a third.** A thing that is not a person — a stratum's wildlife, whatever the Mill has awake, whatever the Weir has bred — states a **Combat Tier equivalent** (`206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 6.7), the same handle a Runtime uses for graded content. A **System-bearer** states a **sheet** instead (Section 13.1.1): five Stats, `4 x Vitality` and `2 x Intelligence` pools, mastery, and no Rank of any kind. **The failed champions of Section 4 are bearers and take the second form**, because the Combat Tier scale caps at S and the Bearer passed that rung sixty-three levels before he ever opened a door here — pricing the only people in existence who can teach him on a ruler pinned at its stop is how a teacher ends up one-shot mid-lesson. **A Runtime that meets something not authored here stops and asks the owner** — Section 18.13 already binds it to that, and this file does not relax it. There is no population formula, no d8 archetype, no anomaly table, and no boss.

**Mana density is the first hazard and it is not survivable indefinitely.** The gray sleep (`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 5) is chronic mana saturation contracted over years of ordinary exposure near Gates. Here the exposure is total. **An awakened human accumulates a day of ordinary Gate-work's saturation roughly every hour**, and an unawakened one cannot survive a visit at all. This is the clock on every expedition and it is the reason nobody could ever have colonised the place even if they had the door.

**Mechanically the clock is his Mana pool, and the rule is `206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 5.2.1.** An awakened body sheds absorbed mana by spending pool — that is what an unawakened body lacks (`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 5) and why no hunter has ever contracted the gray sleep from ordinary Gate-work. Here the shedding never stops: **no Mana recovery accrues at all, and the pool falls by 10% of its maximum every hour.** Ten hours empties a full pool; at zero the drain crosses into Health and the gray sleep arrives as an authored injury (Section 5.3). **There is no resting it off and no drinking through it** — the Stacks permit no rest, and a Greater potion degrades to its Standard form off-world (Section 12.5).

A Runtime narrates this as the body reports it — a cold that gets into him, a slowness he cannot shake off, the sense of being spent without having done anything. **The numbers stay out of the fiction** (Section 20.5), and the Bearer has no instrument here that reads them.

**Time runs, but the strata are not synchronised with each other or with Earth.** Two slices adjacent at a seam may be ten thousand years apart in their own reckoning. Time inside a stratum runs at Earth's rate for anyone standing in it — **there is no relativistic trick here and a Runtime must not invent one.** What is desynchronised is the *held moment*, not the clock.

**Crossing a seam is the only navigation.** Seams are where slices meet, and they are thin in places and welded in others. A thin seam can be walked through — it reads as a wall of grey static a few metres deep, and the far side is another world's ground. **Gate Walker's far door opens onto exactly one stratum**, chosen or not, and everything past the first seam is walking.

## 2.1 Anchors, tunnels, and the door — the rule is that the door is the only way in

Riftwalker anchors and the Gate Walker tunnel network (`206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 18.13.1) both function here, and the Bearer has already proved it: an anchor was set on the Ninth Quiet and an anchored rift-step made from it (`EVT-000784`, `EVT-000785`). The question Section 18.13.1 never asked is what *"any two places he has stood"* means once one of those places is a stratum, and this section answers it.

**A tunnel joins two far-side places. It never bridges to Earth.** Anchors set in the Stacks link to other anchors in the Stacks on the ordinary Section 18.13.1 terms — permanent, two-way, crossed freely by anyone, holding whether or not he is standing in either end. **A tunnel with one end on Earth and one end in the Stacks cannot be formed at all.** The far door remains the sole authored crossing between the two, exactly as Section 18.13 states it, and this is a ruling recorded in play (`EVT-000814`) rather than a new restriction.

**The reason is the thing on the other side of it.** A far door is *closeable* — Section 18.13.1 gives the Bearer a stated price to shut one and one condition that stops him. A tunnel has neither. A permanent, unattended, two-way passage standing open between the Long Shelf and a city on Earth is precisely and completely what Section 4.1's **Out** position has been waiting for since before there were doors, and it would be handed over as a side effect of logistics, while he slept. **The door is a decision he makes every time. A tunnel would be a decision he makes once.**

**The anchored step is the road, and it works here exactly as Section 18.8 writes it.** A step to one of his own set anchors is a **flat 5 Mana from any distance**, and inside the Stacks that is the whole of what it costs — the Bearer has already taken one (`EVT-000785`). **An anchor he holds in a stratum is reachable from any stratum**, so an expedition does not walk the ground it has already walked: he opens the door onto the Landing, steps to the deepest anchor he holds, and starts from there. **This is not a new grant and nothing here rations it.** It is the class ability he has carried since level 25, and the far side is one of the few places its flat cost is the difference between an expedition and an impossibility.

**Setting an anchor here is the ordinary Section 18.8 act** — an exchange spent standing on ground he has reason to hold, one slot against `effective Agility ÷ 20`, released freely. **The Landing is the anchor a Runtime should expect him to want**, because it is where the door is and he has never set one there.

**No step of any kind crosses between Earth and the Stacks, and the anchored step is named here because Section 18.8 calls it unrestricted.** That section frees it from *instance walls* — a Gate, a red gate, an instant dungeon — and the Stacks are not an instance; they are a place, on the far side of the one crossing this world authors. **An anchored step, a travelling step, an answering step, a personal gate and a tunnel all stop at that boundary in both directions.** The far door is the sole authored crossing, and a Bearer standing on the Long Shelf does not step home for 5 Mana.

**The reason is the thing on the other side of it, and it is sharper for the anchored step than for the tunnel.** At System Rank S the Section 18.8 table grants that *"an ally he touches may be sent with him on any step."* A five-Mana step out of the Stacks is therefore not only a way home; it is a way to bring one of Section 4.1's **Out** position with him, on a reaction, from anywhere, for pocket change. **The door is a decision he makes every time, with a price and a condition that can stop him. Everything barred above is a decision he would make once, or in an exchange, without meaning to.**

**A far door always opens onto the Landing (Section 3.1), and no anchor changes that.** He cannot open a door onto a stratum he has anchored, and holding an anchor deeper in confers no shorter way *in* from Earth — only a shorter way onward once he is through, and a shorter way back to the door before he leaves.

**Tunnels remain available and are a different tool.** A standing tunnel is permanent, needs nobody present, and **anyone may cross it** — which an anchored step never allows, since the step is his. Within the Stacks that is worth building where he wants a route someone else can use; **crossing a standing tunnel is instantaneous and costs nothing**, and it is the only movement in this file that costs no clock at all. Each occupies a slot from the same cap as any anchor.

## 2.2 Distance, and what it costs

Section 2 authors a clock and, through Profile 1.108, authored no distances for it to run against — which made every expedition either free or impossible depending on who narrated it. **These are the authored legs.** A Runtime reads travel time from this table and never estimates one; a route not on it is unauthored ground and Section 2's stop-and-ask rule governs.

The costs assume the Bearer's own mobility — Sprint, Flash Step and rift-step, at the ranks a Gate Walker holds — and are what a hundred-level Bearer covers, not what a person covers. **They are traversal only.** Fighting, searching, reading, mining, and talking are additional and are counted at the table's own scale.

| Leg | Clock |
|---|---|
| **The Landing** — the door to its seam | 15 min |
| **Any seam crossing** | 15 min |
| **The Mill** — seam to seam | 25 min |
| **The Ninth Quiet** — seam to seam | 20 min |
| **The Weir** — seam to seam | **2 hours**, plus `1d4 x 15 min` |
| **The Long Shelf** — seam to the valley | 15 min |
| **An anchored step** (Section 2.1) | none, at its Section 18.8 flat 5 Mana |
| **Any standing tunnel** (Section 2.1) | none |

**The Weir is the only leg a Runtime rolls, and it is rolled because the ground is not reliable** (Section 3.4) — the route found on the way in is not necessarily there on the way out, and the die is rolled again for the return. Every other leg is fixed; a stratum's geography is a held moment and does not move.

**What this composes to, and it is the point of the section.** Walked end to end, the Landing to the Long Shelf is **4h30m to 5h15m** one way — 135 minutes of fixed legs either side of the Weir's rolled 135 to 180. Against Section 2's drain of 10% of maximum Mana an hour, a Bearer who pays Section 18.13.1's door price out of a full pool has a little over five and a half hours before the drain crosses into Health. **Walked in both directions the trip is not survivable and is not close**, and no amount of planning makes it so.

**What makes it possible is the anchored step, and this is why Section 2.1 matters more than the table does.** An anchor already held deep in cuts the legs that are merely repetition. Starting from the Ninth Quiet — the one anchor the Bearer actually holds (`EVT-000784`) — the outbound run is **3h20m to 4h05m**, the return is one 5-Mana step back to that anchor and **1h15m** of walking to the door, and the round trip lands at **4h35m to 5h20m against a budget of about 5h34m.** He makes it, by somewhere between fourteen minutes and an hour, and the Weir's die decides which.

**That margin is the intended shape and is not to be widened by narration.** A Bearer who spends an hour fighting, reading, or negotiating on the Long Shelf has spent his way out of it, and Section 6.5's finality applies to running out of pool exactly as it applies to anything else. **The way to buy margin is another anchor, not another hour** — an anchor set on the Landing turns the whole return into a single step and a walk through the door, and one set past the Weir removes the campaign's only rolled leg from the outbound run. **The expedition that reaches the bottom of the Stacks comfortably is the third one**, and each of the two before it is a trip whose whole purpose is to stand somewhere long enough to hold it.

# 3. The Strata

Five are authored. They are not a map — the Stacks are indefinitely large and nobody has an index — but they are what a first expedition can reach, and the seams between them are walkable in the order given.

### 3.1 The Landing — the shelf a far door opens on

**Where a far door from Earth arrives, always.** A dark, level expanse of what was farmland, under a stopped dusk, with a city burning on the horizon that has been burning since before human writing. The fires give the only light and they do not consume. The ground is cold.

It is **empty of anything that will fight**, and that is deliberate: this is the room in which a Bearer discovers that everything he knows about how a place works does not apply here, without dying while he does it. **Combat Tier equivalent: none.** What it costs him is time and saturation.

The furrows run to the horizon and there are no bodies in them — the people of this world were harvested separately from their ground.

### 3.2 The Mill — where the beasts come from

A stratum of black shelving structures the size of mountain ranges, and this is the one that explains the Gates. **Beasts are here in their millions, held, sorted, and marked** — every creature the BGM bestiary has ever catalogued and thousands it has not, each with a **brand seared into its hide** at the shoulder or the flank.

**The brands are shelf-marks.** They are how the process indexes its stock, and this is the authored answer to `200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 9's **first planted clue-line** — the healed weapon-wound and the unrecognised brand on an alpha, matched later to a beast pulled from a Gate on the other side of the planet. Identical brands mean *the same shelf*, not the same Gate.

Things here are **awake and not held**: the sorting is incomplete and always has been. **Combat Tier equivalent: B through S, encountered singly or in the hundreds**, at whatever the individual creature actually is. A Runtime authors each encounter before running it.

### 3.3 The Ninth Quiet — a world that nearly passed

A stratum of terraced stone cities under a white stopped noon, structurally intact, with no fires and no ruin. **This world did not lose a war. It ran out of time.**

Its people are gone. Its libraries are not, and they are legible — the script is unrelated to anything on Earth, but the diagrams are not, and some of them are of **Gates, punctures, and a curve**. Somebody here fit the same chart the Bible's second clue-line describes, and got further with it.

**Combat Tier equivalent: none, natively.** The danger here is that other things come to it — it is the most habitable stratum anyone has found and it is therefore contested.

### 3.4 The Weir — the seam that does not hold

A stratum where the storage has failed: three or four slices have collapsed into each other, and the result is a churn of incompatible geographies, mixed biospheres, and weather from four dead worlds arriving at once. **Nothing here is stable and the ground is not reliable.**

It is also the fastest route between the deep strata and the only place where things from different shelves meet and breed. **Combat Tier equivalent: S and above, unpredictably**, and the profile's Rank ladder genuinely runs out — a Runtime authoring a Weir encounter states its magnitude in plain terms rather than reaching for a Rank that does not describe it.

### 3.5 The Long Shelf — where the candidates are

**Deep, and the only stratum with a society on it.** A single valley under a stopped red evening, unremarkable except that it has been settled: shelters, cultivation of a sort, paths worn by feet over an unimaginable span. **This is where the failed champions live**, and Section 4 is about them.

# 4. The Failed Champions

**The Assay forges one candidate per world** (`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 9). When a world fails, it is harvested — and its candidate is harvested with it, because the candidate is part of the biomass.

**Most died. Some did not, and the reason is a defect in the process rather than mercy.** The System attached to a candidate is instrumentation with no authored failure state for *the exam ended*. When a world is harvested the channel is not closed; it simply stops receiving new instructions. **The System keeps running.** It keeps the workpiece alive, because a dead workpiece is a failed run, and it has not been told the run is over.

They have therefore been alive for a very long time, in a place with no exit, still levelling on whatever they can find to kill.

**Three states, and the difference matters more than anything else in this file.**

- **The Standing.** Still coherent, still themselves, still pursuing something. The oldest are older than human agriculture. They are the only things here that can be negotiated with.
- **The Continued.** Their instrumentation kept forging them past any purpose it was given, for tens of thousands of years, and what it made is not a person and cannot be talked to in any ordinary sense. They are not hostile. They are **finished**, in the way a tool is finished. **Combat Tier equivalent: beyond the ladder.** A Runtime never resolves a fight with one as a fight.
- **The Spent.** Alive, uninterested, sitting where they sat down. Some will answer questions. None will help, and none will move.

## 4.1 What the Standing want, and why it is a problem

Four positions are authored. They are not factions with banners — the population is small and old — but they are the four answers anyone here has arrived at.

| Position | What they want | What it means for the Bearer |
|---|---|---|
| **Out** | Through any door, to anywhere, including a living world. They have been waiting for a door since before there were doors. | **A far door is a way out for them.** Section 18.13's two-way rule is not a mechanical curiosity; it is this. |
| **Stop it** | End the process. Some have worked at this for millennia and one of them has a plan she has never been able to test. | They will help him, up to the point where helping him diverges from stopping it. |
| **Reach the next one** | Find the current candidate and change his outcome — warn, teach, arm, or hijack. **They have been trying for a long time and he is the first they have been able to touch.** | They came looking for him before he came looking for them. |
| **Let it finish** | A world that **passes** is *"spared and absorbed into the Assay's network"* — which means it becomes part of the machinery that does this. **A candidate who wins is not a rescuer; he is a recruit.** | The strongest moral pressure in the campaign: some of them will try to stop him from succeeding, and their reasoning is sound. |

**That last row is the point of this whole file.** The Bearer has spent a hundred levels assuming that winning is good. There are people here who have watched worlds win.

**All four positions now have a face, and that is deliberate (1.109).** Through the first authoring only *Stop it* and *Out* did, which left the two that matter most to a campaign — the one that exists to **help him** and the one that exists to **stop him** — as paragraphs with nobody standing in them. **Stop it** is Sevrin. **Out** is Ilith. **Reach the next one** is Aumry. **Let it finish** is Orenne. Tolo holds none of them on purpose, and Section 4.2 says why that is itself an answer.

**The word *teach* in the third row is now a mechanism (1.109).** `206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 7.1 authors a fifth acquisition route — instruction passed directly between System-bearers, with no instructional item — and **the Standing are the only beings in existence who can use it.** Section 2 puts one System on Earth and Section 13.1 gives an ordinary awakened nothing underneath their Rank to hand over; these are the others, and their instrumentation has run without a teacher for tens of thousands of years.

**What a given champion can teach is authored on that champion**, on this file's ordinary Section 2 discipline: a Runtime that reaches an unauthored technique stops and asks the owner rather than inventing one. **Nothing is taught for free and nothing is taught quickly** — the route runs on Section 7.1's existing three-qualifying-scene threshold, so instruction is a relationship across expeditions rather than a transaction, and each of the four positions above will price it differently. **Stop it** will teach him gladly and stop the moment teaching him diverges from stopping the process. **Reach the next one** has been waiting to do exactly this. **Out** will teach him anything at all, for a door. And **Let it finish** will not teach him, because making the candidate stronger is the thing they are trying to prevent.

**A technique learned this way is the only kind he can pass on.** Section 7.1 records it `transmissible`, and everything else he holds came off a drop table as an inscription he was never shown. What he carries back from the Long Shelf is therefore not loot — Section 18.13.1 still pays him nothing here — but it is the first thing he has ever been able to give another awakened without handing over an object.

**And killing one takes it instead, which is the other half of the same authoring (1.111).** `206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 7.6 now absorbs **fully** from a Section 13.1.1 System-bearer the Bearer personally kills: every skill at **the victim's own mastery level**, their Stat Passives as a Rank floor, and their class abilities without the class. **It reads the same authored entries the taught route reads, and it stops in the same place** — a champion's skill enters at the Rank authored on that champion, and a Runtime reaching an unauthored technique stops and asks the owner rather than inventing one. Section 7.5's ceiling still binds, so nothing here hands him a Rank above `System Rank + 1`.

**This makes teaching the worse trade wherever both are available, and that is a live design cost rather than a flourish.** Instruction is three qualifying scenes and arrives at Novice; a kill is instant and arrives at Master. **Every figure in Section 4.2 is smaller than the Bearer and none carries an equipment line** — the file says so, and it was written when the only thing a corpse could yield was one technique. *Aumry, who taught nine* is now worth more dead than alive on the arithmetic alone, which inverts the entire reason Section 4.1 gives for authoring the **Reach the next one** position. Raised as `F-065` in `engine/004_DESIGN_FLAGS.md`; **this file does not resolve it**, and a Runtime plays the figures as authored until it is ruled.

## 4.2 The named figures

Authored on the Decision 091 character model (want, fear, secret, voice, agenda) like every other Gatefall NPC. **Entity identifiers are not minted here** — that is campaign canon and belongs to a play session under save discipline.

### How these sheets are sized (1.111)

**A champion's Stats are derived from the Bearer's, and the derivation is a rule rather than five pinned numbers.** Every figure below carries a **base-Stat sum between 85% and 90% of the Bearer's own base-Stat sum at the moment of first contact**, fixed permanently from that moment and never re-derived afterward. What is *not* fixed by the rule is the distribution: **each champion spends that budget on their own axis**, which is why Ilith is faster than he is and Aumry cannot lift anything.

**The budget is a sum, never a per-Stat scale, and that is the whole design.** A flat 85-90% applied Stat by Stat produces five figures who are the same person at slightly different sizes, and it would delete the two characterisations this file spends the most words on - *the frailest thing in this file and the most dangerous* and *the worst fighter here and the most valuable person in it.* Spending one budget five different ways keeps them distinct and makes each of them a genuine problem at something: **three of the five exceed the Bearer outright in the Stat they were built around.**

**Two bounds hold and neither is negotiable.** No figure's `maximum_health` may exceed the Bearer's - the owner rejected inflation as a fix, and a bigger pool is exactly the fix that was rejected. And `maximum_health = 4 x Vitality`, `maximum_mana = 2 x Intelligence`, on Section 6.1's Bearer formula, because Section 13.1.1 says a bearer resolves as a bearer whoever they are.

**Why first contact and not the current anchor.** A sheet pinned to a live campaign figure goes stale the moment play moves - this file already demonstrated it, carrying *"the Bearer's 2,826"* and *"10,620"* for several levels after both had changed. Fixing the sheet at first contact means a champion is sized once, by the encounter that produced them, and a Runtime never recomputes a person because the protagonist levelled.

### What each of them knows (1.111)

**Every figure below is a Section 13.1.1 bearer and carries a full technique set, because a bearer with three named tricks is a Rank in disguise.** Each of the five holds **twenty named techniques**, none of them shared with the Bearer and none shared with each other: they came up on different worlds, under different Systems, against different exams. **The sets are the characterisation.** What a person spent four thousand years refining is a more specific statement about them than any of their Stats.

**Their five Stat Passives are the exception and are not listed.** Section 4.4's passives are derived from base Stat on that section's threshold table, every champion's Stats stand far past the base-80 rung, and so every one of them holds all five at the top of the ladder. That is universal to bearers rather than a fact about anybody, and restating it five times would be noise.

**Ranks are authored here because Section 7.6.2 reads them.** A bearer carries no Rank of their own, but a technique does, and Succession enters a taken skill at the Rank authored on that champion. A technique with no authored Rank is the case where a Runtime **stops and asks the owner** - so every row below carries one, and nothing about these figures is left for a Runtime to invent at the body.

### Sevrin, who held the last bridge — Standing, position **Stop it**

- **Want:** to end the process, and to do it before the next world is cut.
- **Fear:** that the plan she has carried for four thousand years is wrong, and that she will never find out.
- **Secret:** she has been to the Weir's deepest seam and seen what is on the other side of it. She will not say what, and it is not the Adjudicator.
- **Voice:** short sentences. Long pauses. She asks a question and then waits past the point of comfort for the answer.
- **Agenda:** she has been mapping seams toward something for longer than human history and is roughly two-thirds done.

**She is a System-bearer and resolves as one** (`206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 13.1.1). No Rank, no Rank Health, no ledger entry — a sheet, because the thing in her works the way the thing in him does.

```yaml
stats:      { strength: 2150, agility: 2200, vitality: 2300, perception: 2650, intelligence: 2700 }
maximum_health: 9200      # 4 x Vitality, the Section 6.1 Bearer formula
maximum_mana:   5400      # 2 x Intelligence
equipment: none           # no shop, no artificer, no cores worth the name in four thousand years
mastery:   Master on everything she still uses; she stopped acquiring and started refining
```

**Read those numbers against his and the point is immediate: she is smaller.** A smaller pool, no equipment line at all, and no elixir, rune, fusion or catalogue behind her — four thousand years of scraps off the Mill against thirty-three days of S-Rank instant dungeons. **She is not a bigger monster and she is not meant to be one.** A single clean Rupture kills her. She has had forty centuries to make sure it is not clean.

**Her axis is denial, and it is the one thing he has no answer to.** She held a bridge. What she has spent an unimaginable span perfecting is **closing a way through** — a seam, a rift, a step, a door. Against a Bearer whose entire survivability is that he can leave any room he is standing in, a woman who can make a rift refuse to open is not a damage problem.

- **She can bar an anchored step.** Section 18.8 calls it unrestricted and unbound by instance walls, and it is — against the world. It is not unbound against her.
- **She does not need to out-damage him and does not try.** On the far side Section 2's drain is 10% of maximum Mana an hour with no recovery, and the way home is a step she can close. **Keeping him somewhere is the lethal outcome, and no blow needs to land for it.**
- **Both of them can end an exchange with one connection**, hers included, and a Runtime softens that in neither direction (Section 13.1.1).

**What she can teach, and it is exactly the thing that makes her dangerous.** The seam-closing technique itself — the counter to the apparatus that has made him unkillable, in the hands of the only person who has ever built one. **She will teach it gladly, and she is position `Stop it`**, which means she teaches him right up to the point where teaching him stops serving the end of the process, and not one scene further. The complete authored entry is owed at ratification under `206_WORLD_RULE_PROFILE.md` Section 7.1's taught route; **what it is called, costs, and does is an owner ruling at that moment**, and this file does not pre-empt it.

**What Sevrin holds — twenty techniques, every one of them about closing a way through.** She stopped acquiring and started refining, and the set shows it: there is nothing here that kills quickly and nothing here he can walk away from.

| Technique | Rank | Mana | Effect |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Seamseal** | S | 30 | Closes one seam, rift, step destination or door for the scene. A rift-step targeting it fails and refunds nothing. |
| **Bridgehold** | S | 25 | Declares a line. No creature crosses it without winning effective Strength against her effective Intelligence. |

### The price, stated as a number

**The first killed champion buys twenty techniques and forecloses eighty.** Profile Section 7.6.2 takes that champion's whole set at their own mastery — and **no remaining champion teaches a candidate who has killed one.** Aumry's position closes outright and he is the only one who was teaching freely; Sevrin's was always conditional and the condition is now failed; Orenne never would; Ilith trades rather than teaches and her price was always a door.

| | Route 5, uninterrupted | Route 6, first kill |
|---|---|---|
| Techniques reachable | **100**, across five sets, three qualifying scenes each | **20**, instantly, at the victim's mastery |
| Techniques foreclosed | none | **80** |
| Compounding | teaching credits the teacher (Profile Section 7.1) | none; a taken technique is `transmissible: false` forever |

**That is the whole of the price and it is deliberately not a punishment.** No authority pursues him, no bounty exists, nothing is confiscated, and **Section 18.13.1 still pays him nothing here** — a killed champion drops no core, no rune and no gear, because none of them has an equipment line and the far side is *reachable, not profitable.* What he loses is the only source in existence of a thing he cannot get anywhere else.

**A Runtime never assembles them into a response.** Section 4.1 is four positions that disagree with each other about everything, and the disagreement is the point — **they do not organise, they do not hunt as a group, and nothing here authors a party.** Each of the five acts on their own line, and the most dangerous of those lines does not involve a blow being struck.

# 5. What Comes Back

**Matter.** Section 18.13 already governs it: real, unranked, unappraisable, with no chain of custody in either jurisdiction — the widest Section 19.1 exposure surface in the profile. Cores and crystals from the Mill are indistinguishable from Gate loot except that no Gate is on record producing them.

**Information**, which is worth more and is harder to use. Nothing the Standing say can be cited to a regulator, and the Ninth Quiet's diagrams are the only physical evidence in the setting that the curve is a designed schedule rather than a phenomenon.

**And people, which is the thing the campaign should be nervous about.** A far door is two-way and the Standing know it. **Whether a champion who crosses to Earth can remain there is deliberately unauthored** — it is a campaign question with a real answer the owner will need to give, and it is the single most consequential thing in this file.
