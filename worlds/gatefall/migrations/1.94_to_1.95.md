## What 1.95 changes

**No Rank, Rank Health, XP value, threshold, damage formula, break timer, population formula, archetype, loot table, price, title, class or reward moves.** All four changes are about **when** and **how often**, not about what anything is worth.

### 1. Adoption opens at tier IV, not III

The world has run **33 in-fiction days** under a profile whose own header called the break rate *"at an all-time high and still climbing"* and whose Section 9.2 implemented it as a constant. Run the ramp's arithmetic across that span - roughly 1.1 breaks a day at Chicago's authored take rates, about +1.2 credit a day - and the accrued credit is comfortably past one rung and close to two. **Starting the ramp at III would make its first act a formal declaration that nothing happened while the Bible said something was happening the whole time.**

**Nothing is recomputed to get there.** No Gate already rolled changes Rank, no break is re-settled, no anomaly is re-rolled. The initial value of a forward-looking counter is set to where the elapsed clock already put it, which is not the same operation as replaying history.

### 2. The forward ramp goes from two rungs to four

Tiers **IV, V, VI, VII** replace the old IV and V. **The endpoint is unchanged** - tier VII carries exactly the old tier V distribution, day-count and anomaly band - and the two new rungs are interpolated between. Three steps to the shoulder was a cliff: the world's character changed twice in the life of a campaign, each time by a large jump, with nothing in between to notice.

**Tier IV deliberately does not raise the Gate count.** It stays at Chicago 1d4+3 and moves the *composition* - A-Rank 2.5% to 3.5%, S-Rank 0.5% to 1%, anomaly band 93 to 91. An A-Rank Gate goes from one a week to one every four days and an S-Rank from one a month to one a fortnight, with no change to how busy the board looks. That is the tier the trade argues about rather than reports.

| | 1.94 | 1.95 |
|---|---|---|
| Tier IV | 2026-09-07 (14 days) | **on adoption** |
| Tier V | 2026-09-14 | **2026-08-29** (5 days) |
| Tier VI | - | **2026-09-02** (4 more) |
| Tier VII | - | **2026-09-05** (3 more) |
| Ramp's end | 2026-09-18 | **2026-09-07** (2 more) |

**The credit threshold falls from 20 to 6.** Credit values are unchanged; against rungs measured in days rather than months a 20-credit gate took seventeen days to fire and the pressure route was decorative again. At about +1.2 credit a day, 6 crosses in roughly five days - the same order as the clock rungs, which is what "the schedule leads and pressure pulls it forward" is supposed to mean.

### 3. New Section 9.5.1 - the Bearer's own anomaly band

A Gate **the Bearer personally enters** rolls the wider of the world's tier band and his own, read from **System Rank**: no uplift below B, then 88-00 at B, **80-00 at A**, **70-00 at S**.

`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 9 authors this directly - the rising break rate *"is, among other things, an audition mill"*, the process *"engineers auditions - Gates seeded to mislabel, red-gate anomalies"*, and it *"keeps steering him into exactly the ordeals that make him stronger."* A candidate the process has invested in does not meet the world's average Gate.

**It is invisible.** Another crew clearing the same posting rolls the world band. What accumulates is a pattern in his own record - a hunter whose jobs go wrong far more often than the trade's do - which is a Section 19.1 exposure surface as well as a clue-line.

**It reads System Rank where Section 9.12 reads Combat Tier, and the split is deliberate.** Section 9.12 is *sizing content* and needs the honest measure of what can test him. This clause is not sizing anything; it is how much attention the process is paying, and the process watches a candidate's progression, which is the ladder System Rank tracks.

**It does not touch Section 9.2's ramp, which still never reads the Bearer.** The ramp governs what the world produces, and keying that to him would make levelling worsen the world for everyone. This governs what he walks into, where the incentive points the other way.

### 4. New Section 8.4.7 - the crucible floor

Sections 8.4.2 and 8.4.3 state what a quest **is** and neither states what happens when the world fails to produce one; through 1.94 the answer was nothing. The Bible says otherwise, in a sentence that is a rule: *"The daily quest and its lethal penalty zone are the process refusing to let its investment coast - **it will not waste a candidate on a slow week.**"*

`quiet_days` counts consecutive in-fiction days closed with no Section 7.4-qualifying contested scene, resetting to 0 the instant one resolves. At **2** the next tick owes a guaranteed Section 9.12 audition on his route; at **3**, also a sited Section 8.4.2-qualifying crisis within reach; at **4 or more**, the audition rolls Section 9.6 twice and takes the worse result.

**It creates conditions, never a quest.** Criteria 2 and 4 still have to hold, the Runtime still may not reveal an unconfirmed Rank or an NPC's intent to fit an offer, self-created danger still disqualifies, and a dry audit is still recorded as dry under Section 8.4.6's telemetry. What it removes is the case where the criteria were never *tested* because the world put nothing in front of them.

**It is not farmable**, because what it pays out is danger rather than reward. **A day spent in a Section 17 instance beneath his tier is a quiet day** - clearing content that cannot contest him is exactly the coasting the section interrupts, which is the finding `F-046` was raised on.

## What did not change

**Section 9.2's ramp still never reads the Bearer**, and Section 9.5.1 is explicitly fenced off from it. **Section 9.12's keying, share table, on-route restriction and legal-minimum switch-off are untouched**, as is its always-roll anomaly from 1.94. Tier III remains the pre-ramp constant and tiers I and II are unchanged. The credit table's values, Section 9.6's table, the tier-VII confluence, Section 9.11, and the whole of 1.92 are unchanged.

## Migration procedure

**1. Advance `profile_version` to 1.95** on the campaign's live character sheet, and re-pin `world_rule_profile:` in `090_CAMPAIGN_STARTUP.md`.

**2. Set `escalation_tier` to IV** on `110_WORLD_LEDGER.md`, and `escalation_credit` to 0. Where the 1.93 adoption has not yet been played, initialise directly to IV rather than to III and then advancing - the two are the same end state and one write is honest about why.

**3. Initialise `quiet_days`.** Count back from the campaign's current anchor to the most recent Section 7.4-qualifying contested scene and record the number of closed in-fiction days since. **This is a read of the existing record, not a judgement**: the campaign's own settlement history classifies every scene, and `EVT-000527`, `EVT-000560`, `EVT-000574`, `EVT-000599`, `EVT-000667` and `EVT-000676` are all on record as non-qualifying. If the count already stands at 2 or more, the floor owes its guarantee on the **next** tick, not retroactively.

**4. The schedule's dates are absolute.** Tier V lands no later than 2026-08-29 whether the campaign adopts 1.95 today or later; retiming does not restart the clock.

**5. Nothing else moves, and nothing is recomputed.** No Gate already rolled changes, no anomaly already resolved is re-rolled under Section 9.5.1's wider band, no break already settled is credited, and no past quiet stretch is charged against the floor. All four changes are prospective from adoption.

**6. A capture restored across this edge needs steps 1, 2 and 3 and no other treatment.**

**Immutable checkpoints are byte-unchanged.** Checkpoint 0120 and earlier keep what they were captured under.
