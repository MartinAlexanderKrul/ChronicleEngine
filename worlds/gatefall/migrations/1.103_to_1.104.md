## What 1.104 changes

**Found by play reaching authored content that had no mechanic under it.** `270_THE_FAR_SIDE.md` Section 2 has said since it was instantiated that **an awakened human accumulates a day of ordinary Gate-work's saturation roughly every hour**, and called that *the clock on every expedition*. Section 18.13.1 restated it. Neither said what the clock does to a character sheet. The Bearer opened his far door, spent about two hours in the Stacks, and returned to a note on `100_CHARACTER_SHEET.md` recording accrued exposure with **no tracked field to carry it and no rule to consult** - the flagged, unresolved item this edge answers.

### The problem the base texts left open

`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md` Section 5 grounds the gray sleep in an **absence**: an unawakened body has *"no rank, no pool, nothing to burn it off,"* so ambient mana settles and accumulates one-directionally. That mechanism explains why civilians near a break contract it and **why no hunter ever has** - an awakened body sheds. The far side's line then applies the condition to a body that has all three, and the two passages cannot both be read literally without a statement of what changed.

**1.104 resolves it by naming the pool as the organ.** An awakened body sheds absorbed mana **by spending Mana**; that is the capacity a civilian lacks. Ordinary Gate-work never registers because the shedding keeps pace. The far side does not take his Mana - his body spends it, continuously and involuntarily, to stay unsaturated. The clock is therefore read off a resource already on the sheet, and the saturation figure in `270` is what he is **offsetting**, never a quantity anyone tracks.

### 1. Section 5.2.1 - draining environments

**New subsection.** A draining environment consumes Mana rather than slowing its return, and **one is authored: the far side.** No Gate, Scar, anomaly, penalty zone, or trial drains, and a Runtime never rules that one does.

On the far side:

- **Section 5.2 recovery does not accrue at all** - neither mode applies, **Conduit adds nothing**, the **Wellspring** title does not reach it, and there is no resting rate, because nothing in the Stacks permits rest. Section 18.2.2 already suspends elapsed-time recovery inside a trial; this is the same construction for a different reason.
- **The pool falls by 10% of maximum Mana per hour**, settled from elapsed fictional time exactly as Section 5.2 settles a gain - one figure per narrated span. In Section 5.2's half-percent units the far side is `rate_units = -20`, and `current_mana` floors at zero.
- **A full pool is ten hours and no more.** A far door costs a flat 4,000 to open, so a Bearer arrives already down that much: the door is the largest item on the expedition's budget and it is spent before he gets there.

This is the authored reason the place cannot be lived in, and it is now arithmetic rather than assertion.

### 2. Section 5.3 - zero is not the bottom

The drain does not stop at an empty pool. **It continues against Health at 10% of maximum Health per hour.** Section 5.3's existing clauses bind throughout - **-1 modifier step on all actions**, and every skill whose cost exceeds a zero pool **unavailable**. That, rather than the Health loss, is what kills a Bearer in the Stacks: an empty pool in a place holding Combat Tier B-through-S things.

**The drain inflicts no injury by the Section 6.3 hit test** - it is not a hit and removes no single quantity. **Crossing below 25% of maximum Health while draining inflicts one injury of type `exhaustion`** (Rules Section 6.8), severity read by the Runtime. That injury **is** the gray sleep arriving in an awakened body: mana he could not shed has settled into tissue, which is precisely the civilian mechanism, reached by a man whose pool ran out rather than by one who never had a pool. It recovers on Section 6.3's clock and answers to the world's restorative alchemy (Section 12.5's off-shop note), never to a System consumable.

**Past that first injury the condition is unauthored.** No text sets what repeated or chronic exposure compounds into, and **a Runtime that reaches it stops and asks the owner** - Section 18.13.2's rule against inventing content against no evidence.

### 3. Section 12.5 - scaling effects degrade off-world

An earlier draft of this rule was defeated by the shop, and the defect is recorded because it is the reason this clause exists. **A Greater mana potion costs 480 g and restores Mana *to full*.** At a level-100 Bearer's pool that is one purchase per five hours of drain, against a crystal table where a single A-Rank crystal sells for 3,400 g. A drain the shop refills for a rounding error is not a hazard; it is a per-turn bookkeeping step that always resolves to *drink and continue*.

**The repair is a distinction the profile already drew.** Section 12.5 has always said Greater potions *restore to full, so they scale with the drinker's own maximum pools*, while *Lesser and Standard restore a flat amount regardless of the drinker's Stats*. That is the line between an effect that **reads his sheet** and one that **delivers a quantity** - and Section 18.13.1 already establishes the far side as where System evaluation stops, which is why nothing there carries a Rank and no drop table fills.

So: **a Greater healing or mana potion drunk on the far side functions as its Standard counterpart** - a flat 90 Health, a flat 40 Mana - and is consumed in full at its own price. Lesser and Standard potions are unaffected.

**The rule reaches purchased consumables only.** Section 3.2's level-up restoration and a claimed Status Recovery settle the Bearer's own progression rather than pricing the ground he stands on, and restore in full on the far side exactly as on Earth.

### 4. Section 18.13.1 and `270_THE_FAR_SIDE.md` - the mechanic made reachable

Through 1.103 the only statement of the far side's clock lived in `270`, which is **owner eyes only**. A Runtime adjudicating an expedition had to open the spoiler file to find a rule. **Section 18.13.1 now carries the mechanic** and `270` carries the fiction plus a pointer, so play never requires the owner-facing document. `270` also gains the narration constraint: the body reports it - cold, slowness, being spent without having done anything - and **the numbers stay out of the fiction** (Section 20.5), because the Bearer has no instrument there that reads them.

## What did not change

**No Rank, Rank Health, XP value, XP threshold, damage formula, Gate rule, escalation tier, anomaly band, break timer, population, loot table, price, skill, title, class grant, cap, or Mana cost moves.** Section 5.2's recovery rates, its deterministic settlement, its Conduit composition order, and the Frozen Gallery halving are byte-identical for every environment that is not the far side. Section 5.3's two clauses are unchanged and merely extended. Every potion's price and its effect **on Earth and in every Gate** are exactly what 1.103 authored. The far door's 4,000 cost, its one-stratum arrival, its closing condition, and every other Section 18.13.1 grant are untouched. **No field is added to any character sheet**, and no counter, tracker, or new Persistent Object is created anywhere.

## Migration procedure

**1. Advance `profile_version` to 1.104** on the campaign's live character sheet, and re-pin `world_rule_profile:` in `090_CAMPAIGN_STARTUP.md`.

**2. No stored state changes.** No pool, counter, injury, anchor, or Event record is read or written. The rules added here are **prospective**: they govern time spent on the far side from adoption forward.

**3. The one already-resolved far-side expedition is not recomputed.** `EVT-000781`-`EVT-000785` settled about two hours in the Stacks under 1.103, with Mana recovery credited at the `active` rate for that span. **That settlement stands.** 1.104 is a compatibility treatment and does not reach back into a resolved Event; the Bearer keeps the pools the chronicle records. Had it been applied retroactively the span would have cost roughly 1,835 Mana of drain instead of crediting 1,834 of recovery, but **no rule requires or permits replaying it**, and immutable checkpoints are byte-unchanged regardless.

**4. The character sheet's flagged gray-sleep note is now answered and should be retired at the next save.** The note at the Mana ledger records *"~2 days' worth of ordinary Gate-work mana saturation ... no tracked field exists for this yet; flagged here pending an owner ruling on where/how it's carried forward."* The ruling is this edge: **no field is carried forward, because the pool is the field.** Retiring the note is a canon write and belongs to a play session, not to this record.

**5. A capture restored across this edge needs step 1 only.** There is no state to recompute.

**Immutable checkpoints are byte-unchanged.** Every checkpoint captured under 1.103 or earlier keeps what it was captured under.
