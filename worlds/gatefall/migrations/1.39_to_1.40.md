## Compatibility treatment

**1.39 → 1.40 compatibility treatment.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon. Adoption requires no recomputation and no state migration.

The removed rule is Section 17's boss-kill close: an instant dungeon ran two hours, "closing at the two-hour mark or on the boss kill, whichever comes first." Under 1.40 the instance closes on the **two-hour mark** or **the Bearer's decision to leave**, and on nothing else. The boss kill settles its core, its boss-drop d100, its kill XP, and the Gate-clear milestone at the moment it lands, exactly as before, and the interior then holds for the remainder of the two hours.

**No completed instant dungeon is reopened, re-looted, re-timed, or recomputed.** Every clear resolved under an earlier profile closed correctly under the rule in force when it happened, and its settled drops, crystals, cores, XP, and milestones stand as recorded. In the Pendragon campaign that covers every instant-dungeon clear on record — the three E-Rank clears of 2026-08-02, the three of 2026-08-05, and the three D-Rank clears of 2026-08-09 — each of which ended on its boss kill as the rule then required. A Bearer does not get retroactive extraction time for a deposit that a boss kill cut short under the old rule, and none of those deposits is re-rolled or re-mined. This is the same no-retroactive-loot boundary the 1.23 → 1.24 record draws for completed instances, applied to the same class of resolved fiction.

**The most recent loss is not compensated.** Dungeon 1 of the 2026-08-09 D-Rank run (`EVT-000232`) rolled a 22-crystal deposit that was never extracted and was lost outright when the boss kill closed the instance around the Bearer. That is precisely the failure 1.40 exists to prevent, and it is precisely the kind of thing this edge does not undo: the loss is promoted canon, and the engine forbids a fix-it-later retcon of promoted canon. The crystals are not restored, not re-rolled, and not converted to gold or any other compensation. 1.40 is why it will not happen again, not a reversal of the time it did.

The rule is **prospective only**, applying to instances opened from adoption forward. Unopened keys would be unaffected as objects in any case: a key stores a Rank, a model, and its provenance, none of which this edge touches, and none of which encodes a close condition. In the Pendragon campaign no instant-dungeon key is banked at adoption — the Armory Key [D-Rank] was consumed opening Dungeon 1 (`EVT-000231`), Dungeon 2's key was bought and spent, and Dungeon 3's boss dropped a rune rather than a key (`EVT-000233`-`EVT-000235`) — so the first instance to run under 1.40 will open from a key not yet acquired.

Two clarifications carried in the same bump settle questions the pre-1.40 text left open, and neither transforms stored state. **Population is rolled once at opening and never respawns**, so the time remaining after a boss kill is time to mine, loot, and walk rather than time to farm — this was already implied by Section 9.7's one-time population formula and is now stated where the timer is authored, because extending the post-kill interior is the first rule that makes the question answerable. **Closing returns everyone inside to the world** rather than stranding them: a System instance keyed to the Bearer expels its occupants at its limit, as the Section 8.3 penalty zone already does at four hours, and Section 9.7's lost-inside rule is a public-Gate rule that does not reach an instant dungeon. No campaign has ever run an instance to its two-hour limit, so neither clarification contradicts a resolved outcome.

Record adoption as `EVT-000247`; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.39-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.40 at readiness.
