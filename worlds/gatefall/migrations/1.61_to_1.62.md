## What 1.62 changed

**1.61 authored the Flux Sight read for a Bearer meeting a monster, and then applied that cadence to everyone.** Player question the same day — *would it fire when I meet an awakened NPC too?* — and following it through exposed the gap: the C rung reads any perceived awakened, and 1.61's cadence was *full read at first perception in a scene*, extended from Section 14.6's designation-color rule. **Owen Callahan lives with the Bearer.** Under 1.61 every domestic scene opened with a combat-style stat block on his partner, restating a number that had not moved in days.

**Three rules follow, all ruled 2026-08-05.**

**1. A subject he already knows: first meeting, then on change (new Section 14.5.6).** The full read fires the first time he ever perceives someone, wherever that happens. Afterwards it renders only when a value has materially moved since his last read — Health or Mana away from where he last saw it, a changed Rank, a condition the pools carry. An unchanged subject renders nothing across any number of scenes.

**The passive is not narrowed and Section 4.4 is untouched** — Flux Sight remains always in effect and the Bearer always knows. What is bounded is when the System *says so*, which is the same principle already governing the designation color (*"not repeated each exchange"*) and a target's pools in 14.5.5 (*rendered in the exchange that changed them*), now carried to people the Bearer lives among.

**It is the more useful rule and not merely the quieter one.** A line that restates an unchanged number carries no information, and a reader who learns to skip it is exactly the reader a real change slips past. Under this rule a rendered line always carries news, and an injured friend coming through the door is the case it fires on.

**2. A populated room renders one room line, then individuals on engagement.** Where the Bearer perceives many awakened at once — a guild hall, a staging floor, a bar — the read emits a single line giving the room's shape, counts by Rank with each Rank's color, then a full individual read on anyone he engages, is approached by, singles out, or fights. The room line carries no pools, a count of strangers having no single pool to state. This is 1.61's grouping rule applied to people rather than monsters: **compress the undifferentiated, name what matters.**

**3. The bracketed head grammar is stated rather than left to pattern-matching (Section 14.5.3).** Every bracketed message shares `[SYSTEM] <EVENT NAME IN CAPS> — <subject>`, the subject omitted where there is none. What differs below the head now differs by a stated rule: **a fixed tuple renders inline on the head row**, `·`-separated in the 14.5.1 order, because the fields are always the same fields in the same order and naming them would be noise; **a variable field set renders as labelled rows**, because the reader cannot know which fields are present. A Flux Sight read is a fixed tuple and is one row; a quest carries objectives, rewards, deadlines and warnings in combinations that vary and is labelled rows. Neither shape is available to the other.

This settles a question 1.61 left answerable only by inspecting the existing quest and title blocks and inferring the pattern — the shapes were already consistent, and now the reason they are consistent is written down.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.61 → 1.62.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.62"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, maximum, Stat, level, XP, System Rank, Card Rank, skill, skill Rank, mastery level, counter, title, item, equipment, gold, cash, deadline, streak, quest, objective, or candidate is touched. Flux Sight's Rank is unchanged, and no NPC record gains a field — **"has he read this person before" is answered from the campaign's existing record of who the Bearer has met**, not from new stored state.

**3. The rules are prospective and apply from adoption forward.** No campaign owes a backfill of which subjects were previously read.

**4. Nothing retroactive.** No promoted Event is rewritten and no past exchange is re-resolved. As at 1.61, a Flux Sight read reveals information and settles nothing — it grants no modifier step, costs no Mana, and has never been an input to any resolution — so no roll, margin or damage figure moves.

**5. A first read after adoption is not a defect.** Where the record does not establish that the Bearer has previously read a given awakened, the first perception after adoption fires the full read and that subject is thereafter on the change cadence. This is the correct behaviour and needs no ruling per NPC.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.61-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.62 at readiness.
