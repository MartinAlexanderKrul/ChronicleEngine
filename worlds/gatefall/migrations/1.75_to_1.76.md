## What 1.76 changed

**A timestamp proved that time passed, not that the world ran.**

Profile 1.73 authored the daily world tick and gated `world_clock.last_ticked`, so a campaign whose clock falls behind its `campaign_time` is a readiness finding. That was the right remedy for the failure it addressed — ninety in-fiction days inside a city opening thirty-five Gates a week, with none of them ever reaching the Bearer.

It leaves one thing unproven. **A tick that advances the anchor and rolls nothing is byte-identical to a tick that rolled and found nothing.** The only artifact either produces is a moved timestamp, so the mechanism that proves the world runs cannot distinguish a world that ran from one that did not.

This is the third time this engine has met that shape:

- `F-002` — deadlines the clock reached with nothing settling them, twice, the second time after the remedy was in place
- `F-035` — supply sources defined in the Data Model, surfaced by a tool, and carrying zero live instances, so "nothing available" cost nothing to say
- the tick — an anchor that moves whether or not anything was rolled

New **Section 9.1.1** applies the remedy the other two already have. Decision 080's negative-assertion discipline, and the exact shape Section 7.5's supply sources use with their `advanced` / `available` pair: **an advance that yields nothing is recorded as having yielded nothing.**

Each tick appends one `world_ticks` entry to the campaign's world ledger, carrying the boundary it ran for and what each of the six steps produced — gates and their rolled Ranks, siting results, breaks, postings, agenda initiations. **`none` is a result and is written as one.** A day on which four Gates opened all sited elsewhere, nothing broke and nobody called is a real day and is recorded as such. What may never appear is a boundary with no entry, because that is what a world that did not run looks like.

## The daily quest is bound to the tick

Section 9.1 already said the tick runs *"at each in-fiction day boundary — 06:00, the same instant the daily quest issues."* That was descriptive. It is now binding, and ordered: **the tick runs, its entry is written, and only then does the daily quest issue.** A Runtime may not render the daily for a day whose tick has no entry. Section 12.5's Daily Premium rotation shares the instant and the ordering.

**The reason is the difference in failure rates, and it is worth stating plainly.** The daily quest is player-facing, arrives every morning, and has never been missed — twenty-six have issued on time. The tick is world-facing, owed to nobody in the scene, and is precisely the class of obligation this engine has repeatedly failed to meet. Coupling them makes skipping the tick cost something a player notices the same morning, rather than something discovered ninety days later by counting Gates that never arrived.

This is the `F-012` lesson inverted. There, a counter at its ceiling moved no visible number, so crediting it looked like nothing was happening and the delta went unwritten. Here, a reliable visible obligation is deliberately made to carry an invisible one.

## What did not change

**No roll, rate, distribution, band or siting result moves.** The tick's six steps are exactly as 1.73 authored them: Chicago `1d4+3`, Section 9.2 Rank frequencies, rarity rolled every time, Section 9.10 board timers, posting regeneration, agenda initiation. The `01-10` / `11-30` / `31-00` siting bands are untouched, and proximity remains an input rather than a veto.

**Nothing is back-dated.** 1.73 initialised `last_ticked` to its adoption anchor and declined to invent ninety days of world history; 1.76 does not revisit that. The first tick still falls **2026-08-20 06:00** and writes the first entry.

**No campaign owes a re-audit.** There are no prior ticks to record, because there have been none.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.75 → 1.76.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.76"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, Stat, level, XP, System Rank, Card Rank, skill, mastery, counter, title, item, gold, cash, quest, candidate, class, fragment, supply source or standing need is touched. No Gate, break, posting or agenda is rolled by this migration.

**3. Initialise `world_ticks` as an empty list** in the campaign's world ledger. Empty is the correct initial value: no tick has run. Do not synthesise entries for boundaries that predate the tick's existence — 1.73 already ruled that ninety days are not back-dated, and inventing them now would be authoring world history to satisfy a record format.

**4. `world_clock.last_ticked` is unchanged.** It keeps its meaning — the anchor through which the world has been run — and gains a companion rather than a replacement.

**5. The first tick under this profile writes the first entry**, before that day's daily quest issues.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.75-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.76 at readiness.
