## What 1.63 changed

**The System had two visual identities and no reason for either to be the one it was.** Player question, and the right one: *is the Flux Sight template the same as the quest templates and the other System messages?* It was not — and the answer was worse than a mismatch between two templates.

**A summoned panel was a framed window.** `╔══ ◈  S Y S T E M ══…`, content at interior column 6, bracketed Ranks, a fixed label vocabulary, a two-glyph status gutter, no truncation, columns aligned on their digits — Section 15.1, governing every panel identically.

**A fired notification was plain text wearing a `[SYSTEM]` prefix**, sharing none of it. Nothing in the fiction justifies the split. It is the same System, addressing the same Bearer, through the same interface, and it looked like two different things depending only on whether he had asked.

**The prefix was load-bearing in the wrong direction.** A prefix is cheap enough to attach to any shape at all, and that is exactly what happened: **four mutually incompatible message shapes** had accumulated beneath one label.

1. **A head with an inline tuple** — the Flux Sight read, level-up, skill mastery.
2. **A head with labelled rows** — the Daily, Urgent and Hidden quests.
3. **A head with an unlabelled record list** — the five Section 18 class panels.
4. **A head with nested indented sub-blocks** — the Title Assessment.

**1.62's Section 14.5.3 declared two of those a contract** and closed with *"neither shape is available to the other."* It described shapes 1 and 2 and was silent on 3 and 4 — a contract covering half its own subject matter, stated as though it covered all of it. That is the defect 1.63 actually repairs; the visual split is what made it visible.

**One render, two forms, and the line between them is what the System is doing.**

- **The window** — what the System **presents**: a read, an event, an offer, an assessment, a panel. Framed always, in the Section 15.1 frame, unchanged and identical. The top rule carries the event name, letter-spaced, where a panel carries `S Y S T E M`. A window with a subject puts it on the first content row: the top rule names *what happened*, the first row names *to whom*.
- **The ledger line** — what the System **counts** while an action resolves: a pool or XP moving, on the Bearer or on a target he acted upon. Unframed, but **indented to interior column 6** — the window's own content column — so the numbers read as System output with no rule above them.

**A window is an occasion and may cost three rows. A ledger line fires several times an exchange**, and framing each would put forty rules through a single dungeon fight, the fiction pushed apart by its own bookkeeping. **The frame marks what the System chose to say; the indent marks what it is merely keeping.**

**A read is one window however many subjects it covers** — everything perceived in the same instant shares a frame, its subjects rendered as columns. One rule for a room, not one rule per creature. The window fires once at first perception; the fight that follows is ledger lines, because arithmetic on a subject already introduced is counting rather than presenting.

**Two long-standing defects fall out of conforming every site rather than merely restating the contract.**

- **The Daily quest rendered `Objectives:` and `Rewards:` where Urgent and Hidden rendered `Objective:` and `Reward:`** — the same field under two names on the same surface, since 1.0. Now one name. A label inside a window is also **never punctuated with a colon**: the column does the work the colon used to, exactly as `Level`, `Card Rank` and `Unspent Points` already did in Section 15.2.
- **The Section 18 class panels rendered Rank as a bare letter** — `Gravecut · D · Field`, `180 E · 44 D` — against Section 15.1's own *"a bare letter is never rendered."* Now bracketed in the panels and in the prose that quotes them.

**Section 15.1's frame rule is corrected against its own templates.** It stated that *no* rule is closed on the right, while every normative template in Sections 15.2–15.4 closed its inner section rules on `╢` and left the top and bottom rules open — so the stated rule and the templates it governs disagreed on every panel in the profile. **The templates are what a Runtime actually copies, so the templates win**: only an inner section rule closes, and it closes on `╢`. The asymmetry earns its keep — a closed rule reads as a divider *between* two things, an open rule as the edge *of* something.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.62 → 1.63.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.63"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, maximum, Stat, level, XP, System Rank, Card Rank, skill, skill Rank, mastery level, counter, title, item, equipment, gold, cash, deadline, streak, quest, objective, or candidate is touched. **This edge is how the System renders, not what it knows, grants, or costs.** No Flux Sight rung, trigger tier, quest criterion, capacity ladder, reward figure, or class mechanic moves.

**3. The render is prospective and applies from adoption forward.** No campaign owes a re-render of past exchanges, and **no promoted Event, checkpoint, or chronicle description is rewritten to the new frame.** A historical Event quoting a `[SYSTEM]` line is a faithful record of what was rendered under the profile then in force, and it stays exactly as promoted.

**4. Nothing retroactive.** No past exchange is re-resolved. A notification's shape has never been an input to any resolution — it grants no modifier step, costs no Mana, and settles nothing — so no roll, margin, damage figure, or reward moves.

**5. A quest's stored record is unaffected by the label correction.** `Objectives`/`Rewards` were rendering labels, never stored field names: canonical `system_state.non_daily_quests` and the daily quest record already store `objective counters`, `reward Rank and XP` and their deadlines under their own schema, which this edge does not touch. Any campaign whose live state happens to carry the old rendering in prose may correct it as an ordinary read-back repair, not as a migration step.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.62-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.63 at readiness.
