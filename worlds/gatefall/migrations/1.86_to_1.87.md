## What 1.87 changes, and why nobody noticed it was missing

**Section 6.2 could resolve a hit and could not resolve a held hand.** It builds a damage figure from the attacker's magnitude and the die, removes it from Health, and hands heavy results to Section 6.3's injury taxonomy. Section 6.5 states that death is final. Between those three there was no term at all for an attacker doing less than he could on purpose. A grep of the whole profile for `subdue`, `non-lethal`, `unconscious`, and `knock out` returned **nothing**.

**Sections 4.0 and 4.1 already promised what 6.2 could not deliver.** Intent precedes method; the die resolves a *declared* action; Section 4.0 forbids rejecting a method for being unanticipated. A player declaring *I am pulling this* was making a legitimate declaration under sections already in force - and it evaporated between the declaration and the resolution, because the damage step had no slot to put it in. **The hole was in the resolution surface, not in the permission.**

**Why ninety days of play never surfaced it.** Effectively every physical exchange in this world's played history has been against Gate creatures, where finality is the point and nobody wants a beast subdued. The single prior civilian case - a training strike against Owen Callahan at `EVT-000116` - was resolved narratively at the striker's Rank magnitude and the general rule was never asked for. **The gap becomes visible on the first occasion the target is a person the protagonist does not want dead**, which in this campaign arrived the same night his effective Strength reached 644 and he was looking at an unarmed man who read as no Rank at all.

**What made it urgent rather than tidy.** At that magnitude the profile's own arithmetic produced an absurd result in the *gentlest* direction available: the least harmful object the Bearer could deliver, a mana crystal at Section 7.3's improvised chassis, still computed **322 a projectile** against a target with no Rank Health whatsoever. There was no dial anywhere on the sheet, and the protagonist is by background a **krav maga instructor** - a discipline whose entire civilian curriculum is control and restraint.

## The rule

> **An attacker may choose to do less than he can, and a person put down by that choice gets up again.**

New **Section 6.2.1**. Before the roll, an attacker may declare a strike **pulled** - free, no action, no Mana, available on any attack he controls (unarmed, wielded, skill, or launched object), and never declarable after a result is known. Damage then resolves at **x0.25** through the ordinary Section 6.2 formula, with the result multiplier, every reduction, and Section 6.3's injury test all applying to the reduced figure exactly as they apply to any other. It is a real strike that hurts less, not a parallel resolution system.

**The payoff is the only genuinely new mechanic:** a target reduced to **0 Health by pulled damage alone is incapacitated rather than killed** - unconscious or unable to continue - and **stabilises on its own**. It takes whatever injury Section 6.3 assigns and heals on that clock. Section 6.5 is not engaged.

**The bright line attaches to the source of the damage, never to the attacker's hopes.** Mixed sources do not average. If **any** unpulled damage contributed to reaching 0, the target reached 0 by ordinary damage and Section 6.5 governs. A pulled opening followed by a full follow-through is a kill, and the section says so in those words.

**Four things it deliberately does not do.** It does not make a high-magnitude attacker safe to be hit by - a quarter of a very large number remains large, and a Bearer far above his target can pull and still empty the pool in one exchange, which is the sought outcome but is not gentleness. It grants no accuracy, no defence, and no modifier step; restraint is not finesse. It is unavailable against monsters, where Section 6.5 continues to govern without exception. And it settles no Section 7.4 counter differently.

## What did not change

**No Rank ladder, baseline, rung, category, dividend, XP threshold, price, timer, population, band or distribution moves.** Section 6.2's formulas are untouched for every unpulled attack; 6.2.1 is a declared modifier on top of them, not a rewrite. Section 6.3's injury thresholds, severities and healing clocks are untouched and now serve pulled strikes on identical terms. **Section 6.5's finality is untouched** - this migration adds a way to avoid engaging it, never a way to survive it.

**No skill, title, item, chassis or multiplier gains or loses anything.** Section 7.3's improvised chassis (`weapon power 0`, `x0.5`) is unchanged; the crystal that computed 322 still computes 322 unpulled, and 81 pulled.

## Migration procedure

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.87"`.**

**2. Nothing is granted by migrating.** Pulling is a declaration available to every attacker in the world, including every NPC and every hunter, from adoption forward. It is not a skill, is not learned, is not tracked, and enters no ledger.

**3. No stored value moves.** No level, XP, Stat, Rank, mastery level, `mastery_progress`, `successful_uses`, `qualifying_scenes_total`, `rank_ascensions`, `dividend_level`, `scope_floor`, `magnitude_floor`, title, item, item line, gold, cash, quest, class, tracked posting, district pressure, supply source or standing need value is touched.

**4. No past Event, roll, damage figure, kill, clear or outcome is altered.** Every historical resolution was an unpulled strike by construction, because the declaration did not exist to be made. **Nothing is retroactively survivable** - no prior death is reopened, reclassified, or made available for reconsideration, and no prior kill becomes an incapacitation.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor. Immutable Profile 1.86-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.87 at readiness.

## What this does not close

**It does not author defender-side or contested restraint.** Grapples, holds, pins and disarms resolved as opposed actions between two parties are a different mechanism and remain unauthored; Overpower's own restraint clauses are untouched and continue to govern what they already governed. If a player asks to hold someone rather than hit them softly, that is a new ruling.

**It does not author a monster exception.** A Gate creature brought to 0 by pulled damage is simply a creature that took less damage; nothing captures it, tames it, or renders it unconscious. Whether anything in this world can be taken alive from a Gate is untouched and unanswered.

**It does not author what incapacitation costs the target beyond the injury.** How long a person stays down, and whether they remember what put them there, is fiction resolved per scene rather than a tracked state - deliberately, because a duration table is exactly the kind of value that goes stale in a ledger nobody reads.

**It does not resolve `F-042`'s wider observation** that a declaration can be legitimate under Sections 4.0-4.2 and still have nowhere to land in a resolution section. This is one instance repaired; whether other declared intents fall through the same seam is not surveyed here.
