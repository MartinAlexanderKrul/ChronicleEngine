## What 1.78 changed

1.77 gave the tick a demand side and a non-Gate event table. Both operate at the scale of a day — a posting taken, a market shift, a guild moving. **Nothing in the engine could produce a change that outlived the week it happened in**, and a world that resets every morning is scenery rather than a setting.

### 1. Breaks are cumulative, and a city can lose ground

Section 9.8 answers **one** break: BGM issues an emergency contract, the nearest guild strike team takes it, the release is contained. It answers the second break in the same district identically, and the third, and it never asks what happens when the answer stops being enough.

The campaign already recorded the pattern without modelling it — the world ledger's own note on *"two E-Rank breaks in the Red Line Corridor inside eleven days… not a coincidence so much as a measurement of how thin the freelance pool is at the bottom of the ladder."* That is a district consuming response capacity faster than it recovers, observed and then discarded.

**New Section 9.1.3** makes it state. Each district carries `pressure`: **+1** a contained break, **+2** at C-Rank or above, **+3** a late response or a second break inside seven days, **−1** per clear week, floor 0. Thresholds are deterministic on the Section 9.10 pattern — no roll, no Runtime discretion:

```text
     0-3    normal                the trade absorbs it
     4-6    strained              rates rise, benches thin, contest penalty
     7-9    cordon                BGM closes blocks; the news carries it
     10+    containment failure   the district is OVERRUN
```

**A joint raid is what the city mounts to stop a failure, not to undo one.** BGM-coordinated, Ironline, Horizon, Karlov and the Coalition contributing elements under one command, **state-directed at B-Rank saturation or above** on the Section 9.4 precedent. Mounted while the cordon still holds it can break the entrenchment and bring pressure down. It has a date, a roster, a command structure, casualties and a settlement; it is a campaign event and never a Section 9.10 posting.

**Mounted too late, or not at all, the district becomes a Scar — and the world had already authored what that is.** This is the correction that mattered most in writing this section. Section 9.8 says **a break spends the Gate**: the mouth collapses, so there is nothing left to close and no boss left to kill. A raid cannot win ground back that has no Gate in it. `210_PLACES.md` describes the **Cicero Scar** in exactly those terms — *"a cordoned tangle of rowhouses and light industry that a break tore through in the chaotic first months, before the Damen Avenue protocols existed to contain it properly… no boss left to kill there, only a wound the city fenced off and has spent a decade managing rather than healing"* — and Prague carries the **Libeň Scar** on the same footing. A first draft of this section had an overrun district *cleared* by a joint raid, which contradicts both.

**A Scar is permanent and is a place added to the map, not a failure state to be reversed.** The core never reverts and keeps the dungeon's warp; the reclaimed edge can be BGM-licensed for escorted harvest work, which is how the Bearer has already worked the Cicero Scar; residents on the perimeter may develop the gray sleep (`200_WORLD_BIBLE.md`).

**The terminal case is authored too — the Jiu Valley Exclusion.** In 2020 an S-Rank Gate's timer ran out over a valley of a quarter-million people: roughly 31,000 dead, multiple S-Ranks lost in the containment attempts, and six years on it is *"fenced, patrolled, evacuated of every survivor, and worked only by rotating multinational containment operations that hold the perimeter and grind the count down a little at a time."* **Finishing it would cost more than any nation will pay.** That is what an unanswered containment failure becomes at S-Rank, and it is why break timers are taken seriously in every agency on Earth.

**So the world may take something away permanently, in its own vocabulary rather than a new one.** Places inside a Scar become unreachable, entities living there are displaced or lost, threads anchored there are cut, and Section 7.5 supply sources inside stop producing — while the edge may become a licensed work site that did not exist before. The Bearer declining to intervene is a legitimate choice with a consequence, not an interruption the fiction routes around.

### 2. The world changes shape on a slower clock

**New Section 9.1.4** rolls one d100 on the first tick of each in-fiction week. 55% the world holds; otherwise a new awakened of note (an A- or S-Rank intake is national news and a recruiting war between all four guilds), a guild rising, a guild falling with its contracts and bench going somewhere, political or regulatory upheaval that can move Section 9.4's own numbers, a standing Gate thread that does not resolve in a day, escalation in another metro thinning the response here, or — at 97–00 — **something the world has not seen before, authored rather than deferred.**

These are threads, not headlines. A guild collapsing changes who holds contracts, who is hiring, and what the board looks like for weeks.

### 3. Both dispatch, and both are recorded

Section 9.1 gains **step 9** (district pressure settles) and **step 10** (the weekly major roll), and Section 9.1.1's `world_ticks` entry carries `contest`, `world_event`, `pressure` and `major` alongside the existing fields. A rule nothing dispatches is the defect this engine has met repeatedly; neither of these is left as prose.

## What did not change

**Section 8.4.2 is untouched.** Neither table creates an Urgent quest, and criterion 2 still admits only a Gate creature, an active Gate break, or a directly perceived sealed-instance hazard.

**No Rank distribution, siting band, break timer, party minimum, anomaly entry, population formula or contest rate moves.** Sections 9.2 through 9.10 are unchanged in effect, and 1.77's contest rates are exactly as authored.

## Migration procedure

**Compatibility treatment, 1.77 → 1.78.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon.

**1. Advance `profile_version` to `"1.78"`.**

**2. Nothing stored changes, and nothing is recomputed.** No pool, Stat, level, XP, Rank, skill, mastery, counter, title, item, gold, cash, quest, candidate, class, fragment, supply source, standing need or tracked posting is touched.

**3. Initialise `district_pressure` from played history, and do not invent any.** Only districts with a real break record get an entry. In `gatefall_pendragon_001` that is the Red Line Corridor (`GB-01` 2026-08-08, `GB-04` 2026-08-12) and the Lakefront Gate Corridor (`GB-02` 2026-08-09) — **all three contained by the Section 9.8 response, all more than a clear week behind the current anchor, so every one has decayed to 0.** A district with no entry is at 0. Do not back-fill pressure for breaks the response handled; the decay rule already accounts for them.

**4. No past break is re-adjudicated and no district starts in cordon.** The campaign has never had a containment failure, and inventing one to populate a new mechanic would be authoring world history to fill a table.

**5. The weekly roll begins with the first in-fiction week under this profile.** No prior weeks are rolled retroactively — 1.73 declined to back-date ninety days of ticks and the same reasoning applies here.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing. Immutable Profile 1.77-and-earlier checkpoints remain byte-unchanged and run the compatibility chain through 1.78 at readiness.
