## What 1.51 changed

**The System window loses its vertical edges.** Section 15.1's frame was a closed box — `╔ ═ ╗ ║ ╚ ╝` with `╟ ─ ╢` section rules — and every interior row carried a `║` at both margins. 1.51 keeps the horizontal rules and removes both vertical edges: a panel opens on `╔══…`, divides on `╟──…`, closes on `╚══…`, and an interior row is its content alone.

**The right edge was the expensive half.** It only holds if every row is padded to an exact width, and these panels are full of glyphs a Runtime can miscount — `★`, `█`, `◈`, `×`, bracketed Ranks. One miscount renders a wall that visibly fails to close, which turns a cosmetic slip into something that reads to the Bearer as a broken window. The left edge then repeated that per-row risk for no gain, since the rules already mark where a panel starts, ends, and divides. What the frame is for, the horizontal rules do on their own.

**Padding goes with it.** Rows are laid out on columns as each template sets them and are never padded rightward, because there is no right margin to pad to; a short row simply ends. The no-truncation rule is unchanged — a value too long for its row still wraps onto an indented continuation row beneath its own label. The rules themselves run to a nominal 78 cells so panels stack evenly, and a rule is now the only thing in a panel with a fixed length.

**The gutter is measured from the row.** Its five cells and the two glyph positions are unchanged in effect: an ordinary row begins at column 6 and a marked row places its glyph at column 3, counted from the start of the row itself rather than from an edge character that no longer exists.

**Every panel changes, and that is not optional.** Section 15.4 states that `/system all` "composes the panels of Sections 15.2 and 15.3 unchanged and authors no layout of its own," so the frame cannot differ between `/system all` and the panels it composes. A frame change is necessarily global, and 1.51 applies it to the Console and all seven focused panels together.

**No mechanic, number, price, timer, pool, capability, or resolved outcome changes.** Nothing about what a panel *reports* moves; only how its frame is drawn. Every value reachable under 1.50 is reachable under 1.51, in the same section, on the same row, in the same order.

## Migration procedure

**1.50 → 1.51 compatibility treatment.** Preserve every stored value and all resolved canon. No stored value is read, written, or re-derived; adoption requires no recomputation and no state migration.

**1. Nothing in canonical state is touched.** `touched_state_paths` is empty and means it. No pool, Stat, level, System Rank, skill, title, counter, holding, quest, gold, cash, or deadline moves, and no Event is reopened. A capture taken under 1.50 is already a valid 1.51 capture.

**2. The change applies at the next render, not retroactively.** A panel drawn under 1.50 was correct when drawn. Rendering is not stored state, so there is nothing to convert: the first `/system` call after adoption draws the new frame, and no prior transcript, checkpoint, or export is rewritten to match.

**3. Authored transcripts keep their boxes.** Any `/system` window already written into a chronicle, changelog, or export is a record of what the Bearer saw at the time and is left exactly as it stands. Rewriting one to the new frame would be editing history to match present layout, which this procedure forbids.

**4. Immutable checkpoints are byte-unchanged**, including any captured panel text they contain, and run the compatibility chain through 1.51 at readiness.

Record adoption as a campaign Event at the adopting campaign's current anchor; consume no fictional time and reopen nothing.
